    Verses¶

    Question¶

    In Daniel 8:14, is the Hebrew word translated 'cleansed' (KJV) more appropriately rendered 'vindicated'? Is 'vindicated' the proper response to the question posed in Daniel 8:13? And does the meaning 'vindicated' include cleansing?

    The crux clause: nitsdaq qodesh — 'the sanctuary shall be vindicated'¶

    And he said unto me, Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed.

    Context: Angelic answer to the 'how long' question of 8:13, closing the little-horn vision of Dan 8. Third clause of the verse: qodesh (holiness/sanctuary) is the subject receiving the action of the verb nitsdaq.

    Notes: Parser confirms nitsdaq = Verb.Niphal.Perf.3ms of tsadaq (H6663), the sole Niphal of tsadaq in the OT (a hapax stem-form). Preceded by conjunctive waw (וְנִצְדַּק). qodesh = Noun.ms.Abs, the grammatical subject. Divine passive: God is the unnamed agent. KJV renders 'cleansed' but the verb is forensic tsadaq ('be vindicated/declared right'), not the ritual-cleansing verb taher. TEXTUAL FINDING: the LOCAL LXX corpus reads Dan 8:14 as 'καὶ καθαρισθήσεται τὸ ἅγιον' — katharisthesetai (from katharizo, G2511, a CLEANSE-verb) tags the verb, NOT dikaioo. See word study G1344 lxx_occurrence_audit and word study G2511 for the versional detail.

    The kaphar->tsedeq bridge verse — Daniel HAD the atonement word and used it here¶

    Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city, to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness, and to seal up the vision and prophecy, and to anoint the most Holy.

    Context: The 70-weeks decree, one chapter after 8:14. Six infinitival purpose clauses spanning atonement and righteousness in one verse.

    Notes: Parser confirms BOTH terms present: לְכַפֵּר (kaphar H3722, Piel InfCon, 'to make reconciliation/atone for iniquity') AND צֶדֶק עֹלָמִים (tsedeq H6664 + olamim, 'everlasting righteousness'). Also פֶּשַׁע (pesha H6588, 'the transgression') as in 8:13. This is the only OT verse pairing kaphar (atone) with tsedeq (righteousness). NEGATIVE CONTROL: Daniel demonstrably had kaphar in his active lexicon and used it here — so his choice of tsadaq (not kaphar, not taher) in 8:14 is deliberate. The atonement/cleansing register (kaphar) and the righteousness register (tsedeq) sit side by side, supporting the inclusion model (tame->tahor->tsedeq progression).

    tsadaq set in synonymous parallel with zakah (be clean/pure) — the inclusion overlap¶

    How then can man be justified with God? or how can he be clean [that is] born of a woman?

    Context: Bildad's speech; a wisdom-couplet questioning whether a mortal can stand right before God.

    Notes: Parser confirms synonymous parallelism: יִּצְדַּק (tsadaq H6663, Qal Impf 3ms, 'be just/justified') || יִּזְכֶּה (zakah H2135, Qal Impf 3ms, 'be clean/pure'). The forensic-rightness verb stands in poetic parallel with a purity verb — Davidson's load-bearing inclusion datum. Shows the tsadaq word-field overlaps the clean/pure word-field in Hebrew poetry, so choosing tsadaq does not exclude the cleanness idea; it subsumes it.

    sdq in parallel with taher; LXX renders the couplet with katharos (verify of Davidson)¶

    Shall mortal man be more just than God? shall a man be more pure than his maker?

    Context: Eliphaz reporting a night vision; the base wisdom question of human righteousness before God.

    Notes: Parser confirms parallelism: יִצְדָּק (tsadaq H6663, Qal Impf 3ms, 'be just') || יִטְהַר (taher H2891, Qal Impf 3ms, 'be clean/pure'). DAVIDSON CLAIM VERIFIED against local LXX: Job 4:17 LXX = 'τί γάρ μὴ καθαρὸς ἔσται βροτὸς ἐναντίον κυρίου ἢ ἀπὸ τῶν ἔργων αὐτοῦ ἄμεμπτος ἀνήρ' — the couplet is rendered with καθαρός (katharos, 'clean', the same kathar- word-group as Dan 8:14's katharisthesetai) and ἄμεμπτος ('blameless'). So the LXX itself renders a tsadaq-line with the clean word-group here, confirming the semantic overlap of the righteousness and purity fields. lxx_integrity --check 'Job 4:17' = no damage.

    The Servant's courtroom vindication — 'he is near that justifieth me'¶

    [He is] near that justifieth me; who will contend with me? let us stand together: who [is] mine adversary? let him come near to me.

    Context: Third Servant Song; the Servant, under accusation, appeals to God as the one who declares him in the right in a courtroom setting.

    Notes: Parser confirms מַצְדִּיקִי (tsadaq H6663, Hiphil Ptcp + 1cs suffix, 'the one justifying me'). Courtroom vocabulary saturates the verse: רִיב (contend/plead a case), עמד (stand together, as litigants), בַּעַל מִשְׁפָּטִי (mishpat H4941, 'master of my judgment/adversary at law'). Pure forensic tsadaq — a verdict of being in the right, with no purity term present. Fixes the forensic core of the word. LXX Isa 50:8 uses δικαιώσας (dikaioo, G1344): 'ὁ δικαιώσας με'.

    'That thou mightest be justified when thou judgest' — tsadaq || zakah inside a purification psalm¶

    Against thee, thee only, have I sinned, and done [this] evil in thy sight: that thou mightest be justified when thou speakest, [and] be clear when thou judgest.

    Context: David's penitential psalm after Bathsheba; God's own rightness is affirmed in judgment. The psalm is saturated with cleansing language (v.7 purge/wash).

    Notes: VERSIFICATION NOTE: English Psa 51:4 = Hebrew MT Psa 51:6 (the KJV superscription is counted as vv.1-2 in the MT, a 2-verse offset). Research key retained as English 'Psa 51:4'; MT reference parsed = 'Psa 51:6'. Parser of MT 51:6 confirms תִּצְדַּק (tsadaq H6663, Qal Impf 2ms, 'be just/justified') in synonymous parallel with תִּזְכֶּה (zakah H2135, Qal Impf 2ms, 'be clean') — the same tsadaq||zakah pairing as Job 25:4, here in a purification-saturated psalm. English v.7 (=MT 51:9, parsed) reads taher + kabas ('purge me... wash me'). So within one psalm the justifying-verdict verb and the cleansing verbs co-occur. LXX MT-51:6 uses δικαιωθῇς (dikaioo, G1344): 'ὅπως ἂν δικαιωθῇς ἐν τοῖς λόγοις σου'. Quoted in Rom 3:4.

    'Purge me with hyssop... wash me' — the cleansing dimension paired with the verdict of v.4¶

    Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.

    Context: Same penitential psalm; the ritual-cleansing petition standing three verses from the justifying-verdict language of English v.4.

    Notes: VERSIFICATION: English Psa 51:7 = Hebrew MT Psa 51:9 (parsed). MT 51:9 = תְּחַטְּאֵנִי (chata Piel, 'de-sin me') + וְאֶטְהָר (taher H2891 Qal, 'and I shall be clean') + תְּכַבְּסֵנִי (kabas, 'wash me'). Supporting verse: shows the cleansing register (taher) and the justifying register (tsadaq, English v.4) coupled in one psalm — the tame->tahor->tsadaq progression in miniature.

    The judgment scene Dan 8:14 compresses — thrones set, books opened¶

    9 I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment [was] white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne [was like] the fiery flame, [and] his wheels [as] burning fire. 10 A fiery stream issued and came forth from before him: thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him: the judgment was set, and the books were opened.

    Context: Aramaic vision of Dan 7; the heavenly court convenes after the little horn's speaking (7:8). 'The judgment was set' (din H1780) — the forensic frame Dan 8:14's single word nitsdaq compresses.

    Notes: Establishes the courtroom that 8:14 abbreviates: a seated court, opened books, a rendered verdict (din, H1780, Aramaic 'judgment'). Structural constraint: Dan 7 (expanded court) and Dan 8:14 (single justice-word) name one judgment event. The horn attacks (7:8) -> court sits (7:9-10) -> verdict for the saints and against the horn (7:22, 7:26).

    'Judgment was given to the saints' — vindication of the persecuted¶

    Until the Ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the most High; and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom.

    Context: Interpretation of the vision; the court's verdict decides FOR the persecuted saints against the horn.

    Notes: din (H1780, 'judgment') rendered in favor of the saints. Parallel to nitsdaq in 8:14: what was trampled/persecuted is set right by a forensic verdict. Vindication = a favorable court decision for the wronged party, exactly the sense tsadaq carries.

    'The judgment shall sit' — the verdict against the horn¶

    But the judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy [it] unto the end.

    Context: The court's action against the little horn: dominion removed, power destroyed.

    Notes: din (H1780) sits and rules against the usurper. The forensic answer to usurpation — the negative side of the same verdict that vindicates the saints (7:22). Confirms the injustice->justice (court verdict) shape that 8:14 answers.

    The plainest forensic tsadaq — judges 'justify the righteous and condemn the wicked'¶

    If there be a controversy between men, and they come unto judgment, that [the judges] may judge them; then they shall justify the righteous, and condemn the wicked.

    Context: Mosaic courtroom procedure; the paradigmatic legal setting for tsadaq.

    Notes: tsadaq (H6663, Hiphil) as a judge's declarative verdict, set opposite 'condemn' (rasha). The controlling antonym pair (justify/condemn) fixes tsadaq as a forensic declaration, not a moral or ritual change. LXX uses δικαιώσωσιν (dikaioo, G1344). The base-sense control for 'vindicate/justify'.

    tsadaq as a forensic verdict — 'he that justifieth the wicked... abomination'¶

    He that justifieth the wicked, and he that condemneth the just, even they both [are] abomination to the LORD.

    Context: Wisdom saying on the perversion of justice; justifying and condemning are the two possible verdicts.

    Notes: tsadaq (H6663, Hiphil, 'justify') paired antithetically with 'condemn'. A declaratory verdict language: to justify is to declare in the right (wrong here only because the object is 'the wicked'). Confirms tsadaq is a courtroom verdict term, reinforcing 'vindicated' for Dan 8:14.

    Pure forensic tsadaq with no cleansing term — the base-sense negative control¶

    I know [it is] so of a truth: but how should man be just with God?

    Context: Job's reply; the question of standing right before God as judge, framed purely as a legal standing.

    Notes: tsadaq (H6663) 'be just/right with God' with NO purity term in the immediate context (contrast Job 4:17 and 25:4 where taher/zakah stand in parallel). Negative control showing the base meaning of tsadaq is forensic standing; cleansing is an INCLUDED facet only where a purity term is present in cultic/relational contexts, not part of the bare lexical core.

    Explicit courtroom vindication contrast — 'condemn me, that thou mayest be righteous'¶

    Wilt thou also disannul my judgment? wilt thou condemn me, that thou mayest be righteous?

    Context: God's speech from the whirlwind; the divine challenge frames righteousness as the outcome of a legal contest.

    Notes: tsadaq (H6663) set opposite 'condemn' and paired with mishpat (H4941, 'my judgment'). Being righteous = winning the legal case. The clearest courtroom framing of tsadaq as a verdict-outcome. Reinforces the forensic core.

    Courtroom summons of the nations — 'that they may be justified'¶

    Let all the nations be gathered together, and let the people be assembled: who among them can declare this, and shew us former things? let them bring forth their witnesses, that they may be justified: or let them hear, and say, [It is] truth.

    Context: Isaiah's trial-of-the-nations scene; a cosmic courtroom where the nations are summoned to prove their case.

    Notes: tsadaq (H6663) 'be justified' = be vindicated as in the right in a legal contest, with witnesses and testimony ('shew us... say it is truth'). Zero ritual-purity content. Forensic verdict language. LXX uses δικαιωθήτωσαν (dikaioo, G1344).

    Legal self-defense before God — 'declare thou, that thou mayest be justified'¶

    Put me in remembrance: let us plead together: declare thou, that thou mayest be justified.

    Context: God invites Israel to present its legal case ('plead together').

    Notes: tsadaq (H6663) 'be justified' in a plead-your-case setting. Forensic through and through. LXX uses δικαιωθῇς (dikaioo, G1344): 'ἵνα δικαιωθῇς'.

    Corporate forensic vindication — 'in the LORD shall all the seed of Israel be justified'¶

    In the LORD shall all the seed of Israel be justified, and shall glory.

    Context: Climax of the Isa 45 salvation oracle; corporate Israel is declared in the right before God.

    Notes: tsadaq (H6663) applied corporately: the whole seed of Israel 'be justified'. LXX Isa 45:25 = 'ἀπὸ κυρίου δικαιωθήσονται' (dikaioo, G1344) — a direct comparandum to Dan 8:14 in the SAME translation corpus: where tsadaq means 'be justified/vindicated' Isaiah's LXX uses dikaioo, but Dan 8:14's LXX uses katharizo. Shows Dan 8:14's Greek is an outlier cleansing-rendering of a normally-justify verb. lxx_integrity --check ok.

    The Servant justifies many BY bearing iniquity — verdict linked to sin-bearing¶

    He shall see of the travail of his soul, [and] shall be satisfied: by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many; for he shall bear their iniquities.

    Context: Fourth Servant Song; the vindicating verdict ('justify many') grounded in the Servant's atoning sin-bearing.

    Notes: tsadaq (H6663, Hiphil, 'justify') with the mechanism stated: 'for he shall bear their iniquities'. The justifying verdict is the declared RESULT of a completed sin-bearing — the structural analogy for how vindication encompasses the atonement/cleansing it presupposes (tame->tahor->tsadaq). LXX uses δικαιῶσαι (dikaioo, G1344): 'δικαιῶσαι δίκαιον'. Bridges tsadaq to the NT atonement->justification chain (Rom 3:24-26).

    Forensic verdict before the divine judge — 'no man living be justified'¶

    And enter not into judgment with thy servant: for in thy sight shall no man living be justified.

    Context: Penitential appeal; entering into judgment (mishpat) with God, no one can be declared righteous.

    Notes: tsadaq (H6663) 'be justified' explicitly inside a 'judgment' (legal) frame. Quoted by Paul in Rom 3:20. LXX uses δικαιωθήσεται (dikaioo, G1344): 'οὐ δικαιωθήσεται ἐνώπιόν σου πᾶς ζῶν'. Pure forensic verdict, no purity content.

    The courtroom model: accuser rebuked, filthy garments removed, festival robes conferred¶

    1 And he shewed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD, and Satan standing at his right hand to resist him. 2 And the LORD said unto Satan, The LORD rebuke thee, O Satan; even the LORD that hath chosen Jerusalem rebuke thee: [is] not this a brand plucked out of the fire? 3 Now Joshua was clothed with filthy garments, and stood before the angel. 4 And he answered and spake unto those that stood before him, saying, Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment. 5 And I said, Let them set a fair mitre upon his head. So they set a fair mitre upon his head, and clothed him with garments. And the angel of the LORD stood by.

    Context: Zechariah's fourth night vision; a heavenly courtroom scene with an accuser (Satan), the accused (Joshua the high priest), and a verdict.

    Notes: The clearest OT model for cleansing-within-vindication as ONE event. Two movements: filthy garments removed + iniquity caused to pass = the cleansing dimension; change of raiment + fair mitre conferred = positive standing conferred (the vindication dimension). Same accuser-court-verdict shape as Dan 7 (accuser/court/verdict) and Job (Satan accuses). Sum/set (H7760, 'set a fair mitre') marks the positive conferral. Not competing descriptions — two aspects of one judicial act.

    One verse, both dimensions: iniquity removed AND raiment conferred¶

    And he answered and spake unto those that stood before him, saying, Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment.

    Context: The pivot of the Zech 3 vision: the verdict spoken over Joshua.

    Notes: In one utterance: 'take away the filthy garments' (cleansing/removal) + 'I have caused thine iniquity to pass' (guilt removed) + 'I will clothe thee with change of raiment' (positive standing conferred). The inclusion hierarchy in a single verse — cleansing is a component of the larger act of setting-right/vindicating.     The attack-question-answer unit: little horn's assault, the 'how long' question (v.13), and the nitsdaq answer (v.14)¶

    And out of one of them came forth a little horn, which waxed exceeding great, toward the south, and toward the east, and toward the pleasant [land]. And it waxed great, [even] to the host of heaven; and it cast down [some] of the host and of the stars to the ground, and stamped upon them.     Context: Daniel's vision of the ram (Medo-Persia) and he-goat (Greece) in ch. 8; the little horn arises out of one of the four Grecian divisions.     Notes: The operative verbs across vv.9-12 are assault/usurpation language: waxed great, cast down (host, stars, truth), stamped/trod upon (ramas root), magnified himself, took away the daily (tamid), sanctuary cast down, host given (natan, H5414) over 'by reason of transgression' (pesha, H6588).     The daily taken away, truth cast down, host given over (natan) by reason of transgression (pesha) — the injustice the vindication reverses¶

    Yea, he magnified [himself] even to the prince of the host, and by him the daily [sacrifice] was taken away, and the place of his sanctuary was cast down. And an host was given [him] against the daily [sacrifice] by reason of transgression, and it cast down the truth to the ground; and it practised, and prospered.

    Context: Verses 11-12 detail the little horn's activity against the prince of the host, the tamid (daily), and the sanctuary. v.12 introduces pesha (transgression, H6588) as the reason/means the host is given (natan, H5414) over. These are the specific wrongs the v.13 question asks the duration of, and which v.14 answers.

    Notes: H5414 (natan, 'give') appears here as the passive 'was given / an host was given' — what God permitted to be given over in v.11-12 is what is set right in v.14 ('what God gave over, God vindicates'). H6588 (pesha, transgression/rebellion) is the moral/religious revolt (Strong's def: 'a revolt, national, moral or religious') naming the violated norm. The register is usurpation and assault (magnified himself, taken away, cast down), not cultic defilement. Guards the focus-area-2 conclusion: this is an injustice problem, not a defilement problem.

    The 'how long' question naming pesha (H6588), shomem/shamem (H8074), tet/natan (H5414) and mirmac (H4823) — the injustice, not defilement, the vindication answers¶

    Then I heard one saint speaking, and another saint said unto that certain [saint] which spake, How long [shall be] the vision [concerning] the daily [sacrifice], and the transgression of desolation, to give both the sanctuary and the host to be trodden under foot?

    Context: The angelic dialogue that poses the crux question. One holy one asks another 'how long' (ad-matay) the vision, the daily, and 'the transgression of desolation' (pesha shomem) will continue — giving (tet, from natan) the sanctuary (qodesh) and host to be trampled (mirmac). Verse 14 answers with 'unto 2300 days; then shall the qodesh be nitsdaq (vindicated).'

    Notes: Parser confirms all four assigned Strong's operate here: (1) pesha H6588 lemma פשׁע 'rebellion/transgression'; (2) shomem from shamem H8074 lemma שׁמם parsed as Verb.Qal.Ptcp 'be desolate'; (3) tet from natan H5414 lemma נתן parsed as Verb.Qal.InfCon 'give'; (4) mirmac H4823 lemma מרמס 'trampled land / trodden under foot'. The controlling imagery is trampling (mirmac), desolation (shamem), giving-over (natan), and transgression (pesha) — usurpation and assault. NO purity/cleansing vocabulary (taher H2891, tame) appears. qodesh (holiness/sanctuary) is here the OBJECT of trampling; in v.14 the same qodesh becomes the SUBJECT of vindication (nitsdaq) — the semantic inversion. An injustice question (how long will this trampling last) naturally takes a justice answer (it shall be set right / vindicated).
