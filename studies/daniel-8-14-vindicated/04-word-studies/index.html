    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    In Daniel 8:14, is the Hebrew word translated 'cleansed' (KJV) more appropriately rendered 'vindicated'? Is 'vindicated' the proper response to the question posed in Daniel 8:13? And does the meaning 'vindicated' include cleansing?

    H6663 — tsadaq¶

    Source: D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6663-tsadaq.md

    Occurrences: 54

    Primitive root 'to be (make) right' in a moral or forensic sense. 41 OT occurrences (54 KJV translation entries). Stem distribution: Qal 22 (be righteous/in the right), Hiphil 12 (justify/acquit/declare righteous), Piel 5 (prove right), Niphal 1 (be vindicated — Dan 8:14, the SOLE Niphal in the OT), Hithpael 1 (clear oneself). KJV renders it 'just/justify/righteous' across all occurrences EXCEPT Dan 8:14 ('cleansed'). Core is forensic/relational, not cultic purity. Strong's own gloss list does include 'cleanse, clear' among its senses, so the lexicon is not ignorant of a cleansing sense — but that is a secondary/derived sense, and 'cleansed' as the rendering is unique to Dan 8:14. Primary LXX renderings: dikaioo (G1344) 21x, dikaios (G1342) 22x, krino 8x, never katharizo. Collocates mishpat/shaphat/tsaddiq/rasha (courtroom). Job dominates usage (~56%).

    Semantic categories: forensic/legal verdict (justify, acquit, declare in the right), being righteous before God (Qal stative), vindication/restoration to right (Niphal, Dan 8:14), proving oneself right (Piel/Hithpael), causing righteousness in others (Hiphil ptcp, Isa 53:11, Dan 12:3)

    Key verses: Dan 8:14, Deut 25:1, Job 25:4, Isa 50:8, Isa 53:11, Psa 51:4, Psa 143:2, Prov 17:15

    Relevance: The central disputed term. Dan 8:14's nitsdaq is the sole Niphal of tsadaq, a hapax stem-form; its forensic core supports 'vindicated' over 'cleansed', while its parallels with purity words (Job 4:17 taher, Job 25:4 zakah, Psa 51:4 zakah) support that the choice does not EXCLUDE cleansing but subsumes it.

    H2891 — taher¶

    Source: D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H2891-taher.md

    Occurrences: 115

    Primitive root 'to be bright' -> 'be pure'; the standard ritual/moral cleansing verb. 94 occurrences (115 KJV instances, 80 verses). Stems: Piel 37 (declare/actively cleanse), Qal 34 (be clean, resulting state), Hithpael 18 (self-purify), Pual 1. Heavily concentrated in Leviticus (46%), the ceremonial-purity system, but also broad: eschatological/spiritual cleansing (Ezek 36:25, Jer 33:8), refining (Mal 3:3). Collocates kohen, kabas, beged, kaphar, mayim (ritual field). LXX: katharizo (G2511) 61x, katharos (G2513) 23x.

    Semantic categories: Levitical cleansing of persons, purification after bodily states, ritual purification of objects/sacred spaces (Lev 16:19), self-purification for worship, divine spiritual cleansing (Ezek 36:25, Mal 3:3), moral purity question (Job 4:17)

    Key verses: Lev 16:19, Lev 16:30, Ezek 36:25, Mal 3:3, Job 4:17, Psa 51:7

    Relevance: The ritual-cleansing verb Daniel did NOT use in Dan 8:14 (the negative control). Lev 16:19,30 use taher for the DOA sanctuary/people cleansing — the exact context 8:14 evokes. Guard against overreach: Mal 3:3 and Ezek 36:25 show taher is broad, not narrow, so the inclusion case for tsadaq rests on tsadaq's breadth, not on shrinking taher.

    H3722 — kaphar¶

    Source: D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H3722-kaphar.md

    Occurrences: 130

    Primitive root 'to cover' (with bitumen) -> figuratively expiate/atone/placate/cancel. 130 occurrences. Dominant Piel: a priest makes atonement for persons/sanctuary/objects with sacrificial blood, issuing in acceptance/forgiveness. Concentrated in Leviticus (52) and Numbers (20). Also: purging/removal of sin (Isa 6:7), appeasement (Gen 32:20), divine forgiveness (Ps 78:38), intercessory/national atonement (Dan 9:24). LXX includes exhilaskomai, katharizo (G2511, 14x). Collocates chattaah, kohen, mizbeach, dam, taher (atonement leading to cleansing).

    Semantic categories: ritual atonement through sacrifice, purging/removal of sin, appeasement/pacification, divine forgiveness/mercy, intercessory/national atonement (Dan 9:24), physical covering (Qal, Gen 6:14)

    Key verses: Lev 16:16, Lev 16:30, Lev 17:11, Dan 9:24, Isa 6:7

    Relevance: The atonement verb Daniel used in Dan 9:24 (one chapter after 8:14) but NOT in 8:14 — the key negative control proving Daniel had the atonement/cleansing vocabulary in his active lexicon and deliberately chose tsadaq instead. kaphar->taher (Lev 16:30) and kaphar->tsedeq (Dan 9:24) establish the atonement-before-righteousness sequence underlying the inclusion model.

    H6664 — tsedeq¶

    Occurrences: 119

    Masculine noun 'righteousness, justice' from the tsadaq root. 119 occurrences; KJV 'righteousness' (~27+ primary), 'just', 'justice', 'right', 'righteously'. Forensic/covenantal register. Paired with kaphar in Dan 9:24 (tsedeq olamim, 'everlasting righteousness'). Derivation (Strong's): from the root idea of being right morally or forensically.

    Semantic categories: righteousness as quality/standard, justice in the courtroom/governance, God's saving righteousness, everlasting righteousness (Dan 9:24)

    Key verses: Dan 9:24, Psa 51:4

    Relevance: The noun 'righteousness' paired with kaphar (atone) in Dan 9:24 — the bridge verse showing atonement issues in righteousness. Its presence in Daniel's own chapter 9 shows the tsadaq word-family (not the taher family) is Daniel's register for what happens after atonement.

    H6666 — tsedaqah¶

    Occurrences: 165

    Feminine noun 'righteousness' from the tsadaq root. 165 occurrences; KJV predominantly 'righteousness', also 'justice', 'right', 'righteous acts'. Same forensic/covenantal semantic field as tsedeq; often God's saving/vindicating righteousness in Isaiah and Psalms.

    Semantic categories: righteousness as covenant faithfulness, God's saving/vindicating righteousness, righteous acts, justice

    Key verses: Isa 45:24, Isa 46:13

    Relevance: Cognate noun confirming the breadth of the tsadaq righteousness field. In Isaiah the tsedaqah of God is His vindicating, salvation-bringing rightness — the noun-side of the forensic vindication that nitsdaq expresses verbally in Dan 8:14.

    H6662 — tsaddiq¶

    Occurrences: 210

    Adjective 'just, righteous' from the tsadaq root. 210 occurrences; KJV overwhelmingly 'righteous'/'the righteous' and 'just'. The courtroom sense: the party in the right, the one a judge should justify (Deut 25:1, 'justify the righteous').

    Semantic categories: the righteous person/party, the just (in the right) in a legal contest, God as righteous

    Key verses: Deut 25:1, Prov 17:15

    Relevance: The adjective completing the tsadaq family. Deut 25:1 and Prov 17:15 pair the verb tsadaq (justify) with tsaddiq (the righteous) in explicit courtroom settings, fixing the forensic core that supports 'vindicated' for Dan 8:14.

    H2135 — zakah¶

    Occurrences: 11

    Primitive root (cf. zakak) 'to be translucent', figuratively 'to be innocent/pure'. 11 occurrences; KJV 'be clean', 'be clear', 'cleanse', 'count pure', 'be pure'. A purity/innocence verb overlapping the clean side of the semantic field.

    Semantic categories: be clean/pure (moral innocence), be clear/innocent, count pure

    Key verses: Job 25:4, Psa 51:4, Job 15:14

    Relevance: Stands in synonymous poetic parallel with tsadaq in Job 25:4 ('be justified... be clean') and Psa 51:4/MT 51:6 ('be justified... be clean'). The load-bearing datum for the inclusion thesis: the righteousness verb and the purity verb are paired in Hebrew poetry, so tsadaq's field overlaps the clean field — choosing tsadaq does not exclude cleansing.

    H1249 — bar¶

    Occurrences: 9

    Adjective from barar 'pure, clean, choice, clear'. 9 occurrences; KJV 'clean', 'pure', 'clear', 'choice'. A purity adjective in the clean/clear semantic field (bor/barar family).

    Semantic categories: pure/clean (of heart), clear/bright, choice

    Key verses: Psa 24:4, Psa 73:1

    Relevance: Part of the purity word-group (bor/bar) that Davidson lists as standing in parallel with sdq (e.g., Psa 18:20 bor). A minor member of the clean field bearing on the inclusion overlap; also the field of barar used in Daniel's own Dan 12:10 ('purified').

    H4941 — mishpat¶

    Occurrences: 448

    Noun from shaphat 'a verdict pronounced judicially; a sentence, formal decree; justice, right, ordinance'. Very frequent. Strong's: 'properly a verdict (favorable or unfavorable) pronounced judicially, especially a sentence or formal decree... including the act, the place, the suit, the crime, and the penalty; abstractly justice'. The primary forensic partner of tsadaq (8x collocation per H6663 study).

    Semantic categories: judicial verdict/sentence, justice/right (abstract), legal cause/suit, ordinance/decree

    Key verses: Isa 50:8, Job 40:8, Psa 143:2

    Relevance: The forensic frame word collocating with tsadaq. Appears in Isa 50:8 ('master of my judgment/adversary at law') and Job 40:8 ('disannul my judgment') alongside tsadaq, confirming that tsadaq operates in a courtroom-verdict register — the frame that makes 'vindicated' the natural rendering.

    H1779 — din¶

    Occurrences: 21

    Hebrew noun 'judgement (the suit, justice, sentence, or tribunal); by implication strife'. 21 occurrences; KJV 'cause', 'judgment', 'plea', 'strife'. From the root din (to judge/contend).

    Semantic categories: judgment/verdict, legal cause/plea, strife/contention

    Key verses: Est 1:13, Prov 20:8

    Relevance: Hebrew cognate of the Aramaic din (H1780) that appears in Dan 7:10,22,26 for the seated court. Ties the Hebrew and Aramaic judgment vocabulary together, framing the forensic courtroom that Dan 8:14 compresses into one justice-word.

    H1780 — din (Aramaic)¶

    Occurrences: 5

    Aramaic noun corresponding to Hebrew din (H1779): 'judgement (the suit, justice, sentence or tribunal)'. 5 occurrences; KJV 'judgment' (all). Occurs in Daniel's Aramaic court scenes.

    Semantic categories: judgment (the court's session), verdict/sentence, tribunal

    Key verses: Dan 7:10, Dan 7:22, Dan 7:26

    Relevance: The courtroom word in Dan 7:10 ('the judgment was set'), 7:22 ('judgment given to the saints'), 7:26 ('the judgment shall sit'). Dan 8:14's single verb nitsdaq compresses this expanded court: the din of Dan 7 IS the vindication event of Dan 8:14 — one judgment, two descriptions.

    H7760 — sum (suwm)¶

    Occurrences: 641

    Primitive root 'to put, set, place, appoint' with a very wide range of applications. Frequent. KJV 'set', 'make', 'appoint', 'lay', 'put', etc.

    Semantic categories: set/place/put, appoint/ordain, make/constitute, lay upon

    Key verses: Zech 3:5

    Relevance: In Zech 3:5 the fair mitre is 'set' (sum) on Joshua's head — the positive conferral of status that, alongside the removal of filthy garments, models the cleansing-within-vindication hierarchy. The act of conferring positive standing (not merely removing defilement) is what vindication adds beyond cleansing.

    G1344 — dikaioo¶

    Occurrences: 48

    Verb 'to render (show or regard as) just or innocent; justify' (from dikaios G1342). NT 48 KJV translation instances; overwhelmingly 'justify/justified/is justified/the justifier'. Strong's derivation: 'to render (i.e. show or regard as) just or innocent'. The primary LXX rendering of Hebrew tsadaq (H6663) — 21x. The Greek narrows tsadaq's range primarily to forensic declaration.

    Semantic categories: justify/declare righteous (forensic), be justified/vindicated, acquit/regard as just

    Key verses: Rom 3:24, Rom 3:26, Isa 45:25 (LXX), Psa 143:2 (LXX)

    Relevance: The Greek side of the tsadaq->dikaioo translation-history argument. The LXX occurrence audit (below) tests whether dikaioo ever appears in a cleansing context. FINDING: across all 29 local LXX occurrences, dikaioo is uniformly forensic/judicial (justify, declare righteous, vindicate, be in the right) and NEVER renders or denotes ritual cleansing. This confirms the tsadaq->dikaioo bridge is forensic, and that Dan 8:14's LXX rendering (katharizo/G2511, a cleanse-verb) is an outlier that departs from how the LXX normally renders tsadaq — i.e. the 'cleansed' reading of Dan 8:14 traces to a versional cleanse-rendering, not to dikaioo and not to the Hebrew verb's own core sense.

    G2511 — katharizo¶

    Occurrences: 37

    Verb 'to cleanse, make clean, purify' (kathar- family). NT ~37 KJV instances: 'clean', 'cleanse', 'cleansed', 'purge', 'purify', 'make clean'. The primary LXX rendering of Hebrew taher (H2891, 61x) and used 14x for kaphar. The Theodotion/LXX cleanse-verb. CORPUS FINDING: the LOCAL LXX text of Dan 8:14 reads 'καὶ καθαρισθήσεται τὸ ἅγιον' with the verb καθαρισθήσεται tagged G2511 — i.e. the local Greek Daniel renders nitsdaq with katharizo (cleanse), NOT dikaioo.

    Semantic categories: cleanse/make clean (ritual), purify/purge, declare clean (priestly), spiritual/moral cleansing

    Key verses: Dan 8:14 (LXX), Heb 9:23, Lev 16:19 (LXX), Matt 8:3, 1 John 1:7

    Relevance: The disputed Greek verb at Dan 8:14. This audit's textual verification confirms the LOCAL LXX corpus renders Dan 8:14 with katharizo (cleanse), tagged G2511 — establishing that the KJV 'cleansed' descends from the Greek/Latin cleanse-tradition, not from the Hebrew tsadaq. Also the verb in Heb 9:23 (heavenly things 'purified') that raises the standing objection, resolved by the inclusion hierarchy (broader tsadaq encompasses narrower katharizo).

    G1342 — dikaios¶

    Occurrences: 81

    Adjective 'righteous, just, right' (the kernel of the dik- family from which dikaioo derives). NT 81 occurrences; KJV 'righteous' (22), 'just' (16), 'the righteous', 'right', 'a just', etc. The LXX adjective for tsaddiq/tsadaq-state (22x for tsadaq). Denotes conformity to what is right; in Revelation, God's ways/judgments are dikaios.

    Semantic categories: righteous/just (of persons), just/right (of acts, judgments), God/Christ as righteous

    Key verses: Rom 3:26, Rev 15:3, Rev 16:5, Rev 16:7, Rev 19:2

    Relevance: The adjective declaring God's ways and judgments righteous in Rev 15:3; 16:5,7; 19:2 — the cosmic-vindication vocabulary. Shows the dik- family (not the kathar- family) carries the meaning 'God's character/government is vindicated as right', a meaning 'cleansed' cannot bear, reinforcing that vindication is the larger category.

    Semantic categories: righteous act/deed, ordinance/statute, judicial decision/verdict, justification

    Key verses: Daniel 8:13, Isaiah 5:5, Isaiah 28:18
