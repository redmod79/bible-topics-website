    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    What is the biblical connection between the flood of Noah and the final fire judgment? Does Scripture itself make this connection explicitly?

    G3056 — logos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3056-logos.md

    Occurrences: 329

    Logos (word, speech, divine expression) — 329 tokens in 314 unique verses across 24 books. Root: lego (to say/speak). Peter uses logos in 2 Pet 3:5 (to tou theou logo, 'by the word of God' — dative of means for creation) and 3:7 (to auto logo, 'by the same word' — linking creation, flood, and fire judgment under a single divine agency). The Johannine prologue (John 1:1-14) personalizes the logos as the divine agent of creation — 'all things were made by him' — which adds theological weight to Peter's argument that creation, flood, and fire are all executed by the same logos.

    Semantic categories: divine creative/authoritative word, gospel message, spoken utterance, account/reckoning, personified Word

    Key verses: John 1:1, 2 Pet 3:5, 2 Pet 3:7, Heb 11:3, Psa 33:6

    Relevance: Central to Peter's argument — the same logos that created the world and destroyed it by water now reserves it for fire. This establishes the flood-fire connection as grounded in divine agency, not mere analogy.

    G5083 — tereo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5083-tereo.md

    Occurrences: 75

    Tereo (keep, guard, reserve) — 75 occurrences. Semantic range includes keeping commandments, physical custody, and critically for this study, reserving for future judgment. Peter uses tereo in 2 Pet 2:4 (angels reserved for judgment), 2:9 (unjust reserved for day of judgment), 2:17 (mist of darkness reserved), and 3:7 (heavens/earth teroumenoi pyri — being kept/reserved for fire). The present passive participle teroumenoi in 3:7 indicates ongoing divine guardianship of the present heavens and earth for the purpose of fire judgment.

    Semantic categories: keeping commandments, physical custody, reserving for future judgment, protective keeping, maintaining condition

    Key verses: 2 Pet 3:7, 2 Pet 2:4, 2 Pet 2:9, Jude 1:6, Jude 1:13

    Relevance: The 'reservation' vocabulary links flood precedents to fire judgment — the same God who reserved judgment then now reserves the present creation for fire.

    G2343 — thesaurizo¶

    Occurrences: 9

    Thesaurizo (to store up, treasure, reserve) — used in 2 Pet 3:7 as perfect passive participle tethesaurismenoi ('having been stored up/treasured'). The perfect tense indicates the reservation was established in the past and remains currently operative. The word literally means 'to lay up treasure' — the present heavens and earth have been 'treasured up' for fire. This is a unique and vivid metaphor: the created order is a stored treasury awaiting its appointed use in fire judgment.

    Semantic categories: storing treasure, reserving for future, laying up

    Key verses: 2 Pet 3:7, Mat 6:19-20, Rom 2:5

    Relevance: The perfect passive participle tethesaurismenoi in 2 Pet 3:7 establishes that the reservation of the present heavens and earth for fire was a completed divine act with ongoing results — the storage is already in place.

    G4747 — stoicheion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4747-stoicheion.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Stoicheion (element, rudiment, basic principle) — only 7 NT occurrences in 4 books. In 2 Pet 3:10,12, it refers to the physical elements of the cosmos that melt with fervent heat (kausoumena, G2741). In Pauline usage (Gal 4:3,9; Col 2:8,20), it refers to 'elements/rudiments of the world' as regulatory cosmic powers. Peter's usage is distinctly physical/cosmological — the material components of the created order subject to fire dissolution. No Hebrew equivalent exists in the LXX, making it a distinctly Hellenistic term. The scholarly debate about Stoic ekpyrosis centers on whether Peter borrowed this term from Greek cosmology, but the OT provides sufficient fire-judgment background (Isa 51:6, Zeph 3:8, Mal 4:1) without requiring Stoic influence.

    Semantic categories: physical elements of cosmos, elemental principles, cosmic powers

    Key verses: 2 Pet 3:10, 2 Pet 3:12

    Relevance: The stoicheia that melt with fervent heat are the material components of the present creation that undergo fire dissolution — central to understanding what Peter claims the fire will do.

    G3089 — lyo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3089-lyo.md

    Occurrences: 46

    Lyo (to loose, dissolve, break, destroy) — 46 tokens in 40 verses across 10 books. Highest density in 2 Peter (3 uses in 61 verses = 4.9/100). In 2 Pet 3:10-12, Peter uses lyo for the dissolution of elements and heavens: lythesetai (v.10, future passive, 'will be dissolved'), lyomenon (v.11, present passive participle, 'being dissolved'), lythesontai (v.12, future passive, 'will be dissolved'). The semantic range includes untying, loosing bonds, breaking/invalidating, and destroying — the dissolution sense in 2 Peter describes the undoing/unloosing of the created order, not necessarily its annihilation.

    Semantic categories: untying/loosing, breaking/invalidating, destroying/dissolving, releasing from restraint

    Key verses: 2 Pet 3:10, 2 Pet 3:11, 2 Pet 3:12

    Relevance: The dissolution verbs in 2 Pet 3:10-12 describe fire judgment's action on the present creation. Lyo means 'to loose/undo' rather than 'to annihilate' — the created order is unloosed/dissolved, not necessarily destroyed absolutely.

    G2537 — kainos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2537-kainos.md

    Occurrences: 44

    Kainos (new in quality, unprecedented) — 44 tokens in 38 verses across 14 books. Distinguished from neos (G3501, new in time/age): kainos describes qualitative newness, not mere chronological recency. Peter uses kainos in 2 Pet 3:13 for 'new heavens and new earth' (kainous ouranous kai gen kainen), and Rev 21:1 uses the same word (ouranon kainon kai gen kainen). This qualitative newness may suggest transformation/renewal rather than creation ex nihilo — the earth made qualitatively new, not a different earth entirely.

    Semantic categories: qualitative incompatibility with old, new covenant, new creation, eschatological realities

    Key verses: 2 Pet 3:13, Rev 21:1, Rev 21:5, 2 Cor 5:17, Isa 65:17

    Relevance: The qualitative sense of kainos supports renewal/transformation over annihilation — the new earth is qualitatively different from the old, just as the post-flood earth was qualitatively different (purified) from the pre-flood earth.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    Apollymi (to destroy utterly, to perish, to lose) — 102 tokens in 86 verses across 15 books. Active voice: to destroy (transitive). Middle/passive: to perish, be ruined. Jesus uses the SAME FORM (apolesen, aorist active) for both flood (Luke 17:27 'destroyed them all') and Sodom fire (Luke 17:29 'destroyed them all'). Peter uses apoleto (aorist middle, 'perished') in 2 Pet 3:6 for the world that 'perished' in the flood. The deliberate use of identical vocabulary for both water and fire destruction in Luke 17 is the strongest verbal link between the two judgments in Jesus's teaching.

    Semantic categories: physical destruction, divine judgment/eschatological ruin, perishing in eternal judgment, losing one's life, losing a possession

    Key verses: Luke 17:27, Luke 17:29, 2 Pet 3:6, 2 Pet 3:9, John 3:16

    Relevance: The identical verb apollymi for both flood and fire destruction in Luke 17:27,29 is key evidence that Jesus himself paired these judgments with identical language.

    G2627 — kataklusmos¶

    Occurrences: 4

    Kataklusmos (flood, deluge, inundation) — the specific Greek term for Noah's flood. Used in Matt 24:38-39 (the days before the flood), Luke 17:27 (the flood came and destroyed them all), 2 Pet 2:5 (bringing in the flood upon the world of the ungodly). From kata (down) + klyzo (to wash/surge). The technical term marks these passages as specifically referencing the Noahic flood event. Peter also uses the cognate verb kataklyzo (G2626) in 2 Pet 3:6 (kataklustheis, aorist passive participle, 'having been flooded').

    Semantic categories: Noahic flood/deluge, divine water judgment

    Key verses: Matt 24:38, Matt 24:39, Luke 17:27, 2 Pet 2:5

    Relevance: The technical flood term appearing in the same passages (Matt 24, Luke 17, 2 Pet 2) that connect flood to eschatological judgment confirms these are deliberate flood-to-fire typological passages.

    G684 — apoleia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G684-apoleia.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Apoleia (destruction, perdition, ruin) — 20 tokens in 19 verses across 11 books. Sharply concentrated in 2 Peter (5 uses in 61 verses = highest density). Peter uses it in 2 Pet 3:7 for 'the day of judgment and perdition (apoleias) of ungodly men' — the same ungodly men whose pre-flood counterparts Peter describes in 2:5 ('the world of the ungodly'). The noun from apollymi (G622) — destruction/ruin as a state or destiny.

    Semantic categories: eschatological ruin, son of perdition, waste, heresy-induced ruin

    Key verses: 2 Pet 3:7, 2 Pet 2:1, 2 Pet 2:3, Matt 7:13, Rev 17:8

    Relevance: Peter's concentration of apoleia in 2 Peter links flood judgment (G622 apollymi in 3:6, 'perished') to fire judgment (G684 apoleia in 3:7, 'perdition of ungodly men') — cognate vocabulary binding the two judgments.

    G4442 — pyr¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4442-pyr.md

    Occurrences: 74

    Pyr (fire) — 74 tokens in 73 verses across 14 books. Revelation dominates (25 occurrences). Semantic range: eschatological punishment (lake of fire, Gehenna), judgment parables (chaff burned, tares), divine theophany (consuming fire, flame of fire), cosmic/apocalyptic plagues, testing/refining, and figurative uses. Peter uses pyr once in 2 Pet 3:7 (pyri, dative, 'reserved unto fire') as the medium of final judgment replacing water. Luke 17:29 uses pyr for Sodom's fire. The fire-brimstone pair (pyr + theion, G2303) originates in Gen 19:24 and recurs throughout Revelation.

    Semantic categories: eschatological punishment, judgment parables, divine theophany, cosmic plagues, testing/refining

    Key verses: 2 Pet 3:7, Luke 17:29, Rev 20:9, Rev 21:8, Matt 25:41, Jude 1:7

    Relevance: Fire is the central medium of final judgment that replaces water in Peter's flood-to-fire argument. The pyr + theion (fire + brimstone) pair originates with Sodom and culminates in Revelation.

    G4451 — pyrosis¶

    Occurrences: 3

    Pyrosis (burning, fiery trial) — related to pyr (G4442). Used in 1 Pet 4:12 ('the fiery trial which is to try you') and Rev 18:9,18 (Babylon's burning). Part of the pyr word family used for fire-judgment vocabulary.

    Semantic categories: burning/ignition, fiery trial

    Key verses: 1 Pet 4:12, Rev 18:9, Rev 18:18

    Relevance: Part of the fire-judgment vocabulary family. Peter uses pyrosis in 1 Peter for trial-by-fire; the same author develops the cosmic fire theme in 2 Peter.

    G2618 — katakaio¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2618-katakaio.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Katakaio (to burn down, consume wholly) — 12 tokens in 11 verses across 7 books. Compound: kata (down/completely) + kaio (burn). Passive voice dominates (58%), reflecting judgment contexts where the focus is on the object being consumed. Used in 2 Pet 3:10 (katakaesetai, 'shall be burned up') for the earth and its works being consumed by fire. Also Matt 3:12 (burn up chaff), Rev 8:7 (third of trees burnt up), Rev 17:16/18:8 (Babylon burned). The kata prefix intensifies: complete, total consumption.

    Semantic categories: judgment parable (winnowing), apocalyptic destruction, Babylon judgment, testing by fire

    Key verses: 2 Pet 3:10, Matt 3:12, Rev 8:7, Rev 18:8

    Relevance: The burning-up verb in 2 Pet 3:10 describes fire's action on the earth and its works. The complete-consumption semantics of katakaio must be balanced against the renewal promise in v.13.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Aionios (eternal, everlasting, age-lasting) — 71 tokens in 69 verses across 19 books. From aion (G165, an age). Dominantly used for 'eternal life' (~43 verses). In judgment contexts: 'everlasting fire' (Matt 25:41), 'eternal fire' (Jude 1:7), 'everlasting destruction' (2 Thess 1:9). Jude 1:7 calls Sodom's fire 'eternal fire' (pyros aioniou) — yet Sodom is not still burning, indicating aionios describes the PERMANENCE OF RESULT rather than duration of burning process.

    Semantic categories: eternal life, eternal judgment/punishment, divine attributes, eternal realities, pre-temporal purpose

    Key verses: Jude 1:7, Matt 25:41, Matt 25:46, John 3:16

    Relevance: The 'eternal fire' phrase in Jude 1:7 applied to Sodom demonstrates that aionios fire describes permanent/irreversible destruction, not never-ending combustion — consistent with how the flood permanently destroyed the old world.

    G2303 — theion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2303-theion.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Theion (brimstone, sulfur) — 7 occurrences, all in Luke (1) and Revelation (6). The Sodom judgment archetype: Gen 19:24 'rained brimstone and fire' is the foundational usage, with Luke 17:29 being the only non-Revelation NT occurrence. In Revelation: fire and brimstone as the lake of fire (Rev 19:20, 20:10, 21:8), torment of the wicked (Rev 14:10), and demonic warfare (Rev 9:17-18). The brimstone consistently signals divine judgment of the most severe and permanent kind.

    Semantic categories: Sodom judgment archetype, eschatological torment, lake of fire imagery, demonic warfare

    Key verses: Luke 17:29, Rev 14:10, Rev 19:20, Rev 20:10, Rev 21:8

    Relevance: Brimstone (theion) paired with fire (pyr) creates the Sodom-to-Revelation judgment chain. Luke 17:29's use connects Jesus's flood-fire typology directly to the Sodom paradigm.

    H784 — esh¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H784-esh.md

    Occurrences: 400

    Esh (fire) — 400 tokens in 348 verses across 33 OT books. The primary OT fire word. Used for divine theophany (burning bush, Sinai), sacrificial fire, judgment fire (Gen 19:24 Sodom, Deut 32:22 cosmic fire, Mal 4:1 eschatological fire), purification (Num 31:23), and figurative uses. Highest density in prophetic books (Joel, Amos, Nahum, Ezekiel) and covenant/law contexts (Leviticus, Deuteronomy).

    Semantic categories: divine theophany, sacrificial fire, judgment fire, purification fire, figurative

    Key verses: Gen 19:24, Deut 32:22, Mal 4:1, Isa 34:9, Dan 7:9-10

    Relevance: The OT fire-judgment tradition (esh passages) provides biblical background for Peter's fire imagery without requiring Stoic ekpyrosis.

    H1614 — gophriyth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1614-gophriyth.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Gophriyth (brimstone, sulfur) — only 7 OT occurrences across 6 books. The foundational usage is Gen 19:24 (Sodom). Used in eschatological judgment (Isa 34:9, Eze 38:22), covenant curse (Deut 29:23), and individual retribution (Job 18:15, Psa 11:6). The NT equivalent is theion (G2303). The Sodom-Gomorrah brimstone paradigm established by gophriyth in Gen 19:24 becomes the template for all subsequent fire-and-brimstone judgment imagery.

    Semantic categories: divine judgment on cities, eschatological judgment, individual retribution

    Key verses: Gen 19:24, Isa 34:9, Deut 29:23, Eze 38:22

    Relevance: The OT brimstone vocabulary originates with Sodom and extends through the prophets to eschatological judgment, establishing the OT fire-judgment tradition that Jesus and Peter draw on.

    H7843 — shachath¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7843-shachath.md

    Occurrences: 164

    Shachath (to corrupt, to destroy) — 148 tokens in 136 verses across 25 books. Semantic range spans moral corruption (Gen 6:11-12, pre-flood corruption), physical destruction of cities/armies (Gen 19:13-14, Sodom), and divine restraint from destruction. The Gen 6 wordplay is critical: the earth was corrupt (shachath, Niphal/Hiphil) so God will destroy (shachath, Hiphil) it — the corruption leads to destruction via the same root. Used for both the pre-flood moral state AND the flood judgment itself.

    Semantic categories: moral/spiritual corruption, physical destruction, ruining/marring objects, divine restraint from destruction, the destroyer as agent

    Key verses: Gen 6:11-12, Gen 6:13, Gen 9:11, Gen 19:13-14

    Relevance: Shachath connects pre-flood corruption to flood destruction via wordplay (corrupt → destroy). Gen 9:11 uses shachath for 'a flood to destroy the earth' — the covenant limits this destruction mode to water but does not preclude fire.

    H3999 — mabbul¶

    Occurrences: 13

    Mabbul (flood, deluge) — the specific Hebrew term for Noah's flood. Appears exclusively in flood contexts. Gen 6:17, 7:6,7,10,17; 9:11,15,28; 10:1,32; 11:10 and Psa 29:10 (the LORD sits upon the flood). Very limited usage — the term is reserved for the Noahic flood event specifically. The LXX renders it as kataklusmos (G2627). Gen 9:11 uses mabbul twice: 'no more flood (mabbul) to destroy (shachath) the earth' — the covenant excludes future mabbul but does not address other judgment modes.

    Semantic categories: Noahic flood/deluge

    Key verses: Gen 6:17, Gen 9:11, Gen 9:15, Psa 29:10

    Relevance: The specificity of mabbul to the Noahic flood reinforces the precision of God's covenant in Gen 9:11 — 'no more mabbul' leaves room for fire judgment.

    H4325 — mayim¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H4325-mayim.md

    Occurrences: 631

    Mayim (water, waters) — 631 tokens in 524 verses across 33 OT books. The medium of flood judgment. Isaiah 54:9 uses mey-Noach ('waters of Noah') — one of the few OT passages that explicitly names the flood typologically. In creation (Gen 1:2, Spirit over the waters), in flood (Gen 7:10-24, waters prevail), and in covenant (Gen 9:11,15, waters of a flood). Peter picks up both the creation-water and flood-water connections in 2 Pet 3:5-6.

    Semantic categories: creation waters, flood waters, covenant reference, literal water

    Key verses: Gen 1:2, Gen 7:17-20, Gen 9:11,15, Isa 54:9

    Relevance: Water as the medium of the first universal judgment (flood) is contrasted with fire as the medium of the final universal judgment in Peter's argument.

    H2319 — chadash¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H2319-chadash.md

    Occurrences: 53

    Chadash (new, fresh) — 53 tokens in 48 verses across 20 books. From the verb chadash (H2318, to be new, to renew). Used in Isa 65:17 for 'new heavens and a new earth' (shamayim chadashim ve'erets chadashah). The root verb means 'to renew' — the adjective carries connotations of renewal/refreshment. The Greek kainos (G2537) corresponds to chadash in the new-heavens/earth chain.

    Semantic categories: qualitatively new, renewed, fresh

    Key verses: Isa 65:17, Isa 66:22, Jer 31:31, Lam 3:23

    Relevance: The Hebrew 'new' in Isaiah's new heavens/earth comes from a root meaning 'to renew' — supporting renewal/transformation over annihilation/re-creation.

    G5179 — typos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5179-typos.md

    Occurrences: 16

    Typos (type, pattern, figure, example, impression) — 16 tokens in 15 verses. From typto (G5180, to beat/strike). Ranges from physical mark (nail print, John 20:25) through architectural pattern (Heb 8:5) to ethical model (Phil 3:17) to typological/doctrinal type (Rom 5:14 — Adam as typos of Christ, 1 Cor 10:6,11 — OT events as ensamples for us). The typological sense establishes that OT events can formally prefigure future events, validating the flood-as-type-of-fire-judgment reading.

    Semantic categories: physical mark, architectural pattern, ethical model, warning example, prophetic type

    Key verses: Rom 5:14, 1 Cor 10:6, 1 Cor 10:11, Heb 8:5

    Relevance: The NT typological vocabulary (typos, antitypos, hupodeigma, deigma) validates reading OT events as patterns of future realities — the framework within which the flood-fire connection operates.

    G2889 — kosmos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2889-kosmos.md

    Occurrences: 187

    Kosmos (world, ordered arrangement) — 187 tokens in 152 verses across 20 books. Peter uses kosmos in 2 Pet 2:5 (archaios kosmos, 'old/ancient world'; kosmos asebon, 'world of ungodly') and 2 Pet 3:6 (ho tote kosmos, 'the world that then was' — the world that perished in the flood). The scope term: kosmos indicates the entire ordered world, not just a region. Peter's argument requires that the flood destroyed the whole kosmos, validating the claim that fire will likewise judge the whole present creation.

    Semantic categories: universe/creation, earth's surface, human inhabitants, moral system opposed to God

    Key verses: 2 Pet 2:5, 2 Pet 3:6, John 3:16, 1 John 2:15-17

    Relevance: Peter's use of kosmos for the flood's scope establishes the universal precedent that supports a universal fire judgment.

    G2920 — krisis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2920-krisis.md

    Occurrences: 47

    Krisis (judgment, decision, condemnation) — 47 occurrences. From krino (G2919, to judge). Used in 2 Pet 3:7 (hemeran kriseos, 'day of judgment') and 2 Pet 2:9 (eis hemeran kriseos, 'unto the day of judgment'). The 'day of judgment' is the appointed time for both the reserved fire and the perdition of ungodly men.

    Semantic categories: judicial decision, day of judgment, condemnation, punishment

    Key verses: 2 Pet 3:7, 2 Pet 2:9, Matt 10:15, Jude 1:6

    Relevance: The 'day of judgment' is the temporal anchor of Peter's flood-to-fire argument — the fire is reserved 'against' (eis) this specific day.

    G3952 — parousia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3952-parousia.md

    Occurrences: 24

    Parousia (coming, presence, arrival) — 24 NT occurrences. Technical term for Christ's second coming. Used in Matt 24:37,39 for the parousia of the Son of Man compared to the days of Noah. Also in 2 Pet 3:4 ('Where is the promise of his coming?') — the scoffers deny the parousia, which Peter refutes by the flood-to-fire argument. The parousia is the event that triggers the fire judgment Peter describes.

    Semantic categories: second coming of Christ, physical presence, royal arrival

    Key verses: Matt 24:37, Matt 24:39, 2 Pet 3:4, 2 Pet 3:12, 1 Thess 4:15

    Relevance: The parousia is what the scoffers deny (2 Pet 3:4) and what the flood-fire argument establishes — the same God who brought the flood will bring fire at the parousia.

    G1349 — dike¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1349-dike.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Dike (justice, punishment, vengeance) — only 4 NT occurrences. Used in Jude 1:7 (diken hypechousai, 'undergoing/suffering punishment') for Sodom's fire judgment. The root of the entire dik- family (dikaiosyne, dikaios, etc.). Rare but significant: Jude uses the root concept of justice itself for Sodom's fate.

    Semantic categories: judicial verdict, retributive justice, divine penalty

    Key verses: Jude 1:7, 2 Thess 1:9, Acts 25:15, Acts 28:4

    Relevance: Dike in Jude 1:7 describes the punishment Sodom 'undergoes' (present tense) — the ongoing state of having suffered permanent fire destruction, serving as the deigma (example) of future fire judgment.

    H776 — erets¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H776-erets.md

    Occurrences: 2665

    Erets (earth, land) — the most common geographical term in the OT with 2,665 tokens. Used in Gen 1:1 (God created the heaven and the earth), Gen 6:11-13 (earth was corrupt/filled with violence), Gen 8:1 (wind passed over the earth), Gen 9:11 (no more flood to destroy the earth), Isa 65:17 (new earth). The same erets that was created, corrupted, flooded, and renewed is the erets promised to be made new (chadash). The continuity of the term throughout the creation-flood-re-creation cycle supports the view that the 'new earth' is the same earth renewed.

    Semantic categories: the physical earth, a nation's land, ground/soil

    Key verses: Gen 1:1, Gen 6:11-13, Gen 8:1, Gen 9:11, Isa 65:17

    Relevance: The continuity of erets through creation, corruption, flood, and renewal supports the transformation model over annihilation — the same earth endures through judgment.

    H8064 — shamayim¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H8064-shamayim.md

    Occurrences: 439

    Shamayim (heaven, heavens, sky) — 439 tokens in 395 verses across 33 books. Grammatically dual/plural. Used with erets (H776) to designate the totality of creation: 'the heavens and the earth' (Gen 1:1). Peter picks up this pair: 'the heavens were of old, and the earth' (2 Pet 3:5); 'the heavens and the earth which are now' (2 Pet 3:7); 'new heavens and a new earth' (2 Pet 3:13). Isaiah uses the same pair: 'new heavens and a new earth' (Isa 65:17, 66:22).

    Semantic categories: physical sky/atmosphere, celestial realm, God's dwelling, totality of creation (with erets)

    Key verses: Gen 1:1, Isa 65:17, Isa 66:22, 2 Pet 3:5,7,13

    Relevance: The shamayim + erets pair marks the total created order — the same pair appears in creation, flood narrative, Isaiah's new-creation promise, and Peter's fire-to-new-creation sequence.

    H1254 — bara¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1254-bara.md

    Occurrences: 59

    Bara (to create) — 59 tokens in 46 verses. Subject is always God (in the creation sense). Used in Gen 1:1 (God created heaven and earth) and Isa 65:17 ('I create new heavens and a new earth'). The same verb for original creation and new creation. Concentrated in Genesis and Isaiah (the two creation poles). If God bara-creates in Isa 65:17 as in Gen 1:1, the new creation is a divine creative act of the same order as the original.

    Semantic categories: divine creation ex nihilo, divine creative renewal

    Key verses: Gen 1:1, Gen 1:27, Isa 65:17, Isa 45:18

    Relevance: Bara in Isa 65:17 uses the same verb as Gen 1:1 for the new creation — the new heavens and earth are a divine creative act, completing the creation-judgment-re-creation cycle.
