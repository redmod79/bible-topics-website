    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Does the Bible say believers have any authority or power in anything? The user suspects this is an unbiblical concept — that any authority or power is from Jesus Christ through the Holy Spirit, and depends on faith, obedience, fasting, prayer — nothing in yourself.

    G1849 — exousia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1849-exousia.md

    Occurrences: 103

    Authority, right, permission — from exesti (it is lawful/permitted). Denotes delegated authority or vested right to act. Distinguished from dynamis (inherent power/force). KJV translates as 'power' 59%, 'authority' 25%. Highest density in Revelation (eschatological authority) and Pauline cosmic-hierarchy letters. Key semantic categories: divine/delegated authority, personal capacity/right, governmental/judicial authority, cosmic/angelic authorities, and authority over demons (always given by a higher source).

    Semantic categories: delegated authority, right to act, governmental power, cosmic powers, jurisdiction

    Key verses: Mat 28:18, John 19:11, Rom 13:1, Mat 10:1, Luke 9:1, Luke 10:19

    Relevance: The core word for authority in the NT. In every case where believers exercise exousia, it is explicitly 'given' (didomi) by Christ or God — never self-generated. The delegation pattern is consistent across all Gospels and Acts.

    G1411 — dynamis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1411-dynamis.md

    Occurrences: 120

    Force, power, ability — inherent capability or miraculous power. From dynamai (to be able). Distinguished from exousia (right/permission). KJV translates as 'power' 60%, 'mighty works' 10%, 'strength' 6%. Spans inherent divine force, Spirit's power in ministry, miraculous works, and cosmic powers. Highest density in Pauline epistles, reflecting his theology of divine power in weakness.

    Semantic categories: God's inherent power, Spirit's power in ministry, miraculous works, resurrection power, cosmic powers

    Key verses: Acts 1:8, Rom 1:16, 1 Cor 1:24, 2 Cor 12:9, Eph 1:19, Luke 10:19

    Relevance: The distinction between dynamis (inherent power) and exousia (delegated authority) is the lexical backbone of this study. Luke 10:19 uses both in one verse: exousia is given to disciples; dynamis belongs to the enemy. Believers receive permission (exousia) but not inherent power (dynamis) — the power remains God's.

    G2904 — kratos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2904-krátos.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Sovereign dominion power, manifested might. Used almost exclusively of God or Christ in the NT. In Eph 6:10 paired with ischus (inherent might) as 'the power of his might' — both belong to God. Also used in doxologies ascribing dominion to God. One notable exception: Heb 2:14 uses kratos for the devil's 'power of death,' which Christ destroys.

    Semantic categories: divine effective power, ascribed dominion and glory, rule over death

    Key verses: Eph 1:19, Eph 6:10, 1 Pet 5:11, Heb 2:14, Rev 5:13

    Relevance: One of three power words stacked in Eph 6:10 — all belonging to God. The same vocabulary describes God's resurrection power in Eph 1:19. Never attributed to believers independently.

    G2479 — ischus¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2479-ischýs.md

    Occurrences: 11

    Inherent might, strength — the capacity or ability one possesses by nature. Distinguished from dynamis (operative power in action) and kratos (dominion/ruling force). Used in the 'greatest commandment' passages (Mark 12:30, Luke 10:27 — 'with all thy strength') and in Pauline power triads (Eph 1:19, 6:10). In Revelation doxologies, ascribed to God/Christ.

    Semantic categories: inherent capacity, divine might in doxologies, operative divine power

    Key verses: Eph 1:19, Eph 6:10, Mark 12:30, Rev 5:12, Rev 7:12

    Relevance: The third power word in the Eph 6:10 triad. Ischus represents inherent might — and in Eph 6:10, it belongs to God (autou, 'his'). When used of what humans owe God ('love the Lord with all your strength'), it refers to the totality of one's capacity directed toward God — not independent authority.

    G1743 — endunamoo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1743-endynamoo.md

    Occurrences: 9

    To empower, to strengthen — from en (in) + dynamoo (to make powerful). Used for divine empowerment for service (1 Tim 1:12, Phil 4:13), strengthening in faith (Rom 4:20, Acts 9:22), exhortation to strength (Eph 6:10, 2 Tim 2:1), and overcoming weakness (Heb 11:34). Always indicates power placed INTO someone from an external source.

    Semantic categories: divine empowerment for service, strengthening in faith, exhortation to strength, overcoming weakness

    Key verses: Eph 6:10, Phil 4:13, 2 Tim 2:1, 1 Tim 1:12, Rom 4:20

    Relevance: Critical word in Phil 4:13 ('Christ which strengtheneth me') and Eph 6:10 ('be strong in the Lord'). In Eph 6:10: endunamousthe is present passive imperative — 'keep being strengthened' by another. The passive construction proves the believer is the recipient, not the source. In Phil 4:13: present active participle — Christ 'continuously infuses strength.'

    G3686 — onoma¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3686-onoma.md

    Occurrences: 228

    Name — not merely a verbal label but the person's identity, character, authority, and covenantal presence. From the base of ginosko (G1097, to know). In the NT, 'in the name of Jesus' (en to onomati) places the believer inside the sphere of Christ's authority. Highest concentration in Acts (59x) for apostolic proclamation 'in the name of Jesus.' The power is the PERSON's, available through the name — not the name-user's own power.

    Semantic categories: personal designation, identity/person reference, authority/representation, divine name invocation, reputation/character

    Key verses: Acts 3:6, Acts 3:16, Acts 4:10-12, Acts 16:18, Mark 16:17, Phil 2:9-10

    Relevance: Central to the 'in the name of Jesus' mechanism. The name is not a formula; it represents the person, authority, and power of Christ. To act 'in the name' is to act as Christ's authorized representative — invoking HIS authority, not claiming one's own. The Sons of Sceva episode (Acts 19:13-16) demonstrates that the name without genuine relationship is powerless.

    G1097 — ginosko¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1097-ginosko.md

    Occurrences: 246

    To know — knowledge gained through experience, observation, or personal encounter. Distinguished from oida (G1492, settled intuitive knowledge) and epistamai (G1987, factual awareness). 223 NT occurrences. Highest concentration in John (56x) and 1 John (25x). In Acts 19:15, the demon uses ginosko for Jesus — personal, experiential recognition of His authority.

    Semantic categories: experiential knowledge, learning/discovery, understanding, sexual knowledge (Hebraism), recognition of authority

    Key verses: Acts 19:15, John 17:3, 1 John 2:3, Matt 7:23

    Relevance: In Acts 19:15, the demon says 'Jesus I KNOW (ginosko)' — personal, experiential recognition of authority. Contrasted with epistamai for Paul. The demon recognizes Jesus's inherent authority through direct encounter.

    G1987 — epistamai¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1987-epístamai.md

    Occurrences: 14

    To put the mind upon, to comprehend, to be acquainted with — factual awareness or intellectual comprehension. 14 NT occurrences, concentrated in Acts (9x). Distinguished from ginosko (experiential/relational knowledge). In Acts 19:15, the demon uses epistamai for Paul — factual awareness of his delegated ministry, not personal recognition of inherent authority.

    Semantic categories: personal acquaintance, intellectual comprehension, recognition/acknowledgment, uncertainty of knowledge, instinctive knowledge

    Key verses: Acts 19:15, Acts 10:28, Mark 14:68, James 4:14

    Relevance: The Acts 19:15 contrast is pivotal: the demon uses ginosko for Jesus (personal recognition of inherent authority) and epistamai for Paul (factual awareness of delegated ministry). This two-word distinction reveals a three-tier authority hierarchy: Jesus (inherent), Paul (delegated), Sceva's sons (none).

    G769 — astheneia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G769-astheneia.md

    Occurrences: 24

    Weakness, infirmity, feebleness — from alpha privative + sthenos (strength). 24 NT occurrences, heavily concentrated in Paul (12/24, 50%), especially 2 Corinthians. Paul theologizes weakness as the venue for divine power. The root meaning is 'without strength' — the exact opposite of any concept of inherent authority.

    Semantic categories: physical illness, moral/spiritual frailty, human limitation as venue for divine power, eschatological transformation

    Key verses: 2 Cor 12:9, 2 Cor 12:10, 2 Cor 11:30, Heb 4:15, Rom 8:26

    Relevance: The inversely proportional relationship between astheneia (weakness) and dynamis (divine power) in 2 Cor 12:9 directly contradicts inherent believer authority. Paul explicitly chooses weakness so that divine power may rest upon him.

    G4102 — pistis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4102-pistis.md

    Occurrences: 244

    Faith, trust, belief — the mechanism of dependence on God. Used as the necessary condition for spiritual power (Matt 17:20, Acts 3:16). Faith is itself a gift from God (Eph 2:8, Phil 1:29), authored by Jesus (Heb 12:2), produced by the Spirit (Gal 5:22), generated through the Word (Rom 10:17). It is the posture of receiving, not possessing.

    Semantic categories: trust in God, fidelity/faithfulness, body of belief, persuasion

    Key verses: Eph 2:8, Heb 12:2, Rom 10:17, Matt 17:20, Acts 3:16, Gal 5:22

    Relevance: Faith is the mechanism by which believers access divine power, but faith itself is not a human achievement — it is God's gift. The Matt 17:20 passage identifies unbelief (lack of faith = lack of dependence) as the reason the disciples could not cast out the demon.

    G4151 — pneuma¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4151-pneuma.md

    Occurrences: 385

    Spirit — the source of all spiritual power. The Holy Spirit distributes gifts sovereignly (1 Cor 12:11, 'as HE wills'). Acts 1:8 establishes that the power believers receive IS the Holy Spirit. Even Christ ministered by the Spirit's power (Matt 12:28, Luke 4:14). The Spirit is not a tool believers wield; the Spirit is a divine Person with sovereign will.

    Semantic categories: Holy Spirit as divine Person, spiritual power source, human spirit, wind/breath

    Key verses: Acts 1:8, 1 Cor 12:11, Eph 5:18, Matt 12:28, Gal 5:22-23, Rom 8:26

    Relevance: The Spirit is the sole source of spiritual power. Believers are filled BY the Spirit (passive), not possessors OF independent power. Even the fruit of the Spirit (Gal 5:22-23) is the Spirit's fruit, not the believer's.

    G1754 — energeo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1754-energeo.md

    Occurrences: 22

    To work, operate, be effective — the same word describes both God's action and human action flowing from it. In Phil 2:13, energeo is used for BOTH God working IN believers and the resulting human work. 22 occurrences with God as subject. The word demonstrates that human spiritual agency is real but its power source is divine.

    Semantic categories: divine operative power, human action from divine source, effective working

    Key verses: Phil 2:13, Eph 1:20, Gal 5:6, Col 1:29

    Relevance: The Phil 2:13 usage is key: 'God who is working (ho energon) in you both the willing and the doing.' The same word (energeo) describes God's action and human response — they are not independent but causally connected.

    G3306 — meno¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3306-meno.md

    Occurrences: 128

    To abide, remain, continue — the word underlying the vine/branches metaphor. Used extensively by John to describe the continuous connection between believer and Christ that enables spiritual life and fruitfulness. Present participle (ho menon) indicates ongoing, continuous action — abiding is not a one-time decision but a permanent posture.

    Semantic categories: remaining in Christ, continuous relationship, permanent dwelling

    Key verses: John 15:4-5, John 15:7, 1 John 2:27-28, 1 John 3:24

    Relevance: The vine/branches metaphor depends on meno — the continuous connection that enables power. Spiritual life flows from Christ to the believer continuously, not as a one-time deposit. A severed branch (one who does not meno) withers and produces nothing.

    G1325 — didomi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1325-didomi.md

    Occurrences: 430

    To give, grant, bestow — the verb consistently used when authority is conferred on believers. In Matt 10:1 (edoken, aorist), Luke 9:1 (edoken, aorist), Luke 10:19 (dedoka, perfect) — authority is always GIVEN by Christ at a specific moment. The consistent use of didomi proves authority is delegated, never self-generated.

    Semantic categories: bestowing authority, granting gifts, divine giving

    Key verses: Matt 10:1, Luke 9:1, Luke 10:19, Matt 28:18, John 19:11

    Relevance: Every NT instance of believers receiving authority uses didomi — it is always 'given.' No NT text uses language suggesting believers generate, possess, or develop their own authority. Even Christ's authority was 'given' (Matt 28:18, edothe — aorist passive).

    G5293 — hupotasso¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5293-hupotasso.md

    Occurrences: 49

    To submit, subject, subordinate — to place under the authority of another. In James 4:7, hupotasso (submission to God) precedes anthistemi (resistance to the devil). The order is theological: submission to God's authority is the prerequisite for exercising delegated authority over the enemy.

    Semantic categories: submission to authority, ordering under, voluntary subordination

    Key verses: James 4:7, Rom 13:1, 1 Cor 15:27-28, Eph 5:21, 1 Pet 5:5

    Relevance: James 4:7 uses hupotagete (aorist passive imperative) — 'be submitted to God' — BEFORE 'resist the devil.' The passive voice suggests being placed under God's authority by an external act. Submission precedes authority — without it, resistance is unauthorized.

    G5485 — charis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5485-charis.md

    Occurrences: 156

    Grace — the external power source behind Paul's agency. In 1 Cor 15:10, Paul attributes both identity and labor to grace ('by the grace of God I am what I am'). Grace is the unmerited, external enablement from God — the opposite of inherent authority.

    Semantic categories: divine favor, unmerited enablement, gift, gratitude

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:10, 2 Cor 12:9, Eph 2:8, James 4:6, Tit 2:11

    Relevance: Grace is the mechanism by which God's power operates through believers. It is unmerited (ruling out earned authority), external (ruling out inherent authority), and continuous (ruling out a one-time deposit of authority).

    H2428 — chayil¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H2428-chayil.md

    Occurrences: 260

    Force, might, army strength, wealth, virtue, valor — a broad Hebrew power word covering military might, personal capability, and resources. 260 OT occurrences. Translated as 'valour' (13%), 'army' (6%), 'strength' (various, ~5%). In Zech 4:6, chayil is explicitly negated in favor of God's Spirit.

    Semantic categories: military might, personal valor, wealth/resources, capable woman (Prov 31)

    Key verses: Zech 4:6, Deut 8:18, Psa 33:16, Prov 31:10

    Relevance: In Zech 4:6, chayil (army/organizational might) is one of two power words negated in favor of God's Spirit. If military/organizational strength is insufficient for God's work, how much less is personal spiritual 'authority'?

    H3581 — koach¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H3581-koach.md

    Occurrences: 144

    Power, force, capacity — from a root meaning 'to be firm.' 144 OT occurrences. Translated as 'power' (17%), 'strength' (14%). Covers physical strength, divine power, political/military might. In Zech 4:6, koach is explicitly negated alongside chayil — both human power types are insufficient.

    Semantic categories: physical strength, divine power and sovereignty, political/military might, human capacity

    Key verses: Zech 4:6, Isa 40:29, Jer 10:12, Dan 8:24

    Relevance: In Zech 4:6, koach (inherent force/capacity) is the second power word negated. In Isa 40:29, God GIVES koach to the faint — the power is transferred from God, not self-generated. The combination of chayil and koach being negated covers both organizational and personal power.

    G3982 — peitho¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3982-peitho.md

    Occurrences: 62

    To persuade, be persuaded, trust — the actual submission word in the NT reflecting participatory yielding rather than coercive compliance.

    Semantic categories: persuasion, trust, obedience through conviction

    Key verses: Heb 13:17, Gal 5:10, 2 Cor 5:11

    Relevance: CBE International notes that NT submission language uses peitho ('be persuaded') rather than demanding blind obedience — reflecting that even human authority structures in the church are participatory, not coercive.

    G4561 — sarx¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4561-sarx.md

    Occurrences: 151

    Flesh — human nature in its weakness and inability. In Rom 7:18, Paul admits 'in me (that is, in my flesh,) dwelleth no good thing.' The flesh represents the human capacity without the Spirit — inherently unable to produce spiritual results.

    Semantic categories: human nature, physical body, weakness/inability, sinful nature

    Key verses: Rom 7:18, Rom 8:3-4, Gal 5:17, Jer 17:5

    Relevance: The flesh (sarx) in Paul's theology is the human capacity without the Spirit — inherently insufficient for spiritual work. Jer 17:5 pronounces a curse on making 'flesh' one's arm/strength. This directly contradicts any concept of inherent human spiritual authority.

    G4982 — sozo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4982-sozo.md

    Occurrences: 120

    To save, deliver, heal — comprehensive salvation term. Salvation itself is God's action, not a human achievement.

    Semantic categories: salvation, deliverance, healing

    Key verses: Eph 2:8, Acts 4:12, Rom 10:9

    Relevance: Even salvation — the foundational spiritual reality — is God's action received by faith, not a human achievement. If salvation itself is not self-generated, how much less can spiritual authority be inherent?
