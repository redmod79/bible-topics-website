    Verses¶

    Question¶

    Does the Bible say believers have any authority or power in anything? The user suspects this is an unbiblical concept — that any authority or power is from Jesus Christ through the Holy Spirit, and depends on faith, obedience, fasting, prayer — nothing in yourself.

    Jesus gives the twelve authority over unclean spirits¶

    And when he had called unto him his twelve disciples, he gave them power against unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal all manner of sickness and all manner of disease.

    Context: Jesus commissions the twelve disciples for their first independent mission. The authority is given at a specific moment (aorist edoken) for a specific geographic scope (Matt 10:5-6, 'lost sheep of Israel') and specific purposes (casting out demons, healing).

    Notes: Greek confirms edoken (aorist of didomi G1325) — specific moment of bestowal. exousia (G1849) is what is given. Luke's parallel (9:1) adds dynamis (G1411) alongside exousia, making explicit that both right AND ability were given — the disciples had neither inherently.

    Sent two by two with power over unclean spirits¶

    And he called unto him the twelve, and began to send them forth by two and two; and gave them power over unclean spirits;

    Context: Mark's parallel to the commissioning. Uses 'gave them power' — same delegation pattern as Matthew.

    Notes: Mark uses exousia for the authority given. The 'two by two' sending pattern reinforces dependence — they were not sent alone. Gill's commentary: 'Christ did the same things, but by a power inherent in himself' — the disciples' authority was derivative.

    Jesus gives BOTH dynamis AND exousia — the fullest delegation text¶

    Then he called his twelve disciples together, and gave them power and authority over all devils, and to cure diseases.

    Context: Luke's parallel uniquely uses BOTH Greek power words. Jesus gave dynamis (ability/force) AND exousia (authority/right) — because the disciples possessed neither inherently.

    Notes: This is the most explicit delegation text in the Gospels. edoken (G1325, aorist active) + dynamis (G1411) + exousia (G1849). The fact that Jesus needed to give BOTH ability AND authority proves the disciples had neither on their own. Bengel: 'Great is the authority of conferring authority.'

    The seventy return — devils subject through Christ's name¶

    And the seventy returned again with joy, saying, Lord, even the devils are subject unto us through thy name. And he said unto them, I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven. Behold, I give unto you power to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy: and nothing shall by any means hurt you. Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.

    Context: The seventy (or seventy-two) report back after their mission. They identify the source of authority — 'through thy name.' Jesus redirects their joy from power to relationship (names written in heaven).

    Notes: v.17: The seventy themselves identify the source — 'through thy name' (dia tou onomatos sou). v.19: dedoka (perfect of didomi G1325, 'I have given') — perfect tense indicates already bestowed. exousia (G1849) is given to disciples; dynamis (G1411) belongs to 'the enemy.' Two different words in one verse: believers receive delegated permission (exousia) over the enemy's inherent power (dynamis). v.20: Jesus redirects — don't rejoice in authority over spirits, rejoice in relationship with God.

    Conditions for authority: prayer and fasting as dependence¶

    And when he was come into the house, his disciples asked him privately, Why could not we cast him out? And he said unto them, This kind can come forth by nothing, but by prayer and fasting.

    Context: After the disciples' failed exorcism of the epileptic boy (Mark 9:14-27). Jesus identifies the cause of failure: the disciples tried to use delegated authority as if it were inherent. Prayer and fasting are means of dependence on God, not techniques for accumulating personal power.

    Notes: N1904 text has only 'prayer' (proseuche G4335), not 'prayer and fasting' — the 'and fasting' is a textual variant. But the theological point holds either way: spiritual authority requires continuous dependence on God through prayer. Matt 17:20 gives the prior reason: 'Because of your unbelief.' Strauss: 'the disciples have derivative authority and need to acknowledge their dependence on God by praying that God will help them cast out the demon, whereas Jesus has personal authority.'

    Disciples' failure attributed to unbelief — faith as root condition¶

    Then came the disciples to Jesus apart, and said, Why could not we cast him out? And Jesus said unto them, Because of your unbelief: for verily I say unto you, If ye have faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye shall say unto this mountain, Remove hence to yonder place; and it shall remove; and nothing shall be impossible unto you.

    Context: Matthew's parallel to the failed exorcism. Jesus identifies unbelief (apistia) as the root cause — a failure of dependence, not a failure of technique.

    Notes: The root issue is faith (pistis G4102) — which is itself a posture of dependence, not a personal possession. The mustard seed analogy suggests quality (genuine trust), not quantity. Even small genuine faith can move mountains because the power is God's — the faith is merely the connection to that power.

    Signs that follow believers — in my name¶

    And these signs shall follow them that believe; In my name shall they cast out devils; they shall speak with new tongues; They shall take up serpents; and if they drink any deadly thing, it shall not hurt them; they shall lay hands on the sick, and they shall recover.

    Context: The longer ending of Mark. Signs are described as following 'them that believe' — but always 'in my name.' The mechanism is Christ's name (His authority), not the believer's independent power.

    Notes: The phrase 'in my name' (en to onomati mou) governs all the signs listed. The signs FOLLOW believers — they don't originate FROM believers. The believers' role is faith and invocation of Christ's name; the power behind the signs is Christ's.

    Peter explicitly denies personal power — attributes healing to Christ's name¶

    Then Peter said, Silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk. ... And when Peter saw it, he answered unto the people, Ye men of Israel, why marvel ye at this? or why look ye so earnestly on us, as though by our own power or holiness we had made this man to walk? The God of Abraham, and of Isaac, and of Jacob, the God of our fathers, hath glorified his Son Jesus; whom ye delivered up, and denied him in the presence of Pilate, when he was determined to let him go. But ye denied the Holy One and the Just, and desired a murderer to be granted unto you; And killed the Prince of life, whom God hath raised from the dead; whereof we are witnesses. And his name through faith in his name hath made this man strong, whom ye see and know: yea, the faith which is by him hath given him this perfect soundness in the presence of you all.

    Context: Peter heals a lame man at the Beautiful Gate, then immediately addresses the crowd to deny personal power. This is the single most explicit denial of 'believer's authority' in the NT.

    Notes: v.12: Peter denies BOTH idia dynamis (own power, G1411) AND eusebeia (personal holiness/piety, G2150) as causes. The idia (G2398, 'own') intensifies the denial — it is specifically their OWN power that Peter rejects. v.16: The grammatical subject doing the strengthening is 'his name' (to onoma autou), not the apostles. Faith is 'by him' (di autou) — even the faith that enables the miracle is attributed to Christ. Peter was 'filled with the Holy Ghost' when he spoke (Acts 4:8).

    Peter before the council — by what power or name?¶

    And when they had set them in the midst, they asked, By what power, or by what name, have ye done this? Then Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, said unto them, Ye rulers of the people, and elders of Israel, If we this day be examined of the good deed done to the impotent man, by what means he is made whole; Be it known unto you all, and to all the people of Israel, that by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom ye crucified, whom God raised from the dead, even by him doth this man stand here before you whole. This is the stone which was set at nought of you builders, which is become the head of the corner. Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.

    Context: Peter and John before the Sanhedrin. Peter again attributes everything to 'the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth.' Note: Peter was 'filled with the Holy Ghost' (v.8) — the Spirit empowered even his testimony.

    Notes: v.7: The council asks 'by what power (dynamis) or by what name (onoma)?' — they recognize these as the two possible sources. v.8: Peter is 'filled with the Holy Ghost' — even his bold testimony is Spirit-empowered, not self-generated. v.10: 'by the name of Jesus Christ' — Peter's consistent pattern of attribution. v.12: 'there is none other name' — the exclusivity of Christ's authority.

    Paul casts out spirit — in the name of Jesus Christ¶

    And this did she many days. But Paul, being grieved, turned and said to the spirit, I command thee in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her. And he came out the same hour.

    Context: Paul casts out a spirit of divination from a slave girl in Philippi. He explicitly invokes 'the name of Jesus Christ' — not his own authority.

    Notes: Paul follows the same pattern as Peter: exorcism is performed 'in the name of Jesus Christ' (en to onomati Iesou Christou). The authority is Christ's, exercised through Paul as His agent.

    Greater works — through Christ, asked in His name¶

    Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that believeth on me, the works that I do shall he do also; and greater works than these shall he do; because I go unto my Father. And whatsoever ye shall ask in my name, that will I do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son. If ye shall ask any thing in my name, I will do it.

    Context: Jesus's promise of 'greater works.' The mechanism is critical: 'because I go unto my Father' (sending the Spirit) and 'in my name' (under Christ's authority). The believer asks; Christ does.

    Notes: v.12: The 'greater works' are possible not because of the believer's authority but 'because I go unto my Father' — the sending of the Spirit. v.13-14: 'I will do it' — Christ is the actor; the believer is the asker. The purpose: 'that the Father may be glorified in the Son' — not that the believer may be glorified.

    Sons of Sceva fail — authority without relationship¶

    Then certain of the vagabond Jews, exorcists, took upon them to call over them which had evil spirits the name of the Lord Jesus, saying, We adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preacheth. And there were seven sons of one Sceva, a Jew, and chief of the priests, which did so. And the evil spirit answered and said, Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye? And the man in whom the evil spirit was leaped on them, and overcame them, and prevailed against them, so that they fled out of that house naked and wounded.

    Context: The definitive negative example. The sons of Sceva attempt to use the name of Jesus as a formula without genuine relationship. The demon's response reveals a three-tier authority hierarchy.

    Notes: v.15: Two different Greek verbs. The demon uses ginosko (G1097, present active indicative — personal, experiential recognition) for Jesus and epistamai (G1987, present middle/passive indicative — factual awareness) for Paul. Three-tier hierarchy: Jesus (inherent authority, recognized and submitted to), Paul (delegated ministry, known about), Sceva's sons (no connection at all — 'who are ye?'). v.16: The physical violence demonstrates that unauthorized use of spiritual authority is not merely ineffective but dangerous. MacLaren: 'the name of Jesus is spoiled and is powerless on the lips of one who repeats it, parrot-like.'

    Submit to God PRECEDES resist the devil¶

    Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

    Context: James's instruction on spiritual warfare. The ORDER is theological: submission to God (hupotasso, aorist passive imperative) comes BEFORE resistance to the devil (anthistemi, aorist active imperative).

    Notes: hupotagete (aorist passive imperative of hupotasso G5293) — 'be submitted to God.' antistete (aorist active imperative of anthistemi G436) — 'resist the devil.' The oun (therefore) connects to the preceding context about humility and grace. The sequence is not accidental: submission to God is the prerequisite for authority to resist the devil. Without submission, resistance is unauthorized — like the sons of Sceva.

    Vine and branches — absolute dependence for spiritual fruitfulness¶

    I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman. Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away: and every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit. Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you. Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me. I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing. If a man abide not in me, he is cast forth as a branch, and is withered; and men gather them, and cast them into the fire, and they are burned. If ye abide in me, and my words abide in you, ye shall ask what ye will, and it shall be done unto you. Herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much fruit; so shall ye be my disciples.

    Context: Jesus's metaphor of the vine and branches, spoken at the Last Supper. The metaphor is biological: a branch has no independent life-sustaining capacity. This is ontological, not aspirational.

    Notes: v.5: choris emou (G5565 + G1473) = 'apart from me' / 'severed from me' — spatial/relational separation, not merely absence. ou (G3756) dynasthe (G1410, present middle/passive indicative, 'are able') poiein (G4160) ouden (G3762) = emphatic double negative, 'absolutely nothing.' The present tense dynasthe indicates ongoing inability, not momentary weakness. meno (G3306) = 'abide/remain' — present active participle, continuous action. The vine/branch metaphor is the definitive statement on dependence: the branch genuinely bears fruit, but the life producing the fruit comes entirely from the vine.

    Strength is made perfect in weakness — the inversion principle¶

    And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me. Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong.

    Context: Paul's response to the 'thorn in the flesh.' God's answer to Paul's prayer: not removal of weakness, but sufficiency of grace. The inversely proportional relationship between human weakness and divine power directly contradicts inherent believer authority.

    Notes: v.9: dynamis (G1411) = power. astheneia (G769) = weakness. teleitai (G5055, present passive indicative) = 'is being brought to completion' — ongoing, passive. The power is God's (he dynamis), and it reaches fullest expression IN weakness. episkenose (G1981, aorist active subjunctive) = 'might tabernacle/rest upon' — the power of Christ takes up residence ON Paul, not IN Paul as a possession. v.10: 'when I am weak, then am I strong' — the paradox. Human weakness is the condition for divine power; human strength is an obstacle to it.

    Treasure in earthen vessels — power deliberately of God, not us¶

    But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us.

    Context: Paul explains why God uses weak human vessels: so that the power (dynamis) is clearly attributed to God, not to the vessel.

    Notes: The deliberately weak vessel ('earthen vessels') ensures proper attribution of power. The hina clause ('that the excellency of the power may be of God') states God's purpose: the power must be seen as God's, NOT as the believer's. This is not a secondary effect — it is the design purpose.

    Not sufficient of ourselves — sufficiency is of God¶

    Not that we are sufficient of ourselves to think any thing as of ourselves; but our sufficiency is of God;

    Context: Paul's statement about apostolic ministry. He denies self-sufficiency even for thinking, let alone acting with authority.

    Notes: hikanoi (sufficient/competent) — Paul denies personal competency. The sufficiency (hikanotes) is 'of God' (ek tou theou). Even the capacity to think rightly (logisasthai) comes from God, not from the apostle.

    I can do all things THROUGH Christ who strengthens me¶

    I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.

    Context: Paul's declaration of ability — but the ability is through Christ, not inherent. Often misused to support self-empowerment; the Greek shows the opposite.

    Notes: panta (all things) ischyo (G2480, present active indicative, 'I am strong/able') en (G1722, 'in' — vital union, not 'through' as independent channel) to (the one) endunamounti (G1743, present active participle, 'continuously empowering/strengthening') me (me). The present participle endunamounti indicates continuous, ongoing infusion of strength from Christ. The preposition en places Paul INSIDE Christ's empowering — vital union, not independent channeling. Robertson: endunamoo = 'to put power into one.' The power is Christ's, continuously poured into Paul.

    Work out salvation — for God works in you both to will and to do¶

    Wherefore, my beloved, as ye have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure.

    Context: The key synthesis text: believers genuinely 'work out' salvation BECAUSE God is genuinely 'working in' them. Human agency is real but its power source is divine.

    Notes: v.12: katergazesthe (G2716, present middle/passive imperative) = 'keep working out.' v.13: Theos gar estin ho energon (G1754, present active participle) en hymin = 'for it is God who is working in you.' energeo (G1754) is used for BOTH God's action (v.13) and the human action that flows from it — the same word describes both divine cause and human effect. God works both 'the willing' (to thelein) and 'the doing' (to energein) — even the desire to obey comes from God, not the believer.

    Power comes when the Holy Spirit comes — Spirit as source¶

    But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.

    Context: Jesus's final instruction before the ascension. The power (dynamis) believers receive IS the Holy Spirit — the Spirit's inherent ability flowing through them.

    Notes: lempsesthe dynamin = 'you will receive power.' The power is received, not generated. epelthontos tou hagiou pneumatos = 'the Holy Spirit having come upon' — the power arrives WITH the Spirit. The Spirit is not a tool believers wield; the Spirit is the power source itself. Passive voice constructions indicate believers are receivers, not generators.

    Be filled with the Spirit — continuous passive reception¶

    And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;

    Context: Paul's command about Spirit-filling. The grammatical construction is critical: present passive imperative — continuous, received from outside, commanded.

    Notes: plerousthe (G4137, present passive imperative) = 'keep being filled.' Present = continuous; passive = by someone else; imperative = commanded. The Spirit's filling is not a one-time event or a self-generated state — it is continuously received. The contrast with wine intoxication suggests the Spirit 'controls' the believer, not vice versa.

    Armor of God — three stacked power words, all God's¶

    Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might. Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand. Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth, and having on the breastplate of righteousness; And your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace; Above all, taking the shield of faith, wherewith ye shall be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked. And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God: Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, and watching thereunto with all perseverance and supplication for all saints;

    Context: The armor of God passage. Every piece of the armor belongs to God. The power vocabulary is triple-stacked and all attributed to God.

    Notes: v.10: Three power words: endunamousthe (G1743, present passive imperative — 'keep being strengthened' by another), kratei (G2904, dative — 'sovereign dominion power'), ischyos (G2479, genitive — 'inherent might/strength'). All three belong to God (autou = 'his'). The passive construction leaves zero room for inherent believer authority. These are the SAME words used in Eph 1:19 to describe God's resurrection power. The armor is 'the whole armour of GOD' — it is His armor, worn by the believer.

    The exceeding greatness of God's power toward believers¶

    And what is the exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe, according to the working of his mighty power, Which he wrought in Christ, when he raised him from the dead, and set him at his own right hand in the heavenly places, Far above all principality, and power, and might, and dominion, and every name that is named, not only in this world, but also in that which is to come:

    Context: Paul's prayer for the Ephesians to understand God's power toward believers. The power vocabulary matches Eph 6:10 — resurrection power, not human power.

    Notes: v.19: dynameos (G1411, genitive — 'power') autou (His). energeian (G1753 — 'working/operative energy') tou kratous (G2904, genitive — 'of the dominion power') tes ischyos (G2479, genitive — 'of the inherent might') autou (His). Four power words stacked: dynamis, energeia, kratos, ischus — all God's. v.20: energeken (G1754, perfect active indicative of energeo — 'He has worked') en to Christo — God worked this power IN Christ by raising Him from the dead. The SAME power vocabulary describes both resurrection power and the armor of God.

    Yet not I, but Christ liveth in me — the agency paradox¶

    I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.

    Context: Paul's statement of the 'yet not I' pattern. Human agency is real ('I live') but its source is redefined ('yet not I, but Christ').

    Notes: zo de ouketi ego (I live yet no longer I) — Paul affirms agency then immediately negates it as independent. ze de en emoi Christos (lives however in me Christ) — Christ is the living subject inside Paul. en pistei zo te tou huiou tou theou (in faith I live, the [faith] of the Son of God) — even the faith Paul lives by belongs to 'the Son of God.' Three present tense 'I live' (zo) statements in one verse, each one redefining the source of the living.

    By the grace of God I am what I am — yet not I, but grace¶

    But by the grace of God I am what I am: and his grace which was bestowed upon me was not in vain; but I laboured more abundantly than they all: yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me.

    Context: Paul's second 'yet not I' statement. He affirms his labor ('I laboured more abundantly') then redefines its source ('yet not I, but the grace of God').

    Notes: chariti (G5485) theou = 'by the grace of God.' Paul attributes both his identity ('I am what I am') and his labor ('I laboured') to grace. The 'yet not I' (ouk ego de) pattern: real agency with redefined power source. The grace was 'with me' (syn emoi) — accompanying presence, not internalized possession.

    Great Commission — grounded in Christ's authority, not ours¶

    And Jesus came and spake unto them, saying, All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.

    Context: The Great Commission. The grammatical structure is critical: Christ's authority claim (v.18, indicative) → 'therefore' (oun, conjunction) → 'go make disciples' (v.19, imperative). The commission flows FROM Christ's authority.

    Notes: v.18: Edothe (G1325, aorist passive indicative — 'was given') moi (to me) pasa exousia (all authority). Even Christ's authority is 'given' (by the Father). v.19: oun (G3767, therefore) — logically connects Christ's authority to the commission. poreuethentes (aorist passive participle — 'having gone') oun matheteasate (aorist active imperative — 'make disciples'). The authority claim is Christ's; the disciples operate under it. 'I am with you always' — the continuous presence that sustains the authority.

    No power except given from above¶

    Jesus answered, Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above: therefore he that delivered me unto thee hath the greater sin.

    Context: Jesus to Pilate. Even governmental authority over Christ is 'given from above.' The universal principle: all authority originates from God.

    Notes: exousian (G1849) = authority. ouk eiches (imperfect active — 'you would not have') — counterfactual. dedomenon (G1325, perfect passive participle — 'having been given') anothen (from above). Even the authority to condemn Christ was given by God. If Pilate's authority over Jesus himself was delegated, how much more is every other authority?

    No power but of God — universal principle of derived authority¶

    Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers. For there is no power but of God: the powers that be are ordained of God.

    Context: Paul's statement about governmental authority. The universal principle: ALL authority (exousia) comes from God — civil, spiritual, or otherwise.

    Notes: ou gar estin exousia (G1849) ei me hypo theou (for there is no authority except from God). This is the broadest statement of derived authority in the NT. Even governmental powers are 'ordained' (tetagmenai, perfect passive participle — 'having been arranged') by God. If ALL authority comes from God, then by definition no believer possesses inherent authority.

    Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit — OT foundation¶

    Then he answered and spake unto me, saying, This is the word of the LORD unto Zerubbabel, saying, Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.

    Context: God's message to Zerubbabel about rebuilding the temple. The foundational OT statement negating human power in favor of God's Spirit.

    Notes: lo (not) be-chayil (H2428, might/army strength) ve-lo (and not) be-koach (H3581, power/force) ki im (but rather) be-ruchi (H7307, my spirit + 1st person suffix). Both Hebrew power words (chayil and koach) are explicitly negated in favor of God's Spirit (ruach). chayil = military/organizational might (260 OT occurrences); koach = inherent force/capacity (144 occurrences). Both are declared insufficient — only God's Spirit accomplishes the work.

    Cursed is the man who trusts in man — OT trust dichotomy¶

    Thus saith the LORD; Cursed be the man that trusteth in man, and maketh flesh his arm, and whose heart departeth from the LORD. For he shall be like the heath in the desert, and shall not see when good cometh; but shall inhabit the parched places in the wilderness, in a salt land and not inhabited. Blessed is the man that trusteth in the LORD, and whose hope the LORD is. For he shall be as a tree planted by the waters, and that spreadeth out her roots by the river, and shall not see when heat cometh, but her leaf shall be green; and shall not be careful in the year of drought, neither shall cease from yielding fruit.

    Context: Jeremiah's trust dichotomy: cursed = trusting in man/flesh; blessed = trusting in the LORD. The imagery parallels John 15's vine/branches.

    Notes: arur (cursed) ha-geber (the man) asher yibtach (who trusts, H982) ba-adam (in man/humanity, H120) ve-sam basar zero'o (and makes flesh his arm). The Hebrew basar (flesh, H1320) as 'arm' = human strength relied upon. The tree planted by waters (v.8) parallels the branch abiding in the vine (John 15:4-5) — both depict dependence on an external life source. 'Neither shall cease from yielding fruit' — fruitfulness comes from being planted by the water, not from the tree's own resources.

    God shows Himself strong for those whose heart is perfect toward Him¶

    For the eyes of the LORD run to and fro throughout the whole earth, to shew himself strong in the behalf of them whose heart is perfect toward him. Herein thou hast done foolishly: therefore from henceforth thou shalt have wars.

    Context: Hanani the seer rebukes King Asa for relying on Syria rather than the LORD. God actively seeks those who depend on Him to display HIS strength on their behalf.

    Notes: le-hitchazeq (to show Himself strong, Hithpael of chazaq H2388) im (with/for) levavam shalem (whose heart is complete/undivided) elav (toward Him). God actively searches for dependence ('whose heart is perfect toward him') so He can display His power. The power is God's ('show HIMSELF strong'); the human contribution is wholehearted dependence.

    He gives power to the faint — they that wait upon the LORD¶

    He giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength. Even the youths shall faint and be weary, and the young men shall utterly fall: But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint.

    Context: Isaiah's promise of divine strength. The recipients are 'the faint' and those with 'no might' — the condition for receiving is weakness and waiting.

    Notes: v.29: noten (gives) koach (H3581, power) la-ya'ef (to the weary). The power is GIVEN by God to those who lack it. v.31: qovei YHWH (those who wait upon the LORD) yachalifu koach (shall renew/exchange strength). The verb chalaph (H2498) means to exchange/renew — they exchange their depleted strength for God's inexhaustible strength. Even youths (the strongest humans) faint, but those who WAIT on God receive supernatural strength.

    Even Jesus attributes exorcism to the Spirit of God¶

    But if I cast out devils by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God is come unto you.

    Context: Jesus's defense of His exorcism ministry. He attributes His own power over demons to the Spirit of God — even Christ operated in dependence on the Spirit.

    Notes: en pneumati theou (by the Spirit of God) — Jesus identifies the Spirit as the agent of His exorcisms. If Christ Himself did not exercise independent human power over demons, how much less can believers? This is devastating to any concept of inherent believer authority: even the Son operated by the Spirit's power during His earthly ministry.

    God chose the weak — that no flesh should glory¶

    But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty; And base things of the world, and things which are despised, hath God chosen, yea, and things which are not, to bring to nought things that are: That no flesh should glory in his presence.

    Context: Paul's explanation of God's selection principle: He deliberately chooses the weak, foolish, and despised so that no human can boast.

    Notes: v.29: hopos me kauchesetai pasa sarx (that not should boast all flesh) — the PURPOSE of choosing weakness. God's design ensures that all glory goes to Him, not to the human instrument. This is the same design principle as 2 Cor 4:7 (earthen vessels).

    Peter denies personal power — healing through Christ's name and faith¶

    And when Peter saw [it], he answered unto the people, Ye men of Israel, why marvel ye at this? or why look ye so earnestly on us, as though by our own power or holiness we had made this man to walk? The God of Abraham, and of Isaac, and of Jacob, the God of our fathers, hath glorified his Son Jesus; whom ye delivered up, and denied him in the presence of Pilate, when he was determined to let [him] go. But ye denied the Holy One and the Just, and desired a murderer to be granted unto you; And killed the Prince of life, whom God hath raised from the dead; whereof we are witnesses. And his name through faith in his name hath made this man strong, whom ye see and know: yea, the faith which is by him hath given him this perfect soundness in the presence of you all.

    Context: Peter explicitly denies that his own power (dynamis) or holiness (eusebeia) caused the healing. The name of Jesus and faith in that name are identified as the cause.

    Notes: v.12: idia dynamis (own power, G1411) and eusebeia (piety, G2150) are both denied. v.16: 'his name through faith in his name' — the grammatical subject is the name, not the apostles. Even the faith is 'by him' (di autou).

    Christ partook of humanity to destroy the devil's power of death¶

    Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil;

    Context: Christ's incarnation and death destroyed the devil's kratos (sovereign power) over death. Authority over Satan belongs to Christ through His atoning work, not to believers independently.

    Notes: kratos (G2904) is used for the devil's 'power of death' — sovereign dominion power. Christ destroyed (katargese, G2673 — rendered inoperative) this through His own death. Believers benefit from this victory but did not accomplish it.

    Christ spoiled principalities and powers through the cross¶

    [And] having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it.

    Context: The definitive statement of Christ's victory over spiritual powers at the cross. The triumph is Christ's, accomplished through the cross — not delegated to believers to accomplish independently.

    Notes: apekdysamenos (G554 — having stripped/disarmed) tas archas kai tas exousias (principalities and authorities). Christ publicly triumphed (thriambeusas, G2358) over them 'in it' (en auto — in the cross). The victory over spiritual powers is Christ's accomplished fact, not a believer's ongoing battle to win.

    Greater is He that is in you than he that is in the world¶

    Ye are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.

    Context: John assures believers of victory over false spirits. The reason for overcoming is not the believer's authority but 'he that is in you' — the Holy Spirit — being greater than Satan.

    Notes: ho en hymin (the one in you) is greater than ho en to kosmo (the one in the world). The victory is attributed to the indwelling Spirit's superiority, not to any inherent believer power. The believer is the vessel; God is the power.

    The victory that overcomes the world is our faith¶

    For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, [even] our faith.

    Context: Victory over the world comes through being 'born of God' and through faith — both are God-originated (new birth is God's act; faith is God's gift per Eph 2:8).

    Notes: he nike he nikesasa (the victory that has overcome) — perfect tense, accomplished fact. pistis (faith) is the instrument of victory, but faith itself is a gift (Eph 2:8) and authored by Jesus (Heb 12:2). The victory is real but its source is divine.

    They overcame by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony¶

    And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.

    Context: The saints overcome the accuser by two means: the blood of the Lamb (Christ's atoning work) and the word of their testimony (faithful witness). Neither is inherent authority — one is Christ's work, the other is faithful proclamation.

    Notes: dia to haima tou arniou (through the blood of the Lamb) — Christ's sacrificial work is the primary instrument. dia ton logon tes martyrias auton (through the word of their testimony) — faithful witness, not personal power. The third element — 'loved not their lives unto the death' — is self-sacrifice, the opposite of authority-claiming.

    Endued with power from on high — power must be received¶

    And, behold, I send the promise of my Father upon you: but tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high.

    Context: Jesus instructs the disciples to wait for the Spirit before attempting ministry. Power (dynamis) comes 'from on high' — it is external, from God, and must be received.

    Notes: endysesthe (G1746 — clothed with) dynamin (G1411 — power) ex hypsous (from on high). The clothing metaphor: power is put ON from outside, not generated from within. The command to 'tarry' shows the disciples could not generate this power themselves — they had to wait for God to send it.

    Some trust in chariots — we will remember the name of the LORD¶

    Some [trust] in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.

    Context: David's contrast between trusting in human military power and trusting in God's name. The dichotomy: human resources versus divine authority invoked through His name.

    Notes: ba-rekev (in chariots) and ba-susim (in horses) represent human power resources. nazkir (we will remember/invoke) beshem YHWH (the name of the LORD). The name of the LORD is the source of confidence, not personal military strength.

    Except the LORD build the house — human effort in vain without God¶

    Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh [but] in vain.

    Context: Solomon's wisdom: human effort without divine agency is futile. Both building and guarding require God's involvement — human labor alone accomplishes nothing lasting.

    Notes: im lo YHWH yivneh bayit (unless the LORD builds the house) — shav (H7723, in vain) amelu vonav (labored its builders). Human effort is real but pointless without God as the primary agent. Parallels John 15:5 ('without me ye can do nothing').

    What the law could not do — God did by sending His Son¶

    For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh: That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.

    Context: The law was powerless because of the flesh's weakness. God accomplished what human effort could not — by sending Christ and the Spirit. Righteousness is fulfilled 'in us' but by the Spirit's work, not human authority.

    Notes: to adynaton tou nomou (the inability of the law) — what was impossible for law/human effort. ho theos... katekrine (God condemned) — God is the actor. en hois... kata pneuma peripatousin (in those who walk according to the Spirit) — righteousness is fulfilled in believers who follow the Spirit, not in those exercising independent authority.

    God puts His Spirit within — causing obedience¶

    A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do [them].

    Context: God's new covenant promise: He will give a new heart, put His Spirit within, and CAUSE obedience. The spiritual transformation and resulting obedience are God's work from start to finish.

    Notes: ve-natati (and I will give) — God as subject throughout. ruchi (my Spirit) etten be-qirbekhem (I will put within you). ve-asiti (and I will cause/make) — God causes the walking in His statutes. Human obedience is real but divinely caused — not self-generated authority.

    A man can receive nothing except it be given from heaven¶

    John answered and said, A man can receive nothing, except it be given him from heaven.

    Context: John the Baptist's foundational principle: all spiritual capacity is heaven-given. No one possesses anything spiritually that was not first given by God.

    Notes: ou dynatai (is not able) anthropos (a man) lambanein (to receive) ouden (nothing) ean me e (unless it be) dedomenon (given, perfect passive participle — having been given) auto (to him) ek tou ouranou (from heaven). Universal principle: every spiritual gift, including authority, is received from above.

    Resist the devil stedfast in the faith¶

    Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour: Whom resist stedfast in the faith, knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world.

    Context: Peter's instruction on resisting the devil. The mechanism is being 'stedfast in the faith' — not exercising personal authority over Satan. Resistance is through faith (dependence on God), not self-generated power.

    Notes: antistete (G436, aorist active imperative — resist) stereoi (G4731 — firm/solid) te pistei (in the faith). Resistance is grounded in faith (pistis), which is itself a posture of dependence on God, not a claim to personal authority. Peter does not say 'command the devil' but 'resist stedfast in the faith.'

    Paul's admission of the flesh's inability — no good thing dwells in me¶

    For I know that in me (that is, in my flesh,) dwelleth no good thing: for to will is present with me; but [how] to perform that which is good I find not. For the good that I would I do not: but the evil which I would not, that I do. Now if I do that I would not, it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me. I find then a law, that, when I would do good, evil is present with me. For I delight in the law of God after the inward man: But I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity to the law of sin which is in my members. O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death?

    Context: Paul's candid admission of the flesh's powerlessness. Even the desire to do good is frustrated by the flesh's inability. This directly contradicts any concept of inherent human spiritual authority.

    Notes: v.18: ouk oikei en emoi, tout estin en te sarki mou, agathon (no good thing dwells in me, that is, in my flesh). v.24: talaiporos ego anthropos (wretched man that I am) — Paul's cry of human inability. The answer in v.25 and ch.8 is deliverance through Christ and the Spirit, not through self-generated authority.

    By grace through faith — not of yourselves, the gift of God¶

    For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: [it is] the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.

    Context: The foundational statement that salvation is entirely God's gift. Even the faith through which salvation comes is 'not of yourselves.' If salvation itself is a gift, all spiritual authority flowing from it must also be derived.

    Notes: chariti (G5485, grace) sesosmenoi (G4982, having been saved — perfect passive) dia pisteos (through faith). kai touto (and this — referring to the whole salvation-through-faith package) ouk ex hymon (not from you) theou to doron (God's gift). hina me tis kauchesesai (that no one should boast). Grace, salvation, and faith are all gifts — no room for inherent authority.

    The love of God shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost¶

    And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.

    Context: Even the love of God in believers' hearts is placed there by the Holy Spirit. The Spirit is 'given' (dothentos, aorist passive participle) — received, not self-generated.

    Notes: he agape tou theou ekkechytai (G1632, has been poured out — perfect passive) en tais kardiais hemon (in our hearts) dia pneumatos hagiou tou dothentos hemin (through the Holy Spirit who was given to us). Even love — the highest virtue — is Spirit-poured, not self-produced.

    The Spirit distributes gifts as HE wills — sovereign distribution¶

    But all these worketh that one and the selfsame Spirit, dividing to every man severally as he will.

    Context: The Holy Spirit is the sole distributor of spiritual gifts, and He distributes 'as HE wills' — not as the believer demands. Spiritual gifts and empowerment are sovereignly given, not humanly claimed.

    Notes: panta de tauta energei (G1754 — works/operates) to hen kai to auto pneuma (the one and same Spirit) diairoun (G1244 — distributing) idia hekasto (to each one individually) kathos bouletai (G1014 — as HE wills). The Spirit's sovereign will governs all spiritual gift distribution. Believers receive; they do not generate or demand.

    Disciples pray for boldness — God grants it through the Spirit¶

    And now, Lord, behold their threatenings: and grant unto thy servants, that with all boldness they may speak thy word, By stretching forth thine hand to heal; and that signs and wonders may be done by the name of thy holy child Jesus. And when they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled together; and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness.

    Context: The disciples pray for boldness — they ask God for it rather than claiming it. God answers by filling them with the Holy Spirit. The boldness, healing, and signs are all attributed to God's hand and Jesus's name.

    Notes: v.29: dos (G1325, aorist active imperative — grant/give) — they ASK God to give boldness. v.30: en to ten cheira ekteinein se (by stretching forth YOUR hand) — God's hand does the healing. dia onomatos... Iesou (by the name of Jesus) — signs done in Christ's name. v.31: eplesthesan hapantes tou hagiou pneumatos (they were all filled with the Holy Spirit) — passive, Spirit-filled, then bold speech followed.
