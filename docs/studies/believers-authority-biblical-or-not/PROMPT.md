5. **The vine-branch metaphor: ontological dependence:**
Run greek_parser.py on John 15:5 to verify choris emou (apart from me) and the emphatic double negative ou...ouden (absolutely nothing). The present tense dynasthe (are able) indicates ongoing inability, not a momentary weakness. The vine/branch metaphor is biological: a severed branch has no independent life-sustaining capacity. This is not aspirational language; it is ontological — the believer has no independent spiritual capacity. READ `D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3306-meno.md` for meno (abide/remain). John uses meno extensively: the abiding metaphor establishes that spiritual life flows from Christ to the believer continuously, not as a one-time deposit. The branch genuinely bears fruit, but the life producing the fruit comes entirely from the vine. Run cross_testament_parallels_v2.py on John 15:5.
**Verses:** John 15:1-8, John 15:5, Gal 2:20, 1 Cor 15:10
**Strong's:** G3306, G5563, G1410

12. **Historical origins of the 'believer's authority' doctrine:**
Multiple scholars trace the 'believer's authority' concept to E.W. Kenyon (1867-1948), who fused Christianity with New Thought metaphysical philosophy. Kenneth Hagin plagiarized Kenyon and popularized the teachings. Jackson's peer-reviewed article in Themelios concludes the doctrine is 'heretical because its claim to be Christian cannot be substantiated.' Three exegetical errors identified: (1) extracting covenantal promises to national Israel for individual believers; (2) literalizing figurative NT language; (3) 'positive confession' distorting prayer into formula. While the historical analysis provides context, the study's primary evidence must be the biblical text itself. The research agent should note these historical findings but focus analysis on what the Greek and Hebrew texts actually say.
**Verses:** Matt 8:20, Mark 10:30, 2 Cor 12:9-10
**Strong's:** G1849, G1411
