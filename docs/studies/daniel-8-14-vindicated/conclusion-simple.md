# Is Daniel 8:14 About the Sanctuary Being "Cleansed" or "Vindicated"?

---

## The Question

Daniel 8:14 says, in the King James Version, "then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." But the Hebrew word behind "cleansed" is *nitsdaq* — a form of *tsadaq*, the ordinary word for being *justified*, *righteous*, or *vindicated* in a court of law. So three questions follow. Is "vindicated" a better translation than "cleansed"? Is "vindicated" the fitting answer to the question asked one verse earlier in 8:13? And if the word means "vindicated," does that leave *cleansing* out — or does it include it?

---

## The Short Answer

**Yes on all three — held together as one picture.** "Vindicated" is the more precise rendering; it is the fitting answer to 8:13; and it *includes* cleansing rather than excluding it. Cleansing is a part of the larger verdict, not a rival to it.

- **"Vindicated" is more accurate than "cleansed."** *tsadaq* is a courtroom word. Across the Old Testament the King James Version translates it "just," "justify," or "righteous" in every place except this one verse, and the Greek Old Testament renders it with the justify-word (*dikaioo*), never the cleanse-word. Daniel had the normal cleansing word (*taher*) and the atonement word (*kaphar*) available — he even uses *kaphar* one chapter later — and chose neither here.
- **It answers the question of 8:13.** Verse 13 asks how long the sanctuary and the host will be *trampled* — a problem of injustice and violence, with no purity word in sight. An injustice problem calls for a justice answer. The sanctuary that is the trampled *object* in verse 13 becomes the vindicated *subject* in verse 14.
- **Vindication *includes* cleansing.** The verdict is the larger category that contains the cleansing it declares finished. Daniel 9:24 sets atonement (*kaphar*) and righteousness (*tsedeq*) side by side; passages like Job 4:17, Job 25:4, and Psalm 51 place the justice-word in parallel with purity words; Zechariah 3 shows cleansing happening *inside* a single verdict; and Hebrews 9 treats the cleansing as the *process* whose *result* Daniel 8:14 announces.

---

## Where the Study Is Careful

Two honest limits are kept in view rather than smoothed over:

- **The Greek witness reads "cleanse."** The surviving Greek text of Daniel 8:14 itself uses the cleanse-verb (*katharizo*), and the local copy does not separate the older Greek from the later revision. So "cleansed" has a real ancient pedigree — which is exactly why the study does not treat cleansing as *absent*. It defeats the strong claim that cleansing is excluded, without making cleansing the primary meaning of the Hebrew.
- **One grammatical detail stays open.** Because this is the only time this form of the verb appears in the whole Old Testament, its exact shade — "declared righteous" versus "put right / restored" — cannot be pinned down. Both live in the justice family, and neither is "cleansed," so this openness does not weaken the main conclusion.

Where a well-known writer argued that "cleansed" is simply a mistranslation and cleansing is *not in view at all*, the study agrees with the smaller point — "vindicated" is the better word and "cleansed" under-translates — but rejects the larger one: cleansing is included, not erased.
