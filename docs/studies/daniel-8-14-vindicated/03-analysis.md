# Analysis: Daniel 8:14 — Is 'vindicated' the better rendering, the fitting response to 8:13, and does it include cleansing?

This study asks three questions about one Hebrew verb. In Daniel 8:14 the KJV reads "then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." The verb behind "cleansed" is *nitsdaq*. First, is "vindicated" the more accurate rendering than "cleansed"? Second, is "vindicated" the fitting answer to the problem Daniel 8:13 raises? Third, does the meaning "vindicated" include cleansing, or exclude it?

The three questions form one hierarchy, not three separate verdicts. The Hebrew verb is forensic (a courtroom setting-right, not a purity wash), that forensic word fits the injustice problem 8:13 names, and the forensic category is the larger one that contains the cleansing it declares complete. Two findings from the global synthesis anchor the whole study and are stated here verbatim:

- The verb is predicated of the sanctuary (qodesh) as its subject in a divine passive: God sets right the very sanctuary that verse 13 showed being trampled.
- The word names a forensic verdict of vindication — the larger category that includes ritual cleansing while reaching beyond it, not a denial of it.

## Verse-by-Verse Analysis

### Daniel 8:14 — the crux clause

**Context:** The angel answers the "how long" question of 8:13, closing the little-horn vision of Daniel 8. The third clause predicates *nitsdaq* of *qodesh* (holiness/sanctuary), the subject that receives the action.

**Direct statement:** The KJV reads, "And he said unto me, Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." The verb rendered "cleansed" is the disputed word.

**Original language:** The parser identifies *nitsdaq* as a Niphal Perfect 3ms of *tsadaq* (H6663), preceded by a conjunctive waw, with *qodesh* as its grammatical subject. This is the sole Niphal of *tsadaq* in the whole Hebrew Old Testament — a one-of-a-kind stem-form. *tsadaq* is the justice/righteousness root, not the ritual-cleansing root *taher* (H2891). The Niphal carries passive force and names no agent, which makes it a divine passive: the sanctuary is set right by an unnamed actor who, in this heavenly vision, is God. So the grammar establishes with certainty that *qodesh* is the party acted upon and God is the one acting. As the sole OT Niphal of the verb, its force stays open between 'declared righteous' and 'restored to rightful state' — both justice-field, neither 'cleansed'.

**Cross-references:** The forensic base sense is fixed by the courtroom corpus below (Deut 25:1, Isa 50:8, Psa 143:2, Job 9:2, 40:8, Prov 17:15), where *tsadaq* means a verdict of being in the right. The inclusion overlap appears where *tsadaq* stands beside purity words (Job 25:4, Psa 51:4).

**Relationship to other evidence:** One complication belongs here and is not smoothed over. The local Greek text of this verse reads "καὶ καθαρισθήσεται τὸ ἅγιον" — the verb *katharisthesetai*, from *katharizo* (G2511), a cleanse-verb, not the justify-verb *dikaioo* (G1344). So the dispute is real at two levels. The Hebrew root is forensic, yet the Greek that transmitted the verse into the Vulgate ("mundabitur") and into English carried a cleanse-verb. This complication is engaged directly in the Difficult Passages section.

### Daniel 8:9-14 — the attack-question-answer unit

**Context:** Daniel's vision of the ram and he-goat. A little horn rises out of one of the four Grecian divisions and mounts a fourfold assault (vv.9-12): it waxes great, casts down host and stars and truth, stamps upon them, magnifies itself against the prince of the host, takes away the daily (*tamid*), and the sanctuary is cast down.

**Direct statement:** The unit frames a problem of usurpation and assault against the sanctuary and host, then poses the "how long" question (v.13) and gives the answer clause (v.14).

**Original language:** Across vv.9-12 the operative verbs are assault and usurpation language — cast down, stamped/trodden (from the *ramas* root), magnified, taken away, given over (*natan*, H5414) "by reason of transgression" (*pesha*, H6588). No ritual-purity or defilement verb (no *taher*, no *tame*) appears anywhere in the unit.

**Relationship to other evidence:** The vocabulary here fixes the shape of the problem. Because the assault is injustice, its answer-word in v.14 is a justice word. The "how long" (*ad-matay*, v.13) and the "unto" (*ad*, v.14) form a syntactic lock-and-key binding answer to question.

### Daniel 8:11-12 — the injustice the vindication reverses

**Context:** Verses 11-12 detail the horn against the prince of the host, the *tamid*, and the sanctuary.

**Direct statement:** The daily is taken away, the sanctuary cast down, the host "given" (*natan*, H5414) over "by reason of transgression" (*pesha*, H6588), and truth cast to the ground.

**Original language:** *natan* (H5414) appears here as a passive giving-over — what was permitted to be given over is what verse 14 sets right. *pesha* (H6588, Strong's "a revolt, national, moral or religious"; 95 OT occurrences, overwhelmingly "transgression") names the violated norm. The register is usurpation, not cultic defilement.

**Relationship to other evidence:** These verses supply the premise that the problem is usurpation and assault. What God gave over (v.12), God vindicates (v.14) — the same *qodesh*, object of the wrong, becomes subject of the setting-right.

### Daniel 8:13 — the question nitsdaq answers

**Context:** The angelic dialogue: one holy one asks another "how long" the vision, the daily, and "the transgression of desolation" will continue, giving the sanctuary and host to be trodden under foot.

**Direct statement:** The verse asks the duration of an injustice — transgression, desolation, and trampling of the sanctuary and host.

**Original language:** The parser confirms four operative terms: *pesha* (H6588, transgression), *shamem* (H8074, be desolate; 103 OT occurrences, "desolate/astonished/lay waste"), *natan/tet* (H5414, give-over), and *mirmac* (H4823, trodden under foot). *mirmac* comes from *ramas* (to trample); Strong's glosses it "abasement," with the dominant KJV rendering "trodden down" across its 8 OT occurrences. No purity verb (*taher*, *tame*) appears. This is the "violated norm" axis: Daniel 8:13 poses an injustice problem — transgression, desolation, trampling, no purity word in view — and an injustice problem takes a justice answer.

**Cross-references:** *mirmac* elsewhere (Isa 5:5, 28:18) carries the same trampling/subjugation register.

**Relationship to other evidence:** *qodesh* is the object of trampling here and becomes the subject of vindication in v.14 — the object-to-subject inversion that binds the answer to the question. An injustice question naturally takes a justice answer, so "vindicated" fits the wrong 8:13 names, while "cleansed" would answer a defilement problem the text does not primarily pose.

### Daniel 8:26 — back-reference confirming 8:14 as the answer clause

**Context:** Part of Gabriel's interpretation (vv.15-26); v.26 reaches back to the "evening and morning" figure of v.14.

**Direct statement:** "The vision of the evening and the morning which was told is true; wherefore shut thou up the vision; for it shall be for many days."

**Relationship to other evidence:** The back-reference anchors v.14 as the load-bearing answer clause the whole unit builds toward. "Is true" (*emeth*) reinforces the verdict register — a true resolution of an assault fits a forensic reading more naturally than a merely ceremonial one.

### Daniel 9:24 — the kaphar->tsedeq bridge verse

**Context:** The 70-weeks decree, one chapter after 8:14, with six infinitival purpose clauses.

**Direct statement:** "Seventy weeks are determined... to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness..."

**Original language:** The parser confirms both *lekapper* (*kaphar*, H3722, Piel Infinitive Construct, "to make reconciliation/atone for iniquity") and *tsedeq olamim* (*tsedeq*, H6664, "everlasting righteousness"). This is the only OT verse pairing *kaphar* (atone) with *tsedeq* (righteousness), and the sequence moves from dealing with sin to bringing in righteousness — atonement issues in righteousness.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the study's central negative control and its central bridge. As a negative control, it proves Daniel had the atonement verb *kaphar* in his active lexicon one chapter after 8:14 (130 OT occurrences of *kaphar*; the DOA verb) — so his choice of *tsadaq* in 8:14 is deliberate. As a bridge, it sets the atonement register directly beside the righteousness register in one breath. Because *tsedeq* is the noun form of the same root as *nitsdaq*, Daniel's own book shows the two registers joined, not opposed. This grounds inclusion.

### Daniel 12:10 — Daniel's own end-time purification word

**Context:** Daniel's closing end-time oracle; a purifying and testing of the righteous.

**Direct statement:** "Many shall be purified, and made white, and tried."

**Original language:** The verbs are *barar* (purified), *laban* (made white), and *tsaraph* (tried) — a register distinct from both *taher* and *tsadaq*.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Daniel commands three distinct registers used to their contexts: *kaphar* (atone, 9:24), *barar/laban/tsaraph* (purify/refine, 12:10), and *tsadaq* (vindicate, 8:14). The pattern sharpens that the choice of *tsadaq* in 8:14 is a purposeful, register-specific selection, not a default.

### Daniel 7:9-10 — the judgment scene Dan 8:14 compresses

**Context:** The Aramaic vision of Daniel 7; the heavenly court convenes after the little horn's speaking.

**Direct statement:** "The thrones were cast down [set], and the Ancient of days did sit... the judgment was set, and the books were opened."

**Original language:** "Judgment" is *din* (H1780), the Aramaic noun for "the suit, justice, sentence or tribunal" (5 OT occurrences, all in Daniel's court scenes: 7:10, 7:22, 7:26).

**Relationship to other evidence:** Daniel 7 stages the courtroom that Daniel 8:14 compresses into one word. The structure runs: horn attacks (7:8) -> court sits (7:9-10) -> verdict for the saints and against the horn (7:22, 7:26). This establishes the forensic frame of the setting-right.

### Daniel 7:22 — judgment given to the saints

**Direct statement:** "Judgment was given to the saints of the most High; and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom."

**Relationship to other evidence:** *din* (H1780) is rendered in favor of the saints — the vindication of the persecuted, parallel to *nitsdaq* in 8:14. What was trampled is set right by a forensic verdict.

### Daniel 7:26 — the judgment shall sit

**Direct statement:** "The judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it unto the end."

**Relationship to other evidence:** *din* (H1780) sits and rules against the usurper — the negative side of the same verdict that vindicates the saints (7:22). Together 7:22 and 7:26 are one two-sided verdict: for the wronged, against the horn. Daniel 8:14's single *nitsdaq* names the same judgment this court renders in expanded form.

### Deuteronomy 25:1 — the paradigmatic forensic tsadaq

**Context:** Mosaic courtroom procedure.

**Direct statement:** "If there be a controversy between men, and they come unto judgment... then they shall justify the righteous, and condemn the wicked."

**Original language:** *tsadaq* (H6663, Hiphil) is a judge's declarative verdict, set as the exact antonym of "condemn." The LXX renders it with *dikaioo*.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This fixes the base sense of *tsadaq* as a declaratory legal verdict, not a moral or ritual change. It is the reference-axis against which "cleansed vs vindicated" and "includes cleansing" are measured.

### Proverbs 17:15 — justify vs condemn as the verdict pair

**Direct statement:** "He that justifieth the wicked, and he that condemneth the just, even they both are abomination to the LORD."

**Original language:** *tsadaq* (H6663, Hiphil, "justify") stands antithetically to "condemn." To justify is to declare in the right — wrong here only because the object is the wicked.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Confirms *tsadaq* is a courtroom verdict term, reinforcing "vindicated" for Daniel 8:14.

### Job 9:2 — the base-sense negative control

**Direct statement:** "I know it is so of a truth: but how should man be just with God?"

**Original language:** *tsadaq* (H6663) means "be just/right with God" with no purity term in the immediate context.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the negative control for the base sense. The forensic meaning stands alone here without any cleansing word. It shows the base sense is forensic and cleansing surfaces only where a purity word is paired — so cleansing is an included facet in cultic or relational contexts, not the bare lexical core. This datum defeats the cleansing-primary reading.

### Job 40:8 — righteousness by winning the case

**Direct statement:** "Wilt thou also disannul my judgment? wilt thou condemn me, that thou mayest be righteous?"

**Original language:** *tsadaq* (H6663) is set opposite "condemn" and paired with *mishpat* (H4941, "my judgment"). Being righteous is the outcome of winning a legal contest.

**Relationship to other evidence:** With Proverbs 17:15 this fixes the justify/condemn antithesis and the *mishpat* frame — the forensic register behind "vindicated."

### Job 25:4 — tsadaq in parallel with zakah (be clean)

**Context:** Bildad's speech; a wisdom couplet on whether a mortal can stand right before God.

**Direct statement:** "How then can man be justified with God? or how can he be clean that is born of a woman?"

**Original language:** The parser confirms synonymous parallelism: *yitsdaq* (*tsadaq*, H6663, Qal Impf 3ms, "be justified") in the first colon, *yizkeh* (*zakah*, H2135, Qal Impf 3ms, "be clean/pure") in the second. *zakah* (11 OT occurrences; "be clean/clear/pure") is a genuine purity verb.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the clearest OT couplet pairing the righteousness verb with a purity verb. The two fields overlap in Hebrew poetry, so choosing *tsadaq* does not seal off the cleanness idea — it subsumes it. This supports inclusion, at modest and defeatable weight (the datum is a poetic parallelism, not lexical identity of the two words).

### Job 4:17 — sdq in parallel with taher; LXX katharos

**Context:** Eliphaz reporting a night vision; the base wisdom question of human righteousness before God.

**Direct statement:** "Shall mortal man be more just than God? shall a man be more pure than his maker?"

**Original language:** The parser confirms *yitsdaq* (*tsadaq*, H6663) in parallel with *yithar* (*taher*, H2891, "be pure/clean"). The local LXX renders the couplet with *katharos* ("clean," the same kathar- word-group as Daniel 8:14's *katharisthesetai*) plus *amemptos* ("blameless"). The corpus integrity check on Job 4:17 returned no damage.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This verifies Davidson's claim against the local corpus: the LXX itself renders a *tsadaq*-line with the clean word-group here. Important precision — the Greek renders the second (*taher*) colon with *katharos*; it does not equate *tsadaq* itself with *katharos*. So the datum shows the couplet is a purity-parallel and the fields overlap, supporting inclusion, not that *tsadaq* lexically means "cleanse."

### Psalm 51:4 — tsadaq || zakah inside a purification psalm

**Context:** David's penitential psalm after Bathsheba; God's own rightness is affirmed in judgment. (Versification: English 51:4 = Hebrew MT 51:6, a two-verse offset from the superscription.)

**Direct statement:** "That thou mightest be justified when thou speakest, and be clear when thou judgest."

**Original language:** The parser of MT 51:6 confirms *titsdaq* (*tsadaq*, H6663, Qal Impf 2ms, "be justified") in synonymous parallel with *tizkeh* (*zakah*, H2135, Qal Impf 2ms, "be clean") — the same *tsadaq* || *zakah* pairing as Job 25:4, here predicated of God. The LXX renders the verb with *dikaioo*, and the verse is quoted in Romans 3:4.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Three verses later the psalm's cleansing petition (Psa 51:7 = MT 51:9) uses *taher* ("and I shall be clean") with *kabas* ("wash me"). So within one psalm the justifying-verdict verb and the ritual-cleansing verbs co-occur — the *tame*-to-*tahor*-to-*tsadaq* progression in miniature. Verdict and cleansing belong to one penitential setting.

### Psalm 51:7 — the cleansing petition

**Direct statement:** "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow."

**Original language:** The parse of MT 51:9 confirms *taher* (H2891, "and I shall be clean") with *kabas* ("wash me") and *laban* ("whiter than snow").

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the cleansing side of Psalm 51, paired with the justifying verdict of v.4 — retrieved for context, showing the two registers coupled in one psalm.

### Psalm 143:2 — forensic verdict before the divine judge

**Direct statement:** "Enter not into judgment with thy servant: for in thy sight shall no man living be justified."

**Original language:** *tsadaq* (H6663, "be justified") stands inside an explicit "judgment" (*mishpat*) frame. The LXX renders it *dikaioo*, and Paul quotes the verse in Romans 3:20.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Another pure-forensic control, this time before the divine Judge, with no purity content — reinforcing the verdict sense.

### Isaiah 43:9 — courtroom summons of the nations

**Direct statement:** "Let them bring forth their witnesses, that they may be justified: or let them hear, and say, It is truth."

**Original language:** *tsadaq* (H6663, "be justified") = be vindicated as in the right in a legal contest, with witnesses and testimony. The LXX renders it *dikaioo*. Zero ritual-purity content.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Part of the Isaiah trial corpus fixing the forensic-verdict sense.

### Isaiah 43:26 — legal self-defense before God

**Direct statement:** "Put me in remembrance: let us plead together: declare thou, that thou mayest be justified."

**Original language:** *tsadaq* (H6663, "be justified") in a plead-your-case setting. The LXX renders it *dikaioo*.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Forensic through and through, reinforcing the base sense.

### Isaiah 45:25 — corporate forensic vindication

**Direct statement:** "In the LORD shall all the seed of Israel be justified, and shall glory."

**Original language:** *tsadaq* (H6663) applied corporately: the whole seed of Israel "be justified." The LXX reads "ἀπὸ κυρίου δικαιωθήσονται" (*dikaioo*).

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is a direct comparandum to Daniel 8:14 in the same translation corpus. Where *tsadaq* means "be justified," Isaiah's LXX uses *dikaioo*; but Daniel 8:14's LXX uses *katharizo*. That contrast isolates Daniel 8:14's Greek as an outlier cleansing-rendering of a normally-justify verb. It also matters that *tsadaq* covers a corporate/institutional subject, since Daniel 8:14's *qodesh* is such a subject.

### Isaiah 50:8 — the Servant's courtroom vindication

**Direct statement:** "He is near that justifieth me; who will contend with me? let us stand together: who is mine adversary?"

**Original language:** The parser confirms *matsdiqi* (*tsadaq*, H6663, Hiphil participle + 1cs suffix, "the one justifying me"). Courtroom vocabulary saturates the verse: *riv* (contend at law), *amad* (stand as litigants), *baal mishpat* (*mishpat*, H4941, "adversary at law"). The LXX uses *dikaioo*.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Pure forensic *tsadaq* — a verdict of being in the right, with no purity term present. It fixes the forensic core.

### Isaiah 53:11 — the Servant justifies many by bearing iniquity

**Direct statement:** "By his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many; for he shall bear their iniquities."

**Original language:** *tsadaq* (H6663, Hiphil, "justify") with the mechanism stated: "for he shall bear their iniquities." The LXX uses *dikaioo*.

**Relationship to other evidence:** The justifying verdict is the declared result of a completed sin-bearing. This is the structural analogy for how vindication can encompass the atonement it presupposes — the verdict is grounded in the atoning act, not set against it. It bridges *tsadaq* to the NT atonement-to-justification chain (Rom 3:24-26).

### Zechariah 3:1-5 and 3:4 — cleansing within vindication

**Context:** Zechariah's fourth night vision; a heavenly courtroom with an accuser (Satan), the accused (Joshua the high priest, in filthy garments), and a verdict.

**Direct statement:** "Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment." Then "let them set a fair mitre upon his head."

**Original language:** The verdict has two movements in one utterance: the negative/cleansing movement (filthy garments removed, iniquity caused to pass) and the positive/conferral movement (change of raiment, and a fair mitre "set," *sum*, H7760, on the head).

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the clearest OT narrative model of cleansing and vindication as two aspects of one judicial act — the accuser is rebuked and the accused is both cleansed and given positive standing. It shares the accuser-court-verdict shape of Daniel 7 and Job. Honest limit: Zechariah 3 uses neither *tsadaq* nor *taher*, so it models the inclusion hierarchy structurally, not lexically. It shows that a judicial setting-right can contain a cleansing movement; the lexical inclusion claim rests on the *tsadaq*-with-purity parallelism data, not on Zechariah 3 alone.

### Leviticus 16:19 — taher for cleansing the altar on the Day of Atonement

**Direct statement:** "He shall sprinkle of the blood upon it with his finger seven times, and cleanse it, and hallow it from the uncleanness of the children of Israel."

**Original language:** The Day of Atonement cleansing act uses *taher* (H2891, "cleanse it") — the standard ritual-cleansing verb for the exact sanctuary context Daniel 8:14 evokes. The LXX renders *taher* here with *katharizo*.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is a negative control. *taher* is the ready-made cleansing verb Daniel did not use in 8:14. Its absence there is a meaningful vocabulary choice, not a lexical gap.

### Leviticus 16:30 — the DOA cleansing text

**Direct statement:** "For on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD."

**Original language:** The *kaphar*-to-*taher* sequence in one verse: "make an atonement" (*kaphar*, H3722) "to cleanse you" (*taher*, H2891). *taher* is the standard cleansing verb (115 KJV instances, heavily concentrated in Leviticus; LXX *katharizo* 61x).

**Relationship to other evidence:** The standard DOA cleansing vocabulary Daniel had available and did not use in 8:14. It establishes the *tame*-to-*tahor* progression that Daniel 9:24 then elevates to *tsedeq*.

### Leviticus 17:11 — blood makes atonement

**Direct statement:** "It is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul."

**Original language:** *kaphar* (H3722, "make an atonement") twice — the blood-mechanism verse.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Defines the blood-atonement register that grounds a justifying verdict. Cited in Hebrews 9:22 ("without shedding of blood is no remission"). The bypassed register is the presupposed ground of the verdict, which fits inclusion, not displacement.

### Leviticus 23:27-30 — the Day of Atonement as a day of judgment

**Direct statement:** Verses 29-30 attach judicial penalties to the day: "whatsoever soul it be that shall not be afflicted in that same day, he shall be cut off from among his people"; "whatsoever soul it be that doeth any work in that same day, the same soul will I destroy from among his people."

**Relationship to other evidence:** The Day of Atonement carries judgment content — "cut off" and "destroy" are judicial separations, not merely purity rites. Together with the cleansing of Leviticus 16:30, the day is both cleansing and judgment. That makes a judgment-word (*tsadaq*) a fitting antitype-word that includes rather than displaces the cleansing. This supports the Bohr/Augsburger inclusion case.

### Malachi 3:3 — taher is not narrow

**Direct statement:** "He shall purify the sons of Levi, and purge them as gold and silver, that they may offer unto the LORD an offering in righteousness."

**Original language:** *taher* (H2891, "purify") used for a deep, refining, eschatological purification of the Levites, issuing in "an offering in righteousness" (*tsedaqah*).

**Relationship to other evidence:** This guards against overreach. *taher* is broad, not restricted to a narrow ritual sense, so the inclusion case cannot rest on shrinking *taher*. It must rest on *tsadaq*'s own breadth. The verse also links purity and righteousness in one thought.

### Ezekiel 36:25 — taher for covenant-wide cleansing

**Direct statement:** "Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you."

**Original language:** *taher* (H2891) used for a sweeping moral and covenant-renewing cleansing. The LXX renders with *katharizo*.

**Relationship to other evidence:** A second guard against shrinking *taher*. The breadth of the cleansing register is real; the case for *tsadaq* rests on *tsadaq*'s breadth.

### Romans 3:24-26 — atonement grounds the justifying verdict

**Direct statement:** "Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus: whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood... that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus."

**Original language:** The parser confirms *dikaion* (*dikaios*, G1342, "just") and *kai dikaiounta* (*dikaioo*, G1344, present active participle, "and the justifier"). The propitiation (*hilasterion*, v.25) is the stated ground and the justifying verdict (v.26) the declared result.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the NT realization of the OT atonement-to-righteousness structure (Dan 9:24's *kaphar*-to-*tsedeq*). Because *dikaioo* is the Greek endpoint of the *tsadaq* chain, Romans 3:26 confirms that the justifying verdict declares the atonement complete rather than excluding it. Honest limit: the bridge runs through the Hebrew *tsadaq* and its normal LXX rendering, since Daniel 8:14's own LXX reads *katharizo*, not *dikaioo* — so this is not a direct Greek-to-Greek link at the crux verse.

### Hebrews 9:22-23 — the katharizo tension

**Direct statement:** "Almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission. It was therefore necessary that the patterns of things in the heavens should be purified with these; but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these."

**Original language:** The parser confirms *katharizesthai* (*katharizo*, G2511, present passive infinitive, "to be purified/cleansed") applied to the heavenly things.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the standing objection: a cleanse-verb is applied to the heavenly sanctuary in the NT. The resolution, carried from the prior study sv-19-righteousness-defilement-vindication, is inclusion. Hebrews 9:23 describes the atoning/cleansing process (blood, better sacrifices); Daniel 8:14's *nitsdaq* names the declared verdict. The broader category (*tsadaq*/vindication) encompasses the narrower one (*taher*/*katharizo*). A cleansing word for the heavenly sanctuary does not refute the vindication reading, because vindication includes the cleansing it declares complete. This is addressed at length in the Difficult Passages section.

### Revelation 15:3-4 — cosmic vindication of God's ways

**Direct statement:** "Just and true are thy ways, thou King of saints... for thy judgments are made manifest."

**Original language:** The parser confirms *dikaiai* (*dikaios*, G1342, Nom Pl Fem, "just") predicated of "thy ways." The noun *dikaioma* (G1345, "judgments/righteous acts") appears in v.4, not v.3.

**Relationship to other evidence:** "God's ways are just" cannot be expressed by a purity/cleanse word — one does not "cleanse" God's ways. Only the righteousness field carries it. Since *tsadaq* is rendered by this same dik- word-group, this shows the *tsadaq*/*dikaios* field is larger than the cleansing field, supporting the exceeds-cleansing half of the hierarchy.

### Revelation 16:5-7 — God's judgments true and righteous

**Direct statement:** "Thou art righteous, O Lord... true and righteous are thy judgments."

**Original language:** *dikaios* (G1342, "righteous") predicated twice — of the Lord and of His judgments.

**Relationship to other evidence:** God's judicial acts are declared right — the vindication of God's character in judgment, a referent "cleansed" cannot carry.

### Revelation 19:2 — final vindication

**Direct statement:** "True and righteous are his judgments: for he hath judged the great whore... and hath avenged the blood of his servants at her hand."

**Original language:** *dikaios* (G1342, "righteous") of God's judgments; the declaration is tied to God "avenging the blood of his servants."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the injustice-to-justice, vindication-of-the-wronged shape of Daniel 7:22 and 8:14 at cosmic scale. It is the widest referent of the verdict *nitsdaq* begins — the sanctuary vindicated, the people vindicated, and God's own government vindicated before the watching universe.

## Patterns Identified

Three cross-unit patterns run through the evidence.

**qodesh flips from object to subject.** The same *qodesh* that is trampled in the question (8:13) is set right in the answer (8:14), bound by the *ad-matay*/*ad* lock-and-key. The clause that names the wrong names the party set right. This ties the answer-word directly to the problem it resolves.

**Cleansing sits within one verdict.** Across multiple contexts a single judicial setting-right contains a cleansing movement rather than opposing it. Zechariah 3:4 removes filthy garments and confers festival robes in one verdict. Daniel 9:24 sequences *kaphar* into *tsedeq*. Romans 3:24-26 grounds the justifying verdict in the atonement. This recurring shape is the structural basis of the inclusion hierarchy.

**The forensic field exceeds cleansing.** The *tsadaq*/*dikaios* righteousness field reaches referents a cleansing word cannot carry — a courtroom verdict of being in the right (Deut 25:1, Isa 50:8, Job 9:2) and God's own ways and judgments vindicated before the universe (Rev 15:3-4, 16:5-7, 19:2). This shows vindication is the larger category that exceeds "cleansed."

## Word Study Integration

*tsadaq* (H6663) has a forensic/relational core, not a cultic-purity core. Across 41 OT occurrences (54 KJV entries), its stems are Qal 22, Hiphil 12, Piel 5, Niphal 1 (Dan 8:14), Hithpael 1. The KJV renders it "just/justify/righteous" in every occurrence except Daniel 8:14, where alone it is "cleansed." Its primary LXX renderings are *dikaioo* (21x) and *dikaios* (22x), with *krino* (judge) 8x, and never *katharizo* across the corpus. It collocates with courtroom vocabulary — *mishpat*, *shaphat*, *tsaddiq*, *rasha*. The cognate word-family confirms the field: *tsedeq* (H6664, 119x), *tsedaqah* (H6666, 165x), and *tsaddiq* (H6662, 210x) are all rendered in the righteousness/justice register.

Honesty requires one qualification. Strong's own gloss list for H6663 does include "cleanse, clear" among its senses, so the lexicon is not ignorant of a cleansing sense. But that is a secondary/derived sense, and the "cleansed" rendering as an actual KJV translation is unique to this one verse. The "clear" element of the gloss ("clear oneself," as in the Hithpael) is itself the forensic "acquit/declare-innocent" sense, not a purity wash. So the objection marks a minority sense, not the base meaning.

The contrast verbs matter. *taher* (H2891) is the standard ritual-cleansing verb (94 OT occurrences; LXX *katharizo* 61x), used in Leviticus 16:19, 30 for exactly the DOA sanctuary context. *kaphar* (H3722, 130 occurrences) is the blood-atonement verb Daniel used in 9:24. Both were available; Daniel chose *tsadaq*.

On the Greek side, the local LXX audit of *dikaioo* (G1344) covers 29 tokens in 28 verses; across all of them *dikaioo* is uniformly forensic and never renders ritual cleansing. This confirms the *tsadaq*-to-*dikaioo* bridge is forensic and isolates Daniel 8:14's *katharizo* (G2511) as an outlier versional rendering.

## Cross-Testament Connections

The OT structure of atonement-issuing-in-righteousness carries into the NT. Isaiah 53:11 grounds the justifying verdict ("justify many") in the Servant's sin-bearing. Daniel 9:24 uniquely pairs *kaphar* with *tsedeq*. Romans 3:24-26 realizes the same shape: the propitiation (*hilasterion*) grounds God being "just and the justifier." Hebrews 9:22-23 supplies the process register (the heavenly things purified with better sacrifices) whose completion the Daniel 8:14 verdict pronounces. Revelation 15:3-4, 16:5-7, and 19:2 extend the *dikaios* verdict to God's own character and judgments — the widest referent of what *nitsdaq* begins. Together the NT shows vindication is the larger category that declares the atonement/cleansing complete, exactly as the OT parallelism data predicts.

## Difficult or Complicating Passages

### The local Greek of Daniel 8:14 reads a cleanse-verb

The single most important complication is textual. The prior research assumed the Greek at Daniel 8:14 split into an Old Greek "justify" reading versus a Theodotion "cleanse" reading. The mandated verification against the local corpus corrects that assumption. The local LXX of Daniel 8:14 reads "καὶ καθαρισθήσεται τὸ ἅγιον" — the verb *katharisthesetai* (from *katharizo*, G2511, a cleanse-verb), not *dikaioo*. The corpus surfaces only this one reading and does not distinguish Old Greek from Theodotion, so the assumed split cannot be sustained from the local evidence.

This is genuine support for the cleansing tradition and must not be dismissed. An early Greek reader rendered *nitsdaq* with a purity verb. Job 4:17 shows the LXX will render a *tsadaq*-line with the *katharos* word-group where a purity term is paralleled, so a cleanse-rendering of *tsadaq* is not unmotivated. Two things keep the Hebrew-side verdict intact. First, across all 29 local occurrences *dikaioo* renders *tsadaq* uniformly forensically and never cleanses, and Isaiah 45:25's *dikaiothesontai* is a direct in-corpus comparandum — so Daniel 8:14's *katharizo* is an outlier against the normal *tsadaq*-to-*dikaioo* mapping. The KJV "cleansed" therefore descends from this Greek and Latin cleanse-tradition (*katharizo* to the Vulgate *mundabitur*), not from the Hebrew verb's own core sense. Second, the corpus cannot decide whether this *katharizo* is an early interpretive reading that heard cleansing-primary in the verse, or a later Theodotion-type overlay. Because it cannot sort those, cleansing-primary and displacement both stay live rather than being pre-decided. The honest resolution: the Greek witness lowers confidence that cleansing is excluded (it supports inclusion against strong displacement) while the Hebrew-plus-*dikaioo* pattern keeps "vindicated" the more accurate rendering of the source term.

### Strong's lists "cleanse, clear" for H6663

A related difficulty is the lexicon itself. Strong's gloss list for H6663 includes "cleanse, clear." The study cannot claim *tsadaq* has no cleansing sense. But tested against usage, the forensic senses dominate: 53 of 54 KJV renderings are justice-words, the sole exception being Daniel 8:14, and the LXX never renders *tsadaq* with *katharizo* elsewhere. The "clear" element of the gloss is the forensic "acquit/declare-innocent" sense. So the correct framing is primary-forensic-with-secondary-cleansing, and the unique "cleansed" rendering at Daniel 8:14 is plausibly downstream of the Greek/Latin cleanse-tradition rather than the root's core meaning. This keeps the door open to inclusion (cleansing as an included secondary facet) rather than a hard displacement claim.

### Hebrews 9:23 applies a cleanse-verb to the heavenly sanctuary

The standing objection to a vindication reading is that Hebrews 9:23 says the heavenly things themselves are "purified" — *katharizesthai* (*katharizo*, G2511, present passive infinitive). A cleanse-verb is used of the heavenly sanctuary in the NT. The prior study sv-19-righteousness-defilement-vindication settles this by inclusion, and the current study carries that resolution rather than re-deriving it. Hebrews 9:22-23 describes the process — what is done, a purification accomplished by better blood, "without shedding of blood is no remission." Daniel 8:14's *nitsdaq* names the meaning, the declared outcome — the sanctuary vindicated/set right. These answer different questions and are not two rival renderings of one word. The two verbs stand in a magnitude relation, not an equation: the broader category (*tsadaq*/vindication) includes the narrower one (*taher*/*katharizo*). You cannot vindicate what is still contaminated, but vindication exceeds mere purification. So Hebrews 9:23 confirms that a genuine cleansing belongs to the heavenly reality — which defeats any strong-displacement claim that cleansing is excluded altogether — while leaving "vindicated" as the correct rendering of Daniel's Hebrew verb. On its own, Hebrews 9:23 removes strong displacement but does not by itself rank cleansing-primary against inclusion, since both predict a heavenly cleansing.

### The hapax stem leaves the precise nuance open

Daniel 8:14 is the sole Niphal of *tsadaq* in the OT. No parallel Niphal of this root fixes the exact passive force. The grammar establishes the root, the stem, the subject, and the divine passive with certainty, but it cannot decide between "be vindicated/declared righteous" and "be put right/restored to rightful state." This is a genuine underdetermination and is carried honestly as a calibration. Both candidate nuances are justice-field readings, and neither is "cleansed," so the openness does not reopen the cleansing reading or weaken the functional verdict that the word names a forensic setting-right.

### The parallelism corpus is small and cross-genre

The inclusion case rests on *tsadaq* standing in poetic parallel with purity words (Job 4:17, Job 25:4, Psa 51:4). Two honest limits apply. The paralleled purity terms are a small corpus (*zakah* 11x, *bar* 9x), so the overlap is real but modest in weight. And the parallels come from wisdom poetry (Job, Psalms) — rhetorical questions about human standing before God — a genre distinct from Daniel 8:14's apocalyptic vision with the sanctuary as subject. So the field-overlap transfers to Daniel 8:14 as a supported possibility grounded in the root's breadth, weighed alongside the pure-forensic controls, not as a dominant statistical pattern. The inclusion conclusion is held with calibrated, defeatable confidence.

## Preliminary Synthesis

The weight of evidence points in one direction across all three sub-questions, held with calibrated confidence.

On the rendering: "vindicated" (or "set right") is the more accurate rendering of *nitsdaq* than "cleansed." The verb is a Niphal passive of the justice root *tsadaq*, predicated of *qodesh*, rendered "just/justify/righteous" in every other OT occurrence, and rendered *dikaioo* in the LXX everywhere else. "Cleansed" under-translates by importing a purity register the Hebrew verb does not carry as its core. This is a necessary inference on the Hebrew, calibrated only by the hapax stem and the local Greek witness.

On the response to 8:13: "vindicated" is the fitting answer to the specific problem 8:13 names. The verse poses an injustice problem — transgression, desolation, giving-over, trampling — with no purity verb, and 8:14 answers with a single justice-word, the *ad*/*ad-matay* lock-and-key and the *qodesh* object-to-subject inversion binding answer to question. An injustice problem takes a justice answer.

On inclusion: "vindicated" includes cleansing as inclusion, not displacement. Vindication is the larger forensic category that subsumes the atonement/cleansing process it declares complete — shown by Daniel 9:24's *kaphar*+*tsedeq*, the *tsadaq*-with-purity parallelisms, the Zechariah 3 one-verdict model, and the Romans 3 / Hebrews 9 redemptive frame. Strong displacement (cleansing excluded) is defeated, and cleansing-primary is defeated on the Hebrew, surviving only as a disfavored versional-minority reading via the local Greek. The three answers form one hierarchy: an injustice question takes a justice answer, and the justice-word is the larger category containing the cleansing it exceeds.
