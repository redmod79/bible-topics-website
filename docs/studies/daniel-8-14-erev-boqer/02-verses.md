# Verses

## Question
In Daniel 8:14, did Daniel intentionally use 'erev boqer' (evening-morning) instead of his usual 'yamim' (days) as a deliberate fingerprint of concealment for the time period — encodable because a day-for-year decoder already existed in Numbers 14:34 and Ezekiel 4:6 — with Gabriel's command to 'seal the vision' (Daniel 8:26) confirming the concealment is intentional? And is the resulting years-reading confirmed (with Daniel 9's 'chathak' the strongest confirmation, not the sole proof)?

### The tamid taken away; the 'how long' question about the daily, the transgression, and the trampled sanctuary

> Yea, he magnified [himself] even to the prince of the host, and by him the daily [sacrifice] was taken away, and the place of his sanctuary was cast down. And an host was given [him] against the daily [sacrifice] by reason of transgression, and it cast down the truth to the ground; and it practised, and prospered. Then I heard one saint speaking, and another saint said unto that certain [saint] which spake, How long [shall be] the vision [concerning] the daily [sacrifice], and the transgression of desolation, to give both the sanctuary and the host to be trodden under foot?

**Context:** Daniel 8, the ram-and-he-goat vision. The little horn removes 'the daily' (ha-tamid) and casts down the sanctuary place. 8:13 poses the 'ad-matay' ('how long') question that 8:14 answers. The question's subject is the tamid + transgression + trampled qodesh/tsava — grounding the tamid-primary candidate: the answer may count in the tamid's own evening-morning units.

**Notes:** Parser (8:13): 'ha-tamid' = Noun.ms.Abs with article (lemma tamid, H8548), 'ha-chazon' Noun.ms.Abs (the vision, lemma chazon H2377), 'qodesh' + 'tsava' (holiness + service/host) both Noun.ms.Abs joined by waw = the compound object trampled. KJV supplies '[sacrifice]' in italics — the Hebrew is the bare substantive 'the tamid.' The question is literally 'until when the vision — the tamid and the transgression...' The tamid is thus the vision's subject, and it is an evening+morning service (see Ex 29:38-42; Num 28:3-8), the datum behind the tamid-primary reading. 8:11 'huram/huwsar' relates to removal of the daily (Hophal). Distinct from concealment: nothing here is sealed; this is the open problem the 2300 answers.

**Context:** Daniel 8:15-19. Daniel sought the meaning of the vision (mareh/chazon). **Notes:** 8:15 'mareh' (appearance of a man) uses H4758 mareh. 8:17 Gabriel's vocative 'ben-adam' (son of man) parallels the angelic address in ch. 9; the imperative 'haben' (understand, from biyn) begins the understanding-chain resumed at 9:23 ('consider the mareh'). 8:17/8:19 'et qets' (time of the end) is the register co-text the apocalyptic-time-unit grammar entry flags as accompanying the sealed span. The chazon is placed at the 'et qets — the distant horizon that grounds the 'for many days' sealing rationale of 8:26.

### The 8-to-9 bridge: 'Understand the matter, and consider the mareh'

> At the beginning of thy supplications the commandment came forth, and I am come to shew [thee]; for thou [art] greatly beloved: therefore understand the matter, and consider the vision.

**Context:** Daniel 9:23. Gabriel (the same interpreter as ch. 8) returns and tells Daniel to 'understand (biyn) the matter and consider the mareh.' KJV renders mareh here 'the vision,' but the Hebrew is mareh (H4758) — the same word 8:26 used for the erev-boqer TIME element, not chazon (the broad vision). This is the linguistic warrant that Dan 9 resumes the ONE unexplained item of ch. 8: the time period.

**Notes:** The word behind 'consider the vision' is mareh (H4758) per the H4758 library entry (Dan 9:23 listed there). biyn ('understand') links 8:16-17 -> 9:23 (the understanding-chain). Since 8:26 attached mareh specifically to 'the evening and the morning' (the time element), and 8:27 left it not understood, 9:23's 'consider the mareh' sends Daniel back to the time period. This is the bridge that lets Dan 9's chathak (9:24) function as continuation/confirmation of the 8:14 count. NOTE this is an inference from the shared mareh vocabulary, not an explicit cross-reference in the text.

### 'A time, times, and an half' — apocalyptic period in the non-standard moed unit

> And I heard the man clothed in linen, which [was] upon the waters of the river, when he held up his right hand and his left hand unto heaven, and sware by him that liveth for ever that [it shall be] for a time, times, and an half; and when he shall have accomplished to scatter the power of the holy people, all these [things] shall be finished.

**Context:** Daniel 12:7. 'A time, times, and an half' — a non-standard apocalyptic time expression (Hebrew moed/moadim + chetzi, the parallel to the Aramaic iddan triple of 7:25). Corroborates the pattern that Daniel's apocalyptic durations often displace yamim with high-register counters — the same displacement seen at 8:14 (erev boqer) and 9:24 (shavuim).

**Notes:** Per apocalyptic-time-unit-grammar: 12:7 Hebrew 'le-moed moadim wa-chetzi' (Noun.ms.Abs moed + Noun.mp.Abs moed + Conj + Noun.ms.Abs chetzi) = the 1+plural+half schema, the Hebrew twin of 7:25 Aramaic 'iddan we-iddanin u-phlag iddan.' Both use a high-register time-lexeme (moed/iddan) NOT yamim. This is corroborating evidence that displacing yamim is a recurring Danielic apocalyptic pattern — bearing on whether 8:14's erev-boqer displacement is marked (focus 1). It is a supporting datum, not itself concealment.
