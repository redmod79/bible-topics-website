# Detailed Analysis: What Does the Scapegoat Represent on the Day of Atonement?

## Verse-by-Verse Analysis

The Day of Atonement rite in Leviticus 16 is the primary text. The analysis follows the ritual as the chapter lays it out, because the order of the acts is itself an argument. Blood-atonement is done first and finished; only then is the live goat handled. The verses below are grouped by the phase of the rite they belong to.

### Leviticus 16:5 — Two goats, one sin offering

**Context:** Aaron receives instructions for the Day. The congregation supplies two young goats "for a sin offering" and one ram for a burnt offering. The Hebrew for "sin offering" is *chattat* (H2403), and it is singular, governing the pair.

**Direct statement:** Two goats are set apart together under a single sin-offering designation.

**Original language:** Both animals are *sa'ir* (H8163), the ordinary word for a he-goat, and both belong to the *ez* (H5795) species. The word *chattat* names both the offense and the sin-offering complex; it is a category label, not a description of what each animal does.

**Cross-references:** The two-bird rite for a cleansed leper (Lev 14:4-7) shows the same one-category, two-animals pattern, where one bird is killed and one released alive.

**Relationship to other evidence:** The "one sin offering" wording is the ground of the Christ-view argument that both goats share one function and so both point to Christ. That inference does not hold. The same chapter assigns the two goats mutually exclusive acts: one is slain (16:9, 15), the other is presented alive and sent away (16:10, 21-22). A shared category cannot entail a shared act when the text prescribes incompatible acts. This is a necessary inference from the ritual itself.

### Leviticus 16:7-10 — The lots, the crux verse, and "for Azazel"

**Context:** Aaron presents both goats before the LORD at the door of the tent and casts lots (*goral*, H1486). One lot falls "for the LORD" (*la-YHWH*), the other "for the scapegoat" (*la-azazel*). The LORD's-lot goat is offered as a sin offering. The other goat is "presented alive before the LORD, to make an atonement with him, and to let him go for a scapegoat into the wilderness."

**Direct statement:** The two goats are separated by lot. One is destined for slaughter, the other to be sent alive to the wilderness.

**Original language:** In 16:8 the word Azazel parses as a proper noun (PropN.ms.Abs, parser-confirmed), and *la-YHWH* and *la-azazel* use the identical preposition *lamed* plus noun. This parallel is the strongest grammatical pressure toward reading Azazel as a personal name. In 16:10 the live goat "shall be presented alive" — *yaomad*, the Hophal (passive) of *amad* (H5975), literally "is made to stand." The atonement phrase is *lekapper alav*, "to make atonement over/upon it," the verb *kaphar* (H3722) with the preposition *al*. Then the goat is sent, *shalach* (H7971), *la-azazel ha-midbarah*, "for Azazel, wilderness-ward," with the directional *he* ending on "wilderness."

**Cross-references:** The same *al* construction lands on the altar in 16:18 and the holy place in 16:16 — inanimate objects that are acted upon, not agents. This governs how *lekapper alav* on the goat should be read.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Two facts sit together here and must be kept distinct. First, the passive *yaomad* and the *al* preposition mark the goat as an object, not an offering agent. **Leviticus 16:10 makes atonement OVER the live goat (the same construction used of the altar in 16:18), never BY it.** Second, the same *lamed* that appears before Azazel in the 16:8 parallel also appears in 16:10 marking a directional goal (*la-azazel ha-midbarah*). So the *lamed* alone cannot fix Azazel as a personal recipient rather than a destination. The word "Azazel" underdetermines its own class — being, place, or common noun for "the goat that departs" — and the text leaves it unresolved.

### Leviticus 16:11 — The bullock of the sin offering

**Context:** Before either goat's blood is handled, Aaron brings the bullock of the sin offering for himself and his house and kills it.

**Direct statement:** The blood-atonement sequence begins with Aaron's own bullock.

**Original language:** The bullock is killed (*shachat*), and its blood will be brought inside. This sets the order: bullock first, then the LORD's goat, and only after that the live goat.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This verse marks the start of the ordered blood phase that must be completed (16:20, *kalah*) before the live goat is brought forward. It establishes the sequence the whole argument depends on.

### Leviticus 16:14-16 — Blood on the mercy seat; the LORD's goat killed

**Context:** Aaron sprinkles the bullock's blood on and before the mercy seat, then kills the LORD's-lot goat and brings its blood within the veil to make atonement for the holy place.

**Direct statement:** Atonement is made by blood brought inside the sanctuary, and the sanctuary itself is what is cleansed.

**Original language:** In 16:15 *shachat* (H7819, "slaughter," Qal perfect, parser-confirmed) governs the LORD's goat. This is blood-atonement by a slain animal. In 16:16 *kipper al ha-qodesh* uses *kaphar* with *al*, "atone upon/for the holy place" — the same object-construction later used of the altar (16:18) and the live goat (16:10). The stated reason is "the uncleanness of the children of Israel, and their transgressions in all their sins."

**Cross-references:** Lev 17:11 supplies the principle: "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul." Heb 9:22 states the same rule for the new covenant: "without shedding of blood is no remission."

**Relationship to other evidence:** Only the LORD's goat is slain; only its blood enters the veil. The live goat sheds no blood at all. This is the register the live goat is excluded from, and it is where the analysis locates Christ.

### Leviticus 16:17 — Atonement "on behalf of" the people

**Context:** No man may be in the tent while Aaron makes atonement in the holy place, until he comes out having atoned for himself, his house, and all Israel.

**Original language:** Here the construction is *kipper ba'ad*, "atone on behalf of," using the preposition *ba'ad*. This is grammatically distinct from *kipper al*. The *ba'ad* form marks the beneficiaries for whom atonement is made; the *al* form marks the object upon which the atoning act is performed.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This contrast is decisive for reading 16:10. The live goat takes *al* (*lekapper alav*), not *ba'ad*. It is an object acted upon, not a beneficiary and not a co-propitiator.

### Leviticus 16:18-19 — The altar: the exact parallel to the live goat

**Context:** Aaron goes out to the altar and makes atonement "for it" with mixed bullock and goat blood, sprinkling seven times, and cleanses and hallows it.

**Original language:** In 16:18 *vekipper alav* ("and he shall make atonement upon/for it") is parser-confirmed as *kaphar* plus *al* plus a third-person suffix — the identical construction to *lekapper alav* on the live goat in 16:10. In 16:19 the altar is cleansed (*taher*, H2891).

**Relationship to other evidence:** The altar is plainly an inanimate object being purged, not a co-atoner. Because the construction on the live goat is identical, the atonement associated with the live goat is made over/concerning it, not by it. This is the grammatical heart of the study and the point at which the Christ-view's strongest grammatical claim fails.

### Leviticus 16:20 — "Made an end" of atoning, then the live goat

**Context:** The transition verse: "when he hath made an end of reconciling the holy place, and the tabernacle, and the altar, he shall bring the live goat."

**Original language:** The opening is *vekhillah*, the Piel perfect of *kalah* (H3615), "and he has finished/completed," followed by *mi-kapper*, "from atoning." The live goat is then "brought" — *hiqriv*, the Hiphil of *qarab*, meaning "bring near," not "offer" or "sacrifice."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the sequencing pillar. The text explicitly closes blood-atonement before the live-goat rite begins. The ordered phases are: cleanse by blood (16:14-19), complete atonement (16:20), then dispose of sin via the live goat (16:21-22). The live-goat rite is a distinct terminal act, sequenced after propitiation, not part of it. The verb *qarab* here means the goat is brought near, not offered up.

### Leviticus 16:21 — Confession and imputation

**Context:** Aaron lays both hands on the live goat's head, confesses over it all the iniquities, transgressions, and sins of Israel, puts them on the goat's head, and sends it away by the hand of a fit man.

**Original language:** The parser confirms *samak* (both hands laid), *hitvaddah* (the Hithpael of *yadah*, H3034, "confess") over the goat, and the full sin triad: *avon* (H5771, iniquity), *pesha* (H6588, transgression), *chattat* (H2403, sin). Then *venatan* ("and he shall put," *natan*, H5414) the sins *al-rosh ha-sa'ir* ("upon the head of the goat") — the imputation gesture. The goat is then sent, *shalach*, by the hand of an *ish itti* (H6261, a hapax, "fit/ready man," not a priestly title).

**Relationship to other evidence:** Sin is placed on the goat by confession. This is imputation onto a bearer, not a voluntary self-offering. The goat neither consents nor intercedes, unlike the Servant of Isaiah 53 who bears sin and makes intercession. The handler is a nameless functional agent, which matters for the Rev 20 correspondence.

### Leviticus 16:22 — The goat bears sin to a solitary land

**Context:** The live goat "shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited," and is let go in the wilderness.

**Original language:** The verb is *venasa*, the Qal of *nasa* (H5375, "bear/carry"). The goat bears "all their iniquities" *el-erets gezerah*, "to a land of separation/cutting-off," where *gezerah* (H1509) is a hapax, occurring only here. Then the goat is sent (*shalach*) into the wilderness (*midbar*, H4057).

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the removal register. Sin is carried away from the community to an empty place. No offering-verb (*zabach* or *qarab*) targets any being there; the stated goal is a place, *erets gezerah*, not a recipient. **The live-goat rite removes and carries away confessed sin after blood-atonement is finished, rather than making atonement itself.** The verb *nasa* is register-neutral, which the cross-testament analysis develops below.

### Leviticus 16:23-24 — Aaron changes garments, offers burnt offerings

**Context:** After the goat is dispatched, Aaron re-enters the tent, removes the linen garments, bathes, dresses, and offers the burnt offerings for himself and the people, making further atonement.

**Relationship to other evidence:** The garment change and washing frame the live-goat dispatch as a bounded, separate ritual segment. The further atonement in 16:24 is by burnt offering, confirming the day's completion structure. This reinforces the sequencing: the live goat belongs to its own phase.

### Leviticus 16:26 — The handler is defiled and must wash

**Context:** The man who led away the goat "for Azazel" must wash his clothes and bathe before returning to the camp. This is the third and last occurrence of Azazel.

**Original language:** The word Azazel (H5799) occurs only four times in the Hebrew Bible, all in Leviticus 16. Here it is *la-azazel* again.

**Relationship to other evidence:** The handler's defilement is a contamination-transfer signal, not the mark of an acceptable sacrifice. One does not become unclean by offering an acceptable sacrifice; one becomes unclean by contact with what carries away impurity. This is a negative control against reading the rite as an offering to a being.

### Leviticus 16:30 — The day cleanses

**Context:** "On that day shall the priest make atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD."

**Original language:** The verse uses a three-verb formula: *yekapper* (make atonement) issuing in *letaher* (to cleanse, *taher*, H2891) so that the people *titharu* (be clean). The forgiveness verb *salach* (H5545) is absent from all of Leviticus 16.

**Relationship to other evidence:** The daily sin offering closes with forgiveness: "the priest shall make an atonement for them, and it shall be forgiven them" (Lev 4:20, *nislach*, the Niphal of *salach*). Leviticus 16 conspicuously lacks that word. The Day cleanses corporately rather than pronouncing individual pardon. This is a further sign the live-goat rite is disposal within a cleansing frame, not a forgiveness mechanism.

### Leviticus 16:33-34 — The annual, corporate scope

**Context:** The closing statute: atonement for the sanctuary, the tent, the altar, the priests, and all the people, "once a year."

**Relationship to other evidence:** The scope is the whole sanctuary system plus all Israel, once a year (*achat ba-shanah*). This confirms the sanctuary-purging, corporate character of the rite, matching the cleansing emphasis of 16:30.

### Leviticus 4:20 — The daily rite yields forgiveness

**Context:** The regular sin-offering formula: the priest makes atonement and "it shall be forgiven them."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the control that shows what is missing in Leviticus 16. The daily rite explicitly yields *salach* (forgiveness); the Day of Atonement does not. The contrast supports reading the live goat as disposal after completed atonement, not as a forgiveness pronouncement.

### Leviticus 10:17 — The priest bears iniquity Godward

**Context:** Moses rebukes Aaron's sons for not eating the sin offering, which was given "to bear the iniquity of the congregation, to make atonement for them before the LORD."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the priestly register of *nasa*. The priest bears iniquity Godward, "before the LORD," in a mediatorial direction. It shows that *nasa* is direction-neutral; here the bearing is Godward, opposite the scapegoat's wilderness-ward removal.

### Leviticus 14:4-7 — The two-bird leper rite

**Context:** For a cleansed leper, two clean birds are taken; one is killed over running water, the living bird is dipped in the blood and released into the open field.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the structural template for Leviticus 16. One creature is killed (*shachat*, 14:5), one released alive (*shalach*, 14:7). The released creature is a disposal/removal vehicle, not a second propitiation. The one-dies, one-goes-alive pattern is a deliberate Levitical form, not unique to the Day of Atonement.

### Leviticus 17:7 — Sacrifice "unto devils"

**Context:** Immediately after the Day of Atonement chapter, Israel is forbidden to "offer their sacrifices unto devils" (*se'irim*, goat-demons) they had gone after.

**Original language:** The parser confirms *yizbechu* (the Qal of the sacrifice-verb *zabach*) with *zivcheihem* (*zebach*, the sacrifice-noun) directed *la-se'irim* (*lamed* plus *se'irim*, H8163, glossed "demon"). This is the only Priestly text pairing an offering-verb with a goat-being as recipient.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the decisive contrast. The verb the Torah uses for sacrificing to a goat-being is *zabach*. That verb is exactly what is absent from the Azazel rite. The live goat is sent (*shalach*) and bears (*nasa*); it is never sacrificed (*zabach*). **Leviticus 16 never sacrifices the goat to a wilderness being: it is sent, not offered, so "for Azazel" marks a destination, not a recipient of sacrifice.**

### Leviticus 17:11 — Blood makes atonement

**Context:** "It is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul."

**Relationship to other evidence:** Atonement is predicated on blood and life (*dam*, H1818). The live goat sheds no blood; combined with Heb 9:22, this forecloses the live goat being a second blood-propitiation. Blood-atonement belongs to the slain goat (16:15), not the live one.

### Leviticus 22:20-21 — The blemish-free requirement

**Context:** General sacrificial legislation: an animal with a blemish must not be offered, for it will not be accepted; the offering must be *tamiym* ("perfect, without blemish") to be accepted.

**Original language:** The key term is *tamiym* (H8549), predicated of the offered animal. The verb for presenting is *qarab*, "bring near/offer."

**Relationship to other evidence:** The Christ-view uses this to argue that both goats, being unblemished, prefigure the sinless Christ, and to exclude Satan from the scapegoat. But the *tamiym* rule governs animals that are offered. The live goat is presented alive, sent away, and never slaughtered or offered as a blood sacrifice. Leviticus 16 never applies *tamiym* to either goat. So the animal's physical soundness does not fix the moral character of what the goat corresponds to, and it does not exclude a Satan correspondence. This is a necessary inference that defeats the "blemish-free excludes Satan" objection.

### Leviticus 23:27-32 and Numbers 29:7-11 — The festival legislation

**Context:** These passages establish the Day of Atonement in the festival calendar: the tenth of the seventh month, affliction of soul, total rest, and the prescribed offerings including "the sin offering of atonement."

**Relationship to other evidence:** They corroborate the annual, corporate, sanctuary-purging character of the rite and situate the two-goat rite within the fixed cultus. The emphasis is cleansing before the LORD, not individual pardon transactions.

### Numbers 8:19 — The Levites bear and atone

**Context:** The LORD gives the Levites to Aaron to do the service and "make an atonement for the children of Israel."

**Relationship to other evidence:** Another priestly-register control. Atonement-bearing here is Godward and mediatorial, the opposite orientation from the scapegoat's wilderness-ward removal.

### Isaiah 53:4-6 — The Servant bears our griefs

**Context:** The Servant "hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows"; "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This bearing is redemptive and substitutionary; "with his stripes we are healed." It secures healing and peace and is directed Godward. This is a different register and a different outcome from the scapegoat, which removes already-atoned sin to a solitary land and heals no one.

### Isaiah 53:11-12 — Nasa plus intercession

**Context:** The Servant "shall bear their iniquities" and "made intercession for the transgressors."

**Original language:** The parser confirms *nasa* (H5375, "he bare the sin of many") coupled with *yaphgia'* (the Hiphil of *paga*, "made intercession"). The Servant justifies "many" (v.11) and intercedes for them (v.12).

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the priestly, redemptive register of *nasa*. It is paired with intercession and issues in justification. This is the register Christ occupies, Godward and mediatorial. It is decisively not the scapegoat's wilderness-ward disposal. Direction and outcome, not the neutral verb *nasa*, decide the referent.

### John 1:29 — The Lamb takes away sin

**Context:** John the Baptist: "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world."

**Original language:** "Taketh away" renders *airo* (G142), which occurs 110 times in the New Testament and is the standard Septuagint equivalent of Hebrew *nasa*. The parser confirms the form *airon*, a present active participle. Christ is the Lamb (*amnos*), not a goat.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Because *airo* is the very Greek word the Septuagint uses for *nasa*, the shared verb between the scapegoat and Christ is real. It is neutralized not by a different word but by direction. Christ's sin-removal is in the Lamb register, sacrificial and Godward, not the eliminative wilderness-ward sending of the scapegoat.

### Hebrews 9:7 — The high priest enters once a year with blood

**Context:** Hebrews frames the tabernacle: the high priest enters the second veil once a year, "not without blood."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This makes the Day of Atonement the type Hebrews applies to Christ, and it anchors Christ to the blood/priestly side of the rite, the LORD's goat and the high priest, not the bloodless live goat.

### Hebrews 9:11-12 — Christ enters by his own blood

**Context:** Christ, "an high priest of good things to come," enters by his own blood and obtains eternal redemption.

**Relationship to other evidence:** Christ's role maps to the blood-bearing LORD's goat and the high priest. Redemption is secured by blood, in contrast to the bloodless live goat. This guards the redemptive control: Christ alone atones and redeems.

### Hebrews 9:22 — No blood, no remission

**Context:** "Without shedding of blood is no remission."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the controlling negative principle. The live goat sheds no blood, so it cannot be the remitting propitiation. Its function must be disposal after blood-atonement is complete (16:20), not a second atonement.

### Hebrews 9:24-26 — Christ appears before God, puts away sin by his sacrifice

**Context:** Christ enters "heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us," and "once in the end of the world hath he appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself."

**Original language:** The sin-removal here is *athetesis* ("setting aside/abolition"), the object is *hamartia* (G266, the Greek the Septuagint uses for *chattat*), and the direction is Godward ("in the presence of God") and once-for-all (*hapax*).

**Relationship to other evidence:** Christ's sin-dealing is sacrificial, Godward, and once-for-all. This keeps Christ in the register of the slain LORD's goat and high priest, not the bloodless, wilderness-ward live goat.

### Hebrews 9:28 — Christ offered to bear sins

**Context:** "So Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many."

**Original language:** "To bear" is *anaphero* (G399), which occurs 12 times in the New Testament. The parser confirms the form *anenenkein*, an aorist active infinitive, with *hamartias* (sins) as its object. The prefix *ana-* marks upward, Godward motion. Its dominant senses are "offer up a sacrifice to God" and "bear/carry sins."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the exact opposite direction from the scapegoat's *nasa* toward *erets gezerah*. Christ bears sin Godward as priest; the scapegoat carries sin wilderness-ward as disposal.

### 1 Peter 2:24 — Christ bore our sins on the tree

**Context:** Christ "his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree."

**Original language:** "Bare" is again *anaphero* (G399), the parser-confirmed *anenenken*, an aorist active indicative, with "the sins" as object. This is one of only two New Testament verses that make sins the object of *anaphero* (the other is Heb 9:28).

**Relationship to other evidence:** Christ bears sin voluntarily and redemptively, in his own body. The scapegoat has sin placed on it by confession (16:21) and consents to nothing. Direction and mode both differ: Godward, sacrificial, voluntary, versus wilderness-ward, eliminative, imputed.

### Exodus 28:38 — The priest bears iniquity Godward

**Context:** The golden plate on Aaron's forehead lets him "bear the iniquity of the holy things... that they may be accepted before the LORD."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is the priestly register of *nasa*: bearing iniquity Godward to secure acceptance. It proves *nasa* itself does not entail wilderness-ward removal. Christ's Godward *anaphero* belongs to this priestly-acceptance direction, not the scapegoat's removal direction.

### Exodus 34:7 — God forgives iniquity

**Context:** The LORD's self-revelation: "forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin."

**Relationship to other evidence:** Here *nasa* denotes divine forgiveness — God lifts guilt off the sinner. The three-term inventory (iniquity, transgression, sin) matches the triad confessed over the scapegoat in 16:21. Because one Hebrew verb spans divine forgiveness, priestly acceptance-bearing (Exo 28:38), redemptive Servant-bearing (Isa 53:11-12), and wilderness-ward removal (Lev 16:22), the bare verb cannot decide the referent. Direction fixes the register.

### Leviticus 16:27 — The slain carcasses burned outside the camp

**Context:** The bullock and the LORD's goat, "whose blood was brought in to make atonement in the holy place," are carried outside the camp and burned.

**Original language:** The parser identifies both subjects as *chattat* animals — the bullock and the LORD's goat — "whose blood was brought in" (*huva*, the Hophal of *bo*) to make atonement in the holy place. These are the blood-bearing slain animals, not the live goat.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is critical for the "outside the camp" argument. The Levitical "outside the camp" motif attaches to the slain sin-offering bodies, not the live goat's banishment. The live goat sheds no blood and never enters the sanctuary. This forecloses reading Heb 13:11-13's "outside the camp" onto the live goat.

### Hebrews 13:11-13 — Jesus suffered outside the gate

**Context:** "The bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp. Wherefore Jesus also... suffered without the gate."

**Original language:** Verse 11 specifies which animals: those "whose blood is brought into the sanctuary... for sin" (*peri hamartias*). Their bodies (*somata*) are burned outside the camp. Verse 12 says Jesus "suffered outside the gate."

**Relationship to other evidence:** The type Hebrews draws runs from the slain-animal carcass burned outside the camp to Jesus' body suffering outside the gate. Both share shed blood brought into the sanctuary and a body disposed outside. This maps onto the slain LORD's goat of 16:27, not the live goat of 16:21-22. Hebrews' "outside the camp" argument therefore attaches Christ to the slain sin-offering body, corroborating that Christ occupies the LORD's-goat register. This is addressed in full in the Difficult Passages section below.

### Zechariah 3:1-2 — Satan the accuser

**Context:** Joshua the high priest stands before the angel of the LORD, with Satan at his right hand to resist him; the LORD rebukes Satan.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This establishes Satan as the accusing adversary in a sanctuary/priestly setting. It supplies the judicial (accountability/removal) framework for the Satan correspondence. It is not a Leviticus 16 text and supplies no identification of Azazel.

### Genesis 3:15 — The protoevangelium

**Context:** "I will put enmity between thee and the woman... it shall bruise thy head."

**Relationship to other evidence:** This supplies the adversary/enmity framework and the serpent's ultimate defeat. It is background for the judicial destiny of the adversary, not evidence that Azazel is Satan.

### 1 John 3:8 — The devil sins from the beginning

**Context:** "The devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil."

**Relationship to other evidence:** The "originator of sin" claim, advanced by some interpreters, is grounded here, not in Leviticus 16. Any framing of sin returning to its instigator must rest on this text and stay judicial, never redemptive. Leviticus 16 never says Azazel is Satan or the origin of sin.

### Revelation 20:1-3 — Satan bound alive in the abyss

**Context:** An angel with the key of the abyss binds Satan, casts him into the bottomless pit, and seals it for a thousand years, "till the thousand years should be fulfilled: and after that he must be loosed a little season."

**Original language:** The binding verb is *deo* (G1210, "bound," 49 occurrences), the destination is the *abyssos* (G12, 9 occurrences).

**Relationship to other evidence:** The structural correspondence to Lev 16:20-22 holds at reasonable-inference strength: after completed atonement, a living creature confined alive, a nameless functional agent (the angel, matching the *ish itti*), and a desolate destination. But the abyss is not lexically the wilderness (*midbar* / *eremos*), Rev 20 never cites Leviticus 16, and the "little season" release exceeds the type. This is developed in Difficult Passages.

### Revelation 20:10 — The lake of fire

**Context:** After his release, the devil is cast into the lake of fire.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This marks where the antitype exceeds the scapegoat type. The scapegoat is sent alive and the narrative ends; Satan is released and finally destroyed. The type does not carry these features, which is a calibration limit on the correspondence.

### Romans 3:24-26 — Christ set forth as a propitiation

**Context:** Christ is "set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood."

**Original language:** "Propitiation" is *hilasterion* (G2435), which occurs twice in the New Testament and is the Septuagint rendering of *kapporeth*, the mercy seat, the very blood-focus of the Day of Atonement.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This bridges Christ to the mercy-seat/LORD's-goat blood side of the rite, "through faith in his blood." Christ is the propitiation by blood, occupying the atoning role, not the bloodless scapegoat.

### Galatians 3:13 — Christ made a curse

**Context:** Christ "redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us."

**Relationship to other evidence:** Christ's sin-dealing has facets beyond the two-goat pattern, such as the curse/tree motif. This guards against over-mapping every element of Christ's work onto the Leviticus 16 goats. The type is partial, which also caps how far the Satan correspondence may be pressed.

### Matthew 27:15-26 — Barabbas and Jesus

**Context:** Pilate releases Barabbas and delivers Jesus to be crucified.

**Relationship to other evidence:** This is a real but inverted echo of the one-released, one-condemned pattern. The guilty party (Barabbas) is freed and the innocent (Jesus) condemned, the reverse of Leviticus 16 where both goats are innocent and the LORD's goat dies. The inversion warns against a tidy scapegoat-equals-Christ mapping and supplies no route from the scapegoat to Christ.

## Patterns Identified

Four independent lines of evidence converge on a single functional verdict, and the convergence is what gives the conclusion its strength. First, grammar: atonement is made over the live goat (*kipper-al*, 16:10), exactly as it is made over the altar (16:18), an object acted upon. Second, sequence: blood-atonement is finished (*kalah*, 16:20) before the live goat is brought forward, so the live-goat rite falls outside the propitiation. Third, register: the goat bears sin (*nasa*) to *erets gezerah* (16:22), a removal aimed at a place. Fourth, effect: the day's stated result is cleansing (*kaphar* to *taher*, 16:30) with individual forgiveness (*salach*) conspicuously absent. Each strand alone is suggestive; together they point unavoidably to terminal disposal of already-atoned confessed sin rather than propitiation.

A second pattern decides the referent. Because *nasa* is register-neutral — attested in divine (Exo 34:7), priestly-Godward (Exo 28:38; Lev 10:17), and removal (Lev 16:22) registers — the shared verb cannot fix identity. Direction does. The scapegoat carries sin wilderness-ward; Christ bears sin uniformly Godward and Lamb-ward (*anaphero*, Heb 9:28; *airo*, John 1:29). Since *airo* is the Septuagint equivalent of *nasa*, the verb overlap is genuine, and it is neutralized only by the opposite vector, not by a different word.

A third pattern is a firewall between axes. The grammatical apposition of *la-azazel* to *ha-midbarah* in 16:10 separates what the goat does from the destination Azazel. So the underdetermined lexical class of the word cannot reopen the settled functional and referent conclusions. A fourth pattern is the non-sacrificial dispatch: Leviticus 16 uses only send, bear, and impute verbs, and the handler is defiled and washes — disposal signals — never the sacrifice-verb *zabach* that Lev 17:7 uses for offering to goat-beings.

## Word Study Integration

The original-language data changes the English reading at several points. The English "to make an atonement with him" in 16:10 obscures the Hebrew *lekapper alav*, "to make atonement over/upon it," the same *kaphar* plus *al* that lands on the altar in 16:18. The preposition, not the mere presence of the atonement word, carries the meaning: the goat is the object the atonement is made over, not an agent that atones. The contrast with *kipper ba'ad* in 16:17 (atonement "on behalf of" the people) sharpens this: the live goat takes *al*, the object marker, not *ba'ad*, the beneficiary marker.

The verb *kalah* (H3615) in 16:20 is a completion verb; *vekhillah mikapper* means the atoning is finished. This fixes the live goat's rite as a phase after the propitiation closes. The verb *nasa* (H5375, 759 occurrences) is the pivot of the referent question. Its wide register — divine forgiveness, priestly bearing, redemptive Servant-bearing, wilderness-ward removal — means the verb alone decides nothing. The two Greek verbs that carry Christ's sin-bearing, *anaphero* (G399) and *airo* (G142), both point Godward or through the Lamb's death, the opposite direction from the goat.

The disputed word Azazel (H5799) occurs only four times, all in Leviticus 16. It parses as a proper noun, and the 16:8 parallel with *la-YHWH* presses toward a personal reading. But the compound etymology *ez* plus *azal* ("goat of departure"), the Septuagint's descriptive *apopompaios* ("one sent away"), and the destination use of the same *lamed* in 16:10 keep the common-noun and place readings alive. **The word 'Azazel' underdetermines its own class — being, place, or common noun for 'the goat that departs' — and the text leaves it unresolved.** This uncertainty is quarantined and does not touch the functional conclusion.

## Cross-Testament Connections

The New Testament fixes Christ's sin-bearing in a direction opposite the scapegoat. Where the goat carries sin away to a solitary land, Christ bears sin Godward as priest (*anaphero*, Heb 9:28; 1 Pet 2:24) and takes it away as the Lamb (*airo*, John 1:29). Hebrews 9 keeps his sin-putting-away sacrificial, blood-borne, and once-for-all (9:24-26), and Romans 3:25 applies the mercy seat (*hilasterion*) to Christ "through faith in his blood." The positive New Testament evidence locates Christ on the blood/mercy-seat side of the rite, the side the live goat is excluded from. **The live goat contributes nothing to salvation: bloodless and never slain, it carries off already-atoned sin rather than redeeming.**

Isaiah 53 supplies the Old Testament sin-bearer against which the scapegoat is tested. The Servant bears iniquity and makes intercession (53:11-12), justifying many. That bearing is Godward and mediatorial, the register Christ occupies, not the goat's. The two-bird leper rite (Lev 14:4-7) supplies the Old Testament template of one killed, one released alive, confirming that the released creature is a disposal vehicle. The Barabbas episode (Mat 27:15-26) inverts the release pattern, so it maps nothing onto Christ. Together the cross-testament data yield the necessary inference that **the scapegoat is not Christ; it corresponds at most to Satan as a judicial figure receiving accountability, never as a redeemer.**

## Difficult or Complicating Passages

### Hebrews 13:11-13 — "Outside the camp"

The Christ-view advances Heb 13:11-13 as evidence that the scapegoat maps onto Christ, because the live goat too is sent "outside the camp." The tension is real, since the live goat is indeed sent out of the camp. But Hebrews specifies which animals it means. Verse 11 says "the bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp." That describes the slain sin-offering carcasses of Lev 16:27 — the bullock and the LORD's goat — whose blood was brought inside. It does not describe the live goat, which sheds no blood and whose blood never enters the sanctuary.

The prior study atone-03-outside-the-gate settles this directly, showing through the "blood inside means body outside" rule (Lev 6:30; 10:16-18) that Hebrews 13:11's markers identify the slain Day-of-Atonement sin-offering body of Leviticus 16:27, not the live goat. The prior study atone-19-sin-path-two-goats traced this same distinction and settled that the two "outside the camp" motions are different rites: the slain carcass disposal of 16:27 and the live-goat banishment of 16:21-22. The prior study doa-two-goats-referents likewise kept the two goats in separate registers. Applying that here narrows the tension to a category error: the Christ-view conflates the slain goat's carcass disposal with the live goat's banishment. Hebrews' type runs from the slain body burned outside the camp to Jesus' body suffering outside the gate, both marked by shed blood brought into the sanctuary. So Heb 13:11-13 supports Christ as the LORD's goat and high priest, not as the scapegoat. The passage corroborates the finding rather than undermining it. The ancient two-goat Christ typology (Barnabas 7; Justin, Dialogue 40) read the scapegoat as Christ, but that is patristic interpretation, not what Leviticus 16 states, and it rests on the same conflation of two rites.

### Revelation 20:1-3 — The correspondence and where it exceeds the type

The Satan referent rests on a structural correspondence between the live-goat rite (Lev 16:20-22) and Satan bound (Rev 20:1-3). Both occur after completed atonement or judgment, both involve a living creature confined alive, both involve a nameless functional agent, and both involve a desolate destination. The prior studies rev-20-1-3-scapegoat-day-of-atonement and rev-20-scapegoat-satan-bound established this six-element removal-phase parallel. But Revelation 20 goes further than the type. The scapegoat is let go in the wilderness and never returns, whereas Satan is "loosed a little season" (20:3, 7) and then destroyed in the lake of fire (20:10). The abyss (*abyssos*) is not lexically the wilderness (*midbar* / *eremos*), and Revelation 20 never cites Leviticus 16.

Where the antitype exceeds the type, the correspondence must be stated at reasonable-inference strength as "corresponds to," never as a direct "Azazel is Satan" identification. The prior Rev-20 studies drew exactly this boundary: the removal phase is a real parallel, but the release and destruction have no counterpart in the scapegoat rite and must not be read back to over-determine Leviticus 16. This is why the adversary material stays anchored in the texts that actually name him — Zechariah 3:1-2, 1 John 3:8, Genesis 3:15 — and why the Satan correspondence is carried at a weaker strength than the not-Christ verdict. The correspondence is judicial and non-redemptive throughout: Christ alone atones and redeems (Heb 9:11-12; Rom 3:25; John 1:29).

## Preliminary Synthesis

The weight of evidence points strongly and consistently in one direction on the functional question and firmly on the not-Christ question, while leaving one narrow matter open. On function, four independent strands — grammar (atonement over the goat), sequence (completion before the goat is handled), register (bearing sin to a place), and effect (cleansing, not forgiveness) — converge at necessary-inference strength: the live goat disposes of and removes already-atoned confessed sin. On the referent, the scapegoat is not Christ, a necessary inference from the opposite direction of sin-bearing plus the blood/mercy-seat evidence. It corresponds to Satan only as a judicial, non-redemptive figure, a calibrated reasonable inference, since Leviticus 16 never names him and Revelation 20 exceeds the type. The demon-destination-as-sacrifice reading fails at the level of syntax, because Leviticus 16 uses no sacrifice-verb toward any being. The one genuinely open matter is the lexical class of the word Azazel, which the text underdetermines. That uncertainty is quarantined and does not weaken the functional or referent conclusions.
