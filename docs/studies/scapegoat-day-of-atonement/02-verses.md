# Verses

## Question
What does the scapegoat represent on the Day of Atonement?

### Two goats taken 'for a sin offering' (one chattat) plus a ram

> And he shall take of the congregation of the children of Israel two kids of the goats for a sin offering, and one ram for a burnt offering.

**Context:** Day of Atonement instructions to Aaron; the congregation supplies the two goats. Singular chattat ('sin offering', H2403) governs the pair.

**Notes:** Answering Adventism's 'one sin offering' argument rests here: two goats named for one chattat. But shared designation does not entail shared FUNCTION — the ritual (16:9,15 vs 16:10,21-22) divides labor: one is killed, one is sent alive. Both goats are sa'ir (H8163) of the ez (H5795) species.

---

### Both goats presented; lots cast (la-YHWH // la-Azazel); the LORD's goat offered, the Azazel goat presented alive to make atonement OVER it and sent to the wilderness

> And he shall take the two goats, and present them before the LORD [at] the door of the tabernacle of the congregation. And Aaron shall cast lots upon the two goats; one lot for the LORD, and the other lot for the scapegoat. And Aaron shall bring the goat upon which the LORD'S lot fell, and offer him [for] a sin offering. But the goat, on which the lot fell to be the scapegoat, shall be presented alive before the LORD, to make an atonement with him, [and] to let him go for a scapegoat into the wilderness.

**Context:** The lot-casting that assigns the two goats. Aaron presents both before the LORD, then casts lots (goral, H1486). One lot la-YHWH, one la-azazel.

**Notes:** 16:8 crux: azazel parses PropN.ms.Abs (parser-confirmed); la-YHWH and la-azazel use the identical lamed prep + noun. 16:10 crux: the Azazel goat is 'presented alive' (yaomad, Hophal of amad H5975 = passive 'is made to stand'), lekapper alav ('to make atonement OVER/upon it', kipper + al), then lashallach ('to send it away', shalach H7971) la-azazel ha-midbarah ('for Azazel, wilderness-ward', directional he). The SAME lamed marks both a recipient (16:8) and a destination-goal (16:10 la-azazel ha-midbarah), so the lamed alone cannot fix Azazel as a personal recipient rather than a destination. The goat is NEVER slain here.

### Aaron brings the bullock of the sin offering for himself — establishes the blood-atonement sequence

> And Aaron shall bring the bullock of the sin offering, which [is] for himself, and shall make an atonement for himself, and for his house, and shall kill the bullock of the sin offering which [is] for himself:

**Context:** Before either goat's blood is handled, Aaron makes atonement for himself and his house with the bullock.

**Notes:** Marks the ordered blood-atonement phase (bullock, then LORD's goat) that must COMPLETE before the live goat is handled (cf. 16:20 kalah).

### Blood of the bullock and the LORD's goat sprinkled on/before the mercy seat; the LORD's goat KILLED (shachat); kaphar for the holy place

> And he shall take of the blood of the bullock, and sprinkle [it] with his finger upon the mercy seat eastward; and before the mercy seat shall he sprinkle of the blood with his finger seven times. Then shall he kill the goat of the sin offering, that [is] for the people, and bring his blood within the vail, and do with that blood as he did with the blood of the bullock, and sprinkle it upon the mercy seat, and before the mercy seat: And he shall make an atonement for the holy [place], because of the uncleanness of the children of Israel, and because of their transgressions in all their sins: and so shall he do for the tabernacle of the congregation, that remaineth among them in the midst of their uncleanness.

**Context:** The core blood-atonement mechanism inside the veil. 16:15 governs the LORD's goat with shachat (H7819, slaughter) and brings its blood within the veil.

**Notes:** 16:15 shachat (Qal perfect, parser-confirmed) governs the LORD's goat — the blood-atonement BY a slain animal. Contrast the live goat, never shachat. 16:16 kipper al ha-qodesh (Piel of kaphar + al = 'atone for/upon the holy place') — an inanimate place acted upon, the same al-construction pattern later used of the altar (16:18) and the live goat (16:10). The sanctuary itself is what is cleansed.

### The man who led the goat 'for Azazel' must wash — contamination-transfer / disposal signal on the handler

> And he that let go the goat for the scapegoat shall wash his clothes, and bathe his flesh in water, and afterward come into the camp.

**Context:** The third Azazel occurrence: the handler of the live goat is rendered unclean and must wash before re-entering the camp.

**Notes:** 16:26 la-azazel (third and final Azazel token). The handler's defilement is a contamination-transfer/disposal signal, NOT a propitiatory sacrifice to a deity: one does not become unclean by offering an acceptable sacrifice, but by contact with what carries away impurity. Negative control against reading the rite as an offering TO a being.

### 'On that day shall the priest make atonement (kaphar) for you, to cleanse (taher) you' — the day CLEANSES the sanctuary/people

> For on that day shall [the priest] make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, [that] ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD.

**Context:** Summary purpose of the Day: kaphar issuing in taher (cleanness) before YHWH.

**Notes:** 16:30 unique three-verb kaphar->taher formula (yekapper ... letaher ... titharu). The Day CLEANSES (taher H2891); note salach (H5545, individual forgiveness) is ABSENT from Lev 16 — unlike the daily rite (Lev 4:20 kaphar->salach). The Day purges the sanctuary and cleanses corporately; it does not pronounce individual pardon, a further sign the live-goat rite is disposal, not remission.

### Two-bird leper rite: one bird killed, one released alive (shalach) — the one-killed/one-released Levitical template

**Context:** Cleansing of the healed leper: two clean birds, one slain over running water, the living bird dipped in the blood and released into the open field.

**Notes:** Structural analogy for the two-goat pattern: one creature killed (shachat, 14:5), one released ALIVE (shalach Piel, 14:7) into the open field. Confirms the one-dies/one-goes-alive template is a deliberate Levitical form, not unique to Lev 16 — and that the released creature is a disposal/removal vehicle, not a second propitiation.

> For the life of the flesh [is] in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it [is] the blood [that] maketh an atonement for the soul.

**Context:** The rationale for the blood prohibition and the atonement principle: life is in the blood, given on the altar to atone.

### The blemish-free (tamiym) requirement for an acceptable offering

> [But] whatsoever hath a blemish, [that] shall ye not offer: for it shall not be acceptable for you. And whosoever offereth a sacrifice of peace offerings unto the LORD to accomplish [his] vow, or a freewill offering in beeves or sheep, it shall be perfect to be accepted; there shall be no blemish therein.

**Context:** General sacrificial-holiness legislation (Lev 22), addressed to any Israelite who brings an offering. It states the standing rule that a sacrificial animal presented to the LORD must be tamiym ('perfect / without blemish') and free of any mum ('blemish'); a blemished animal is rejected as not le-ratson ('for acceptance'). The Christ-view invokes this to argue the two goats of Lev 16, both being unblemished sin-offering animals, prefigure the sinless Christ — and to exclude Satan (who is not blemish-free) from the scapegoat referent.

**Notes:** The key term is tamiym (H8549, 'complete / without blemish'), parsed Adj.ms.Abs in 22:21, predicated of the offering animal ('it shall be perfect [tamiym] to be accepted'). The negated counterpart is mum (H3971/H3970, 'blemish'): 22:20 'whatsoever hath a blemish shall ye not offer'; 22:21 'there shall be no blemish therein.' The verb for presenting is taqriv/yaqriv (Hiphil of qarab, H7126, 'bring near/offer'). SCOPE LIMIT to note for analysis: the immediate context of vv.20-21 is peace/vow/freewill offerings (zevach shelamim, neder, nedavah) — the tamiym rule is a GENERAL sacrificial requirement, not stated of the Lev 16 live goat specifically. Lev 16 itself never applies tamiym to either goat; it uses no blemish-vocabulary for the two goats. The Christ-view's inference (both Lev 16 goats must be unblemished, therefore point to the sinless Christ) rests on the general rule of chapters 1/22, not on an explicit Lev 16 datum. Whether the tamiym requirement even attaches to the LIVE goat is exegetically open: the tamiym rule governs animals OFFERED (qarab / a sacrifice), and the live goat is presented alive, sent away (shalach), and never slaughtered or offered as a blood sacrifice.

The DOA background (the high priest entering the holy place 'every year with blood of others,' v.25 = Lev 16) is applied to Christ, who instead enters 'heaven itself... in the presence of God for us' (v.24) and 'once in the end of the world' puts away sin 'by the sacrifice of himself' (v.26). This is the NT anchor for the Christ-view: Christ's sin-dealing is a completed, once-for-all sacrifice presented God-ward.

**Notes:** Direction and register are decisive. v.24: Christ enters 'heaven itself, now to appear (emphanisthenai, Aor Pass Inf of emphanizo) to the face (to prosopo) of God (tou Theou) on our behalf (hyper hemon)' — GOD-WARD, into the divine presence, the opposite of the live goat's wilderness-ward banishment (Lev 16:22). v.26: 'once (hapax) at the consummation of the ages (epi synteleia ton aionon) he has been manifested (pephanerotai, Perf Pass of phaneroo) for the putting-away of sin (eis athetesin tes hamartias) through his sacrifice (dia tes thysias autou).' The sin-removal noun is athetesis ('setting aside / abolition') and the object is HAMARTIA (G266, N-GSF) — the very Greek word the LXX uses for chattat (sin / sin-offering). Christ's sin-removal is thus (a) sacrificial ('by the sacrifice of himself,' thysia), (b) God-ward (into God's presence), and (c) once-for-all (hapax). This keeps Christ's sin-dealing in the sacrificial/priestly register of the SLAIN LORD's-goat + High-Priest roles, NOT the bloodless, wilderness-ward removal register of the live goat. The Christ-view rightly grounds Christ's sin-'putting-away' here; the datum simultaneously distinguishes that register from the live goat's.

### Sin-offering BODIES burned outside the camp; Jesus suffered outside the gate

> For the bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp. Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate. Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach.

**Context:** Hebrews' closing exhortation draws a type from the sin-offering disposal rite of Lev 16:27 (and the daily/general sin-offering law, Lev 4:11-12, 21; 6:30). The animals 'whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin' have their BODIES burned outside the camp; correspondingly Jesus 'suffered without the gate' to sanctify the people 'with his own blood.' The passage is the Christ-view's proof-text that the 'outside the camp' motif points to Christ.

**Notes:** The mapping is precise and must be read carefully. v.11 specifies WHICH animals: 'those beasts whose blood is brought (eispheretai, Pres Pass) into the sanctuary (eis ta hagia) by the high priest for sin (peri hamartias, G266)' — i.e., the BLOOD-BEARING SLAIN sin offerings, exactly the subjects of Lev 16:27 (par + se'ir ha-chattat, 'whose blood was brought in'). Their SOMATA ('bodies,' N-NPN) are 'burned outside the camp' (katakaietai exo tes parembolES). v.12: 'Jesus also (kai Iesous)... suffered (epathen) outside the gate (exo tes pylES).' The type Hebrews draws is: SLAIN-animal carcass burned outside camp -> Jesus' body suffering/death outside the gate. Both share (a) shed BLOOD brought into the sanctuary and (b) the body disposed OUTSIDE. This maps onto the SLAIN LORD's-goat/carcass rite of Lev 16:27, NOT onto the LIVE goat's banishment of Lev 16:21-22 (which sheds no blood and whose blood never enters the sanctuary). The shared 'exo tes parembolES' (outside the camp) with Lev 16:27's 'el-michuts la-machaneh' is the lexical bridge. The Christ-view's 'outside the camp' argument therefore attaches Christ to the slain sin-offering body, corroborating (not contradicting) the finding that Christ occupies the LORD's-goat register. v.13 turns the type into ethical application ('let us go forth unto him outside the camp, bearing his reproach,' ton oneidismon autou pherontes) — not a further referent claim.
